# Editorial assessment — *Nonlinear Vacuum Medium Model* (Michael Blasco, October 2026)

## Classification

**External candidate-model manuscript; not an RGS paper and not an established physical theory.** The 38-page Library copy is authored by Michael Blasco. Its publication or peer-review status is not established by the copy reviewed here.

Unlike a number of speculative compilations in the archive, this manuscript repeatedly distinguishes its ontology from established fact, states that the Minkowski 3+1 scaffold is assumed rather than emergent, and marks electromagnetic, gravitational, quantum, fermionic and experimental correspondences as not established. It should therefore **not** be labelled pseudoscience merely because it explores a nonlinear-substrate hypothesis. Its own evidential restrictions are comparatively explicit and should be preserved.

## What the manuscript actually claims

The text specifies a classical constrained field model with a real scalar amplitude \(\rho\), an independent unit-vector field \(n:M\to S^2\), a composite two-form
\[
H_{\mu\nu}=n\cdot(\partial_\mu n\times\partial_\nu n),
\]
and a candidate action containing scalar-gradient, orientation-gradient, symmetry-breaking potential and quartic derivative terms. It develops field equations, a constrained Hamiltonian/Legendre structure, a principal-symbol framework, a Derrick scaling condition and a staged numerical test architecture.

The manuscript does **not** report a confirmatory localized solution, a strict continuum existence theorem, a physical calibration, an experimental prediction or an established quantum/gravitational correspondence. Those limits are stated explicitly in its abstract, status table and conclusion.

## What can be retained in the catalogue

- The action and field content may be cited as a **specified candidate model**, not as a verified description of the vacuum.
- The scalar and projected orientation equations may be cited as **the manuscript's analytic derivations**, pending independent symbolic verification.
- The Derrick identity \(E_4=E_2+3E_0\) is a necessary stationarity condition for the stated static scaling class; it is not an existence or stability theorem.
- The Hopf invariant is a topological label under the stated smoothness, target constraint and asymptotic assumptions; it does not establish a particle.
- The Test-A/Test-B/... hierarchy is a research protocol, not evidence that any test has passed.

## Independent review still required

1. Re-derive the orientation Euler–Lagrange equation, including the constrained tangent projection and all signs/factors in the quartic \(H_{\mu\nu}H^{\mu\nu}\) term.
2. Independently verify the reduced canonical momenta, Dirac constraint classification and the inverse of the tangent kinetic matrix, including its domain of regularity.
3. Recompute the principal symbol from the full constrained equations. Establish whether the displayed block reduction is complete and whether the proposed sufficient hyperbolicity diagnostics are valid on the claimed background class.
4. Check the energy functional and Derrick scaling with the exact boundary conditions and function spaces. The virial identity is necessary only for the stationary class to which the scaling argument applies.
5. Specify the actual computational Test-A domain, boundary conditions, discretization, continuation/search coverage and frozen acceptance criteria before confirmatory execution.
6. Do not use a finite directional sample of characteristic covectors as proof of full-domain strong hyperbolicity.
7. Keep the physical promotion gates separate: a numerical localized solution would not, by itself, establish a particle, quantum state, photon, gravity, Born rule, Bell mechanism or effective QFT.

## Editorial decision

**Retain as an external candidate-model manuscript under critical review. Do not rewrite its author's claims as RGS results.** Its explicit limitations are scientifically appropriate and should not be removed. The immediate question is mathematical: whether the specified canonical action admits an admissible, converged localized solution under a fully frozen and independently reviewed Test-A protocol.

No confirmatory Test-A dataset or result is reported in the reviewed manuscript. The manuscript itself says so; the catalogue must preserve that status.
