# H4 / 600-cell — actual two-plane rotation audit

**Date:** 26 November 2026  
**Classification:** explicit elements of the finite Coxeter group, exact cyclotomic consequences, and exhaustive floating-point group-enumeration cross-check.  
**Status:** the two factors are found in genuine H4 elements, but **not simultaneously in the two planes of one element** in the exhaustive numerical enumeration. No ad-hoc SO(4) block matrix is used.

## 1. Why the previous construction is invalid as an H4 test

A block-diagonal matrix
\[
R_{\theta_1}\oplus R_{\theta_2}
\]
with \(\cos\theta_1=\varphi/2\) and \(\cos\theta_2=1/2\) is indeed in \(SO(4)\), but those angle choices were input data. Unless the matrix is independently shown to be a word in the simple reflections of H4, it is not evidence about the 600-cell symmetry group. The previous script tested the chosen matrix, not membership in H4.

## 2. A concrete H4 reflection representation

Use the simple-root Gram matrix
\[
G=\begin{pmatrix}
1&-\cos(\pi/5)&0&0\\
-\cos(\pi/5)&1&-1/2&0\\
0&-1/2&1&-1/2\\
0&0&-1/2&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
This is the Coxeter diagram [5,3,3] of H4. In simple-root coordinates, the four generating reflections are
\[
S_i=I-2e_i(e_i^TG),\qquad i=1,2,3,4.
\]
They preserve G. Every word in the \(S_i\) is therefore an actual H4 element; even-length words have determinant +1 and act as rotations in the four-dimensional Euclidean space with metric G.

For any proper element U, the complex eigenvalues occur in conjugate pairs
\[
e^{\pm i\theta_1},\quad e^{\pm i\theta_2}.
\]
The associated real invariant planes are the realifications of the two conjugate eigenspaces. If the two quadratic factors coincide (the isoclinic case), the splitting into two individual real planes is not canonical: the whole four-space is an isotypic sum.

## 3. Two actual H4 elements and their angles

The audit constructs the following words, with multiplication convention \(U=S_{i_1}S_{i_2}\cdots S_{i_m}\).

### Element A — the order-three / vesica chord

\[
U_3=S_1S_2S_1S_2S_1S_3S_2S_1S_2S_1S_3S_2S_1S_2S_4S_3S_2S_1S_2S_1S_3S_2S_1S_2S_4S_3S_2S_1S_2S_1S_3S_2S_1S_4S_3S_2S_1S_2S_3S_4.
\]
The exact \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\) matrix audit verifies \(U_3^3=I\) and the characteristic polynomial is
\[
\chi_{U_3}(x)=(x^2+x+1)^2.
\]
Thus both rotation angles are \(2\pi/3=120^\circ\) (up to orientation). For
\[
C(U)=I-U,\qquad C(U)^*C(U)=2I-U-U^{-1},
\]
we obtain
\[
C(U_3)^*C(U_3)=3I_4,
\qquad \|C(U_3)v\|=\sqrt3\,\|v\|.
\]
This is a genuine H4 occurrence of \(\sqrt3\), arising from an order-three element. Since the element is isoclinic, it does not select a unique pair of invariant planes.

### Element B — the order-five / golden chord ratio

\[
\begin{aligned}
U_5={}&S_1S_2S_1S_2S_1S_3S_2S_1S_2S_1S_3S_2S_1S_4S_3S_2S_1S_2S_1S_3S_2S_1S_2S_3S_4S_3.
\end{aligned}
\]
The exact \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\) matrix audit verifies \(U_5^5=I\) and its characteristic polynomial is
\[
\chi_{U_5}(x)=x^4+x^3+x^2+x+1=\Phi_5(x).
\]
The two real invariant planes have rotation angles \(2\pi/5=72^\circ\) and \(4\pi/5=144^\circ\). Therefore the two singular values of \(I-U_5\) are
\[
s_1=2\sin36^\circ,\qquad s_2=2\sin72^\circ,
\]
and
\[
\frac{s_2}{s_1}=\frac{\sin72^\circ}{\sin36^\circ}
=2\cos36^\circ=\varphi.
\]
Thus the golden ratio is a genuine H4 chord-singular-value ratio for this element. It is not obtained by inserting \(\varphi\) as a block cosine.

### 3.1 Exact matrices of the invariant planes

For the order-five element, the two real invariant planes are unique. In simple-root coordinates their exact \(G\)-orthogonal projectors are the following polynomials in \(U_5\):
\[
P_{72}=\frac25\left[
I+\cos72^\circ(U_5+U_5^{-1})
+\cos144^\circ(U_5^2+U_5^{-2})
\right],
\]
\[
P_{144}=\frac25\left[
I+\cos144^\circ(U_5+U_5^{-1})
+\cos72^\circ(U_5^2+U_5^{-2})
\right].
\]
Here \(\cos72^\circ=(\sqrt5-1)/4\) and
\(\cos144^\circ=-(\sqrt5+1)/4\). Exact arithmetic in
\(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\) verifies
\[
P_i^2=P_i,\quad P_{72}P_{144}=0,\quad
P_{72}+P_{144}=I,\quad
P_i^TG=GP_i,\quad U_5P_i=P_iU_5,
\]
and \(\operatorname{tr}P_i=2\). Thus these are explicit rank-two invariant-plane projectors, not merely eigenangle labels. The audit script constructs and checks their full \(4\times4\) matrices exactly.

For the order-three element, the two planes are not uniquely selected. Put
\[
K=2U_3+I.
\]
The exact checks give \(K^2=-3I\) and \(K^TG+GK=0\), so
\(J=K/\sqrt3\) is a \(G\)-orthogonal complex structure. For any nonzero
\(v\), the plane \(\operatorname{span}(v,Jv)\) is invariant, and its
\(G\)-orthogonal complement is invariant as well. This describes a family
of valid plane decompositions; there is no canonical pair attached to this
isoclinic element.

## 4. Do both factors occur in the two planes of one H4 element?

The script enumerates the group generated by the four simple reflections (14,400 elements, with 7,200 determinant-positive elements) and records the two rotation angles for every proper element. In this exhaustive floating-point enumeration:

- elements with angle pair \((120^\circ,120^\circ)\) occur (40 elements);
- elements with angle pair \((72^\circ,144^\circ)\) occur (288 elements);
- no element has one plane at \(120^\circ\) and the other at \(72^\circ\) or \(144^\circ\).

Accordingly, **the two constants are both present in the H4 rotation data, but the tested evidence places them in different group elements, not in the two invariant planes of a single common element**. The non-coexistence statement is currently an exhaustive numerical classification, not yet a stand-alone exact conjugacy-class proof.

## 5. General chord law and the correct interpretation

For an orthogonal U with invariant orthogonal planes \(\Pi_1,\Pi_2\),
\[
v=v_1+v_2,\qquad v_j\in\Pi_j,
\]
and rotation angles \(\theta_1,\theta_2\), one has
\[
\boxed{
\|(I-U^k)v\|^2
=4\sin^2(k\theta_1/2)\|v_1\|^2
+4\sin^2(k\theta_2/2)\|v_2\|^2.}
\]
This is an exact consequence of orthogonality and the two-dimensional rotation formula. It does not imply that arbitrary selected constants occur for H4: the angles must first be obtained from an actual group element.

The H4 examples establish:
- \(\sqrt3\) as the common chord factor for an order-three isoclinic element;
- \(\varphi\) as the ratio of the two chord singular values for an order-five element.

They do **not** establish one H4 element whose two planes simultaneously realize the claimed \(\sqrt3\) and \(\varphi\) branches.
