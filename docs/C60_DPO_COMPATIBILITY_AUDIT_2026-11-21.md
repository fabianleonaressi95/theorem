## 2. Three distinct objects: macroscopic cracks, (M), and microscopic jumps

In the classical Del Piero–Owen setting, a structured deformation is described by ((\kappa,g,G)):
- (g) is the macroscopic deformation (piecewise differentiable and injective in the classical formulation);
- (\kappa) is its macroscopic crack/discontinuity set;
- (G) is the deformation without disarrangements;
- (M:=\nabla g-G) is the disarrangement density.

The approximation theorem concerns a determining sequence of injective, piecewise-smooth maps (f_n) such that, in the appropriate topology,
[
f_n\to g,\qquad \nabla f_n\to G.
]
The limit relation identifies (M) with a local average of the jumps of the **approximating maps** (f_n), not with the jump of the limiting macroscopic map (g) itself. See the approximation theorem and limit relation discussed in the references below.

Therefore:
- a jump of the piecewise-defined macroscopic (g) belongs to the macroscopic discontinuity/crack set (\kappa);
- (M=\nabla g-G) is a separate bulk density generated by submacroscopic jumps in a determining sequence;
- edge-jump energy of the current piecewise map is a legitimate geometric diagnostic, but it is not, by itself, the DPO tensor (M).

For the tree-faithful construction (\nabla g|_f=B_fD_f=G_f), one has (M_f=0) on each face. The edge discontinuities do not change that identity; they belong to (\kappa). To obtain nonzero (M_f) in the variational family, one must distinguish
[
F_f:=\nabla g|_f=B_fR(\phi_f),\qquad
G_f:=B_fR(\theta_f),\qquad
M_f=F_f-G_f.
]
The approximation theorem then asks for an injective determining sequence for this pair. It does not follow merely from minimizing an edge-jump functional.

## 3. Correct Frobenius coefficient in the variational penalty

Assume (B_f^TB_f=I_2) and (R(\alpha)\in SO(2)). Then
[
\begin{aligned}
\|M_f\|_F^2
&=\|B_f(R(\phi_f)-R(\theta_f))\|_F^2\\
&=\|R(\phi_f)-R(\theta_f)\|_F^2\\
&=4\bigl(1-\cos(\phi_f-\theta_f)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
]
Thus the expression (2(1-\cos(\phi_f-\theta_f))) is correct only if the energy uses one half of the squared Frobenius norm. The convention must be stated, because it rescales the effective parameter (\mu) and shifts the reported numerical crossover.

## 4. What is automatically true face by face

For both (F_f=B_fR(\phi_f)) and (G_f=B_fR(\theta_f)),
[
F_f^TF_f=G_f^TG_f=I_2,
\qquad
J_2(F_f)=J_2(G_f)=\sqrt{\det(F_f^TF_f)}=1.
]
Hence each facewise map has rank two, preserves the chosen orientation, and preserves area locally. The (3\times2) gradient has no ordinary determinant. A positive (2\)-Jacobian is a local immersion check, **not** a global non-interpenetration condition.

A facewise normal extension
[
Q_f=B_fR(\phi_f)B_f^T+n_fn_f^T\in SO(3)
]
has determinant (+1) on that face. This is a local algebraic lift only. It does not prove that the extensions agree across edges, define a globally injective map of a thickened body, or satisfy the hypotheses of the classical three-dimensional DPO theorem.

## 5. Necessary next tests

The next computational/theoretical stage is not another unconstrained minimization. It is:

1. **Recover and version the actual artifacts** for the piecewise map and Pareto calculation (scripts, input matrices, JSON/NPZ, exact command and dependency versions). Re-run them from the repository.
2. **Global surface injectivity:** for every face image, test polygon self-injectivity, pairwise face-interior intersections, edge-image intersections, and vertex incidence. Local rank two does not settle this.
3. **Specify the body to which DPO is applied:** either formulate a surface-valued approximation theorem with precise hypotheses, or construct a justified three-dimensional thickening and prove compatibility of its facewise extensions.
4. **Construct determining maps:** produce explicit injective piecewise-smooth (f_n), not merely smoothed versions of (g), and test both (f_n\to g) and (\nabla f_n\to G) in the required topology.
5. **Only then** classify the pair as DPO-admissible. Until then, it remains a candidate.

The sampled change between (\mu=1) and (\mu=3) in the reported table should be called a steep numerical crossover, not a sharp transition or phase transition, unless global optimality and parameter-continuation checks are supplied.
