# Vesica Piscis, circular Moiré modulation, and the Maxwell bridge

**Research note — 2026-11-27**  
**Status:** exact geometry established; scalar graph model specified and reproducible; vector Maxwell construction open.  
**Classification:** research model, not a new electromagnetic law.

## 1. Scope and epistemic boundary

This note formalizes the two-circle/Vesica–Moiré line developed in the research discussion. It deliberately separates:

1. exact Euclidean geometry;
2. interference/modulation of two explicitly defined radial patterns;
3. a scalar graph-Laplacian experiment;
4. a future vector-valued Maxwell discretization;
5. physical claims about the electromagnetic spectrum.

A pair of ideal circle boundaries alone does **not** produce Moiré fringes. Fringes arise only after assigning a spatially varying pattern to each circle (here, concentric radial phase gratings). The model below is one explicit and testable choice, not a unique interpretation of the Vesica.

No claim is made that the factor \(\sqrt3\) selects optical frequencies, that the visible-spectrum endpoints are generated by the Vesica, or that the construction derives Maxwell's equations.

## 2. Exact Vesica geometry

Let two circles have common radius \(R>0\), with centres
\[
C_1=(-a,0),\qquad C_2=(a,0),\qquad a=R/2.
\]
Their centre separation is \(2a=R\). Their intersections are
\[
P_\pm=(0,\pm\sqrt{R^2-a^2})=(0,\pm\sqrt3\,R/2).
\]
The common chord therefore has length \(\sqrt3 R\), while the centre separation is \(R\). Hence
\[
\frac{|P_+-P_-|}{|C_2-C_1|}=\sqrt3.
\]
This is an exact Euclidean identity. It is not a ratio of electric and magnetic field amplitudes.

## 3. Radial phase gratings and the Moiré field

Define
\[
\rho_1(x,y)=\sqrt{(x+a)^2+y^2},\qquad
\rho_2(x,y)=\sqrt{(x-a)^2+y^2}.
\]
For equal radial wavenumber \(k=2\pi/\lambda_g\), take
\[
g_1=\cos(k\rho_1),\qquad g_2=\cos(k\rho_2).
\]
Their sum obeys
\[
g_1+g_2=
2\cos\!\left(\frac{\Delta\phi}{2}\right)
 \cos\!\left(\frac{\Sigma\phi}{2}\right),
\]
where
\[
\Delta\phi=k(\rho_1-\rho_2),\qquad
\Sigma\phi=k(\rho_1+\rho_2).
\]
The first factor is the slowly varying difference-phase envelope when the two local phases are close. The second is the carrier factor. The term “Moiré” here refers to this explicitly defined superposition of radial gratings.

Equal-phase contours are
\[
\Delta\phi=2\pi m,\quad m\in\mathbb Z.
\]
Their local normal wavevector is
\[
\mathbf q_M=\nabla\Delta\phi
=k\left(\frac{(x+a,y)}{\rho_1}
       -\frac{(x-a,y)}{\rho_2}\right),
\]
away from the two centres, where the radial distance functions are not differentiable.

On the symmetry axis \(x=0\),
\[
|\mathbf q_M(0,y)|=\frac{2ak}{\sqrt{a^2+y^2}},
\qquad
d_M(y)=\frac{2\pi}{|\mathbf q_M(0,y)|}
=\frac{\lambda_g}{2}\sqrt{1+\frac{y^2}{a^2}}.
\]
Thus
\[
d_M(0)=\lambda_g/2,\quad
d_M(a)=\lambda_g/\sqrt2,\quad
d_M(\sqrt3a)=\lambda_g.
\]
The last location is the Vesica intersection height. These are local spacings between equal-phase contours along the normal direction, not a global constant fringe period.

## 4. Complex phase and topological-defect caution

The unit-modulus field
\[
Z(x,y)=e^{i\Delta\phi(x,y)}
\]
stores the relative phase. For this particular two-distance construction, \(\Delta\phi\) is a globally single-valued real phase away from the centre differentiability points. It does not automatically contain a vortex or a dislocation. In particular, a nonzero winding number
\[
n_\gamma=\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint_\gamma d\arg Z
\]
must be computed on a specified closed loop \(\gamma\); it must not be inferred from a visually crossing fringe pattern. A robust phase singularity generally requires a zero of a complex amplitude (or a specified singular/ multivalued phase field). The present unit-modulus ansatz has no amplitude zeros.

Accordingly, this note records no dislocations or disclinations as discovered. Their existence is an open, separately testable extension.

## 5. Scalar graph model

On an \(N\times N\) square grid with spacing \(h\), evaluate \(\Delta\phi\) at vertices. For each nearest-neighbour edge \(e=(p,q)\), define
\[
w_e=1+\epsilon\cos\!\left(\frac{\Delta\phi_p+\Delta\phi_q}{2}\right),
\qquad |\epsilon|<1.
\]
The weighted graph operator is the stiffness matrix
\[
L_M=B^\top W B/h^2,
\]
where \(B\) is an oriented edge–vertex incidence matrix and \(W=\operatorname{diag}(w_e)\). The uniform control is \(L_0=B^\top B/h^2\).

Since \(w_e\ge 1-|\epsilon|>0\), both operators are symmetric positive semidefinite. On a connected grid with only internal edges, the constant vector is the single zero mode (Neumann/free-boundary graph convention). The script computes low eigenvalues and reports the frequency proxy \(\omega_j=\sqrt{\lambda_j}\) in normalized units.

This is a scalar variable-coefficient graph wave model. It is **not** a Maxwell solver, and \(\omega_j\) is not an optical frequency unless a physical dimensional calibration and constitutive model are supplied.

## 6. What the scalar calculation can and cannot establish

It can establish, for declared grid and parameters, that a spatially varying positive edge weight changes the spectrum of the scalar graph operator. Results must include the grid, domain, \(a,k,\epsilon\), boundary convention, solver tolerance and convergence under grid refinement.

It cannot establish:
- that the electromagnetic field is generated by the Vesica;
- that \(\sqrt3\) selects a band edge or the visible spectrum;
- that a scalar eigenmode is a transverse electromagnetic mode;
- the existence of topological defects without a winding/amplitude-zero test;
- a new physical law.

No unverified numerical eigenvalue table from exploratory discussion is included as a certified result. The committed script is the reproducible source of numerical values; run it to generate the report for the chosen environment.

## 7. Requirements for a genuine Maxwell discretization

A suitable next step is a discrete de Rham / compatible finite-element or DEC formulation, not a scalar Laplacian relabelled as electromagnetism. In a three-dimensional compatible discretization, let \(G\), \(C\), and \(D\) denote discrete gradient, curl, and divergence maps, with exact-sequence identities
\[
CG=0,\qquad DC=0.
\]
A cochain formulation has the schematic form
\[
\dot b=-Ce,\qquad
\dot d=C^\top h-j,
\]
with constitutive Hodge maps
\[
d=M_\varepsilon e,\qquad b=M_\mu h.
\]
The precise primal/dual spaces, orientations, boundary conditions and metric-dependent Hodge matrices must be stated. A two-dimensional calculation must declare whether it is a TE or TM reduction; it is not automatically the full three-dimensional system.

For a lossless source-free model, the discrete energy
\[
\mathcal E=\tfrac12(e^\top M_\varepsilon e+h^\top M_\mu h)
\]
must be positive and conserved (up to solver/time-discretization error). The Gauss constraints must propagate, and the uniform-medium limit must recover the expected Maxwell dispersion. Only after these checks may a Moiré-dependent constitutive/Hodge perturbation be studied for band shifts or gaps.

The factor \(\alpha(x,y)=1+\epsilon\cos\Delta\phi\) may be used as a **candidate coefficient field** for an exploratory medium. It must not be silently interpreted as vacuum permittivity or permeability. The relation between the two must be derived from a declared physical model.

## 8. Proposed validation programme

1. Reproduce the scalar graph spectrum from \`audits/vesica_moire_2d.py\`.
2. Run grid-refinement and \(\epsilon\to0\) controls; verify \(L_M\to L_0\).
3. Check symmetry, nonnegative spectrum and the constant null mode.
4. Compute phase winding on explicit loops; record zero/nonzero results and amplitude minima.
5. Implement compatible discrete Maxwell (3D, or a clearly specified TE/TM reduction).
6. Verify \(CG=0\), \(DC=0\), Gauss-constraint propagation, energy conservation and uniform-medium dispersion.
7. Add the Moiré coefficient through a declared constitutive/Hodge model; compare band structures against the uniform control.
8. Test whether any spectral feature persists under refinement and modest changes of grid, phase origin and boundary conditions.
9. Only then compare a predicted, dimensionally calibrated spectrum with measurements.

## 9. Relation to the Melchizedek interpretation

The geometric ratio \(\sqrt3\) is exact for the stated circle construction. The further interpretation of Vesica axes as electric and magnetic components, or as a determinant of electromagnetic frequencies, is not a consequence of this geometry and is not part of established Maxwell theory. It is retained here only as a historical/source-level hypothesis to be translated into explicit, falsifiable equations before testing.
