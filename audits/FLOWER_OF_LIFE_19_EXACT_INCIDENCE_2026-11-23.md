# Exact incidence audit of the 19-circle Flower of Life

**Date:** 2026-11-23  
**Classification:** exact finite geometry and exact symbolic computation for the explicitly defined arrangement. This does not reconstruct the Rapporto Vesica's undocumented ray grids.

## 1. Configuration

Set \(R=1\); any \(R>0\) is obtained by homothety. Let
\[
a_1=(1,0),\qquad a_2=(1/2,\sqrt3/2),
\]
and
\[
\Lambda_{19}=\{ma_1+na_2:\max(|m|,|n|,|m+n|)\leq2\}.
\]
There are \(1+6+12=19\) centres. At each centre \(c\), place the unit circle \(C_c=\{x:\|x-c\|=1\}\). The usual enclosing contour is not counted as a twentieth equal-radius circle; it is a separate boundary convention.

## 2. Exact intersections

For two centres \(c_i,c_j\), put \(d=c_j-c_i\), \(d^2=\|d\|^2\), and \(m=(c_i+c_j)/2\). Intersecting pairs have only
\[
d^2\in\{1,3,4\},
\]
with 42, 30, and 27 unordered pairs, respectively. Hence 99 unordered pairs of circles intersect.

Writing \(J(x,y)=(-y,x)\), the two intersection points for \(d^2<4\) are
\[
m\pm\frac{\sqrt{1-d^2/4}}{\sqrt{d^2}}Jd,
\]
and the single tangency point for \(d^2=4\) is \(m\). All coordinates lie in \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)\).

After exact deduplication, there are **31 distinct intersection points**. Their incidence multiplicities are
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
k\text{ incident circles}&2&3&4&6\\\hline
\text{number of points}&12&6&6&7
\end{array}
\]
and the total number of circle-point incidences is
\[
12\cdot2+6\cdot3+6\cdot4+7\cdot6=108.
\]
The pair count independently checks:
\[
12\binom22+6\binom32+6\binom42+7\binom62=171
=42\cdot2+30\cdot2+27\cdot1.
\]
Thus the 31-point count is not confused with the 99 intersecting circle pairs or the 108 incidences.

## 3. Incidence matrix and exact spectrum

Order the intersection points \(p_1,\ldots,p_{31}\) and circles \(C_1,\ldots,C_{19}\). Define
\[
B\in\{0,1\}^{31\times19},\qquad
B_{\alpha i}=1\iff p_\alpha\in C_i.
\]
The row sums are the point multiplicities above. The column sums show that 13 circles contain six distinct intersection points and six circles contain five.

The circle-side Gram operator is
\[
G=B^\mathsf TB\in\mathbb Z^{19\times19}.
\]
Exact symbolic computation gives
\[
\operatorname{rank}(B)=\operatorname{rank}(G)=19,\qquad
\operatorname{tr}(G)=108,\qquad
\det(G)=260861534208.
\]
Its exact characteristic polynomial is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\chi_G(\lambda)={}&(\lambda-6)^2(\lambda-3)^2(\lambda-2)^3\\
&\cdot(\lambda^2-8\lambda+11)\\
&\cdot(\lambda^3-19\lambda^2+78\lambda-76)^2\\
&\cdot(\lambda^4-38\lambda^3+375\lambda^2-1350\lambda+1584).
\end{aligned}
\]
This factorization, rather than rounded eigenvalues, is the spectral certificate. Since \(G=B^\mathsf TB\) and has full rank, it is positive definite.

## 4. Dihedral symmetry

The centre and intersection sets are invariant under \(D_6\), generated by a \(60^\circ\) rotation and a reflection. The 31 intersection points split into orbits of sizes
\[
1,\ 6,\ 6,\ 6,\ 12.
\]
The singleton orbit has incidence multiplicity 6, the 12-element orbit has multiplicity 2, and the three six-element orbits have multiplicities 6, 4, and 3. This is an exact finite symmetry statement; it does not imply a 12-dimensional physical space.

## 5. Comparison with the earlier three-circle \(C_6\) benchmark

The earlier three-circle audit selects six outward directions from the centroid and connects consecutive directions, obtaining the angular cycle \(C_6\) and
\[
\operatorname{spec}(L_{C_6})=\{0,1,1,3,3,4\}.
\]
That is a six-vertex angular graph Laplacian.

The present model instead has 19 circle centres, 31 intersection points, 108 incidences, and a \(31\times19\) incidence matrix. Its \(19\times19\) operator \(B^\mathsf TB\) records shared intersection points. These are different operators on different spaces; their spectra must not be conflated. The earlier \(C_6\) is a local angular-direction model, while this is a global circle-point incidence model.

A canonical angular-ray graph has not yet been defined for the 19-circle arrangement: it still needs an explicit ray origin, an oriented/unoriented convention, and a rule selecting intersection points.
