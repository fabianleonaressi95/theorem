# Jitterbug: Neumann inductance, finite-surface flux, and Dirichlet–Neumann boundary maps

**Status:** implementation and numerical CI completed successfully (24 November 2026; PR #414). This note separates a shared Green-kernel structure from distinct operators. No physical generator claim is made.

## 1. Actual mechanism replaces the prescribed angular probe

The receiver loops are rigidly attached to the eight triangular panels generated by the existing Gray/Jitterbug path:

\[w(s)\xrightarrow{T\text{-orbit}}W(s)\xrightarrow{\text{rigid triangles}}\{(R_j(s),c_j(s),n_j(s))\}_{j=1}^8.\]

The implementation does not impose a separate sinusoidal angular law. It uses Kabsch panel rotations and centroids from the existing Jitterbug kinematics and eight-coil coupling modules.

A fixed-current circular primary winding supplies a spatially varying field. Its polygonal Biot–Savart field is

\[B(x)=\frac{\mu_0 I_p}{4\pi}\sum_q\frac{\Delta\ell_q\times(x-x_q)}{|x-x_q|^3},\]

where x_q is the segment midpoint. For each receiver disk S_j(s), flux linkage is evaluated by finite-surface quadrature,

\[\lambda_j(s)=N_j\int_{S_j(s)}B(x)\cdot n_j(s)\,dS,\qquad e_j=-\frac{d\lambda_j}{dt}.\]

Thus the external field is not replaced by a uniform-field, centre-point approximation. Disk quadrature and source-filament discretization are explicit convergence parameters.

The receiver inductance matrix uses one common finite-core regularized Neumann Green kernel for both diagonal self terms and off-diagonal mutual terms. Using a thin-wire diagonal approximation together with separately softened mutual terms had produced a negative eigenvalue in the first CI attempt; the implementation now uses the same core radius (the conductor wire radius) in every matrix entry. This consistency is required for the quadratic field-energy form to remain positive semidefinite. The coupled receiver equation remains

\[\frac{d}{dt}(L(s)i)+Ri=e_{\rm ext}.\]

## 2. Receiver-side energy identity

For symmetric L(t), define magnetic energy W_m=1/2 i^T L i. Multiplying the circuit equation by i^T gives

\[i^Te_{\rm ext}=\frac{dW_m}{dt}+i^TRi+\frac12 i^T\dot L i.\]

The last term is the power transferred through the changing receiver inductance (electromechanical conversion term, with sign determined by the chosen positive mechanical coordinate). For a finite step, the midpoint product rule has a cross term. With Δi=i₁−i₀ and ΔL=L₁−L₀, the exact discrete identity used in the code is

\[E_{\rm port}=\Delta W_m+E_{\rm Joule}+\left(\frac12 i_m^T\Delta L i_m-\frac18\Delta i^T\Delta L\Delta i\right),\]

up to floating-point roundoff. The final quadratic correction is reported separately and vanishes as the time step is refined. This prevents a discretization residual from being mislabeled as physical energy production or loss.

The report accumulates all four terms and the residual. This is deliberately a receiver-side balance. It does not yet include the primary winding's electrical supply dynamics, actuator inertia/friction, or the full force law of the source–receiver pair. Therefore it is not a closed energy balance of the complete machine.

## 3. What “Neumann–Dirichlet” means here

There are two related but non-identical uses of “Neumann”:

1. **Neumann mutual-inductance formula (coil coupling).** For two filamentary circuits,
   \[M_{ij}=\frac{\mu_0N_iN_j}{4\pi}\oint_{C_i}\oint_{C_j}\frac{d\ell_i\cdot d\ell_j}{|x-y|}.\]
   This is the Green-kernel interaction of the three-dimensional Laplacian, G(x,y)=1/(4π|x-y|), applied to divergence-free line currents. It is an energy/coupling bilinear form.
2. **Dirichlet-to-Neumann map (boundary-value problem).** In a current-free subdomain, a scalar magnetic potential phi satisfies
   \[\Delta\phi=0,\qquad H=-\nabla\phi.\]
   Its boundary map is
   \[\Lambda_{DN}: f=\phi|_{\partial\Omega}\longmapsto g=\partial_n\phi|_{\partial\Omega}.\]
   The Neumann-to-Dirichlet map takes g back to f. For a connected domain, after fixing the additive constant / restricting to compatible mean-zero data, the ND map is the inverse of the DN map.

The bridge is the **same Laplace Green function and its boundary traces**, not an identity between the coil Neumann integral and the DN map. In boundary-element language, the single-layer boundary operator V, double-layer K, adjoint K', and hypersingular W form the Calderón system. The Neumann line-integral kernel is the free-space Green kernel contracted with current tangents; the DN map is obtained by solving the boundary trace problem and taking a normal derivative. They are different contractions/traces of the same underlying Green operator.

For magnetostatics with actual current filaments, the scalar potential is not globally single-valued on a domain linking those currents. One must either work in a current-free cut domain with circulation/jump data, or use the Coulomb-gauge vector potential

\[-\Delta A=\mu_0J,\qquad \nabla\cdot A=0,\]

and formulate the corresponding vector boundary trace map. This topological qualification is essential; a scalar Laplace DN map cannot simply be applied across a current-carrying winding.

## 4. A precise route to a boundary-operator theorem

A rigorous next theorem candidate should be split into three lemmas:
- **Green-kernel identity:** the mutual-inductance bilinear form is the line-current pairing induced by G=(-Delta)^(-1), under disjoint smooth loops / controlled filament limits.
- **Boundary Calderón identity:** on a specified smooth current-free subdomain, DN and ND maps are related by inversion on compatible trace spaces; establish signs and jump conventions for the selected normal orientation.
- **Coupled reduction:** show when the finite-dimensional coil matrix L_ij is the restriction/projection of the field energy operator to the eight loop-current basis functions, and how the boundary map supplies exterior-domain response/correction terms.

The first is classical magnetostatics; the second is classical elliptic boundary theory; the third is the project-specific result still to be derived. No equality M=L_DN, nor any direct identification with the viscosity DN map, is asserted.

## 7. Connection to the established Q6 hypercube–gradient–Green–Kubo programme

This section connects the electromechanical calculation to existing Q6 results; it does not identify the Jitterbug eight-panel mechanism with the 64-vertex hypercube. The configuration-space/incidence map needed for such an identification remains in issues #398–#401.

### 7.1 The Q6 gradient and its Green operator

Let B1:C1→C0 be the oriented cubical boundary matrix of the Q6 graph, and define the discrete gradient d0=B1^T:C0→C1. With the standard Euclidean cell inner products, the vertex Laplacian is

\[L_0=d_0^*d_0=B_1B_1^T.\]

For a vertex potential u, the discrete Dirichlet energy is

\[\mathcal E_{Q6}(u)=\tfrac12\|d_0u\|^2=\tfrac12u^TL_0u.\]

On the mean-zero subspace, the graph Green operator is \(L_0^\dagger\). It solves \(L_0u=f\) for mean-zero \(f\), and its quadratic form is the inverse Dirichlet energy. The same incidence/adjoint pattern appears in the face-cochain sector, but there the relevant operator is A=Δ2|ker(B3^T), not L0: its dimension is 129 and its exact spectrum is 4^15, 6^40, 8^45, 10^24, 12^5.

### 7.2 Green–Kubo as a dynamical resolvent, not merely a static Green function

Let \(H_0=\{x\in\mathbb R^{64}:\mathbf1^Tx=0\}\), and restrict \(L_0\) to \(H_0\), where it is strictly positive. The specified reversible OU process is
\[
dX_t=-\gamma L_0X_t\,dt+\sqrt{2\gamma\sigma^2L_0}\,dW_t,
\qquad X_t\in H_0,\quad \gamma,\sigma>0.
\]
Its backward generator on smooth functions on \(H_0\) is
\[
(\mathscr Lf)(x)=-\gamma\langle L_0x,\nabla f(x)\rangle
+\gamma\sigma^2\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(L_0\nabla^2f(x)\right),
\]
where the gradient and Hessian are taken intrinsically on \(H_0\). The invariant law is the centered Gaussian \(N_{H_0}(0,\sigma^2I_{H_0})\); equivalently its stationary covariance, viewed on \(\mathbb R^{64}\), is \(\Sigma=\sigma^2P_{H_0}\), with \(P_{H_0}=I-\frac1{64}\mathbf1\mathbf1^T\). The constant mode is therefore absent, not merely assigned a finite relaxation time.

For \(b\in H_0\), define the centered linear observable/current \(J_b(t)=\langle b,X_t\rangle\). Then
\[
C_b(t)=\mathbb E_{\rm eq}[J_b(t)J_b(0)]
=\sigma^2\langle b,e^{-\gamma L_0t}b\rangle.
\]
Since \(L_0|_{H_0}>0\), this correlation is integrable and
\[
\boxed{\int_0^\infty C_b(t)\,dt
=\frac{\sigma^2}{\gamma}\langle b,L_0^\dagger b\rangle
=\frac{\sigma^2}{2\gamma}\sum_{k=1}^6\frac{\|P_kb\|^2}{k}.}
\]
For arbitrary \(b\in\mathbb R^{64}\), the observable depends only on \(b_0=P_{H_0}b\), so the right-hand side must use \(b_0\). The isotropic Q6 mean for \(b\) uniform on the unit sphere of \(H_0\) is \(1517\sigma^2/(7560\gamma)\). In the distinct closed-face OU model, the corresponding exact mean is \(2263\sigma^2/(15480\gamma)\). These are exact for their declared reversible OU models; neither number is a measured material viscosity.

The continuum counterpart is the familiar resolvent identity for a reversible generator \(\mathcal L\): the time-integrated equilibrium correlation is an inverse-generator quadratic form on the centered subspace, provided the integral converges. Thus the shared architecture is gradient → positive Laplacian/energy → Green or inverse operator → time correlation/resolvent. The static Q6 fourth-moment law Δμ4=−8w⁴FQ6 supplies a separate curvature functional; it does not itself specify the stochastic generator or the stress observable.

### 7.3 Where the Jitterbug Neumann and Dirichlet maps enter

The coil Neumann integral is a current-current Green pairing. The magnetic scalar Dirichlet-to-Neumann map is a boundary response for a harmonic potential; the hydrodynamic traction map is a different boundary response for Stokes flow. Their common structure with Q6 is the Green-operator/Dirichlet-form framework, not equality of the operators.

For Stokes flow, the boundary map \(\Lambda_\eta:g\mapsto(2\eta D(u)-pI)n\) depends on the viscosity. The Q6 Green–Kubo programme supplies a candidate route to a constitutive coefficient only after a physical microscopic stress/current, equilibrium dynamics, normalization and limiting procedure are derived. T130's Q6 least-squares shape gradient and positive local resistance assembly provide a discrete geometric/variational component, but their physical calibration and nonlocal Stokes convergence remain open.
