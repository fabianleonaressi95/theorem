# Ratti's vacuum-extraction proposal: bibliography and critical integration with FDT/RGS–PRISM

**Research note — 11 November 2026**  
**Scope:** bibliographic provenance, reconstruction of S. P. Ratti's proposal, comparison with FDT/RGS–PRISM, and evidence classification. This note does not endorse the proposed extraction mechanism.

## 1. Why this material belongs in the FDT/RGS–PRISM dossier

The Ratti documents describe a proposed device intended to separate positrons from virtual electron–positron pairs, transport the positrons, and use annihilation energy; a related extension proposes metallic-ion production and a three-click accumulation/reduction mechanism. This is relevant as a **case study in vacuum-energy claims, gravitomagnetic coupling, particle production, and experimental falsifiability**. It is not, by itself, evidence for FDT, RGS, PRISM, or a geometric vacuum model.

The appropriate integration is comparative and critical: identify the standard phenomena invoked, reconstruct the author's additional assumptions, and state what measurements would be required to promote any claim.

> Established phenomenon → author's proposed mechanism → explicit quantitative map → independent measurement → reproducible energy balance.

A citation to a real effect (Casimir, Lamb shift, pair production, gravitomagnetism) does not validate the additional mechanism proposed by Ratti.

## 3. Reconstructed proposal and quantitative claims

### 3.1 Positron extraction

The proposed sequence is: (i) virtual-pair separation by crossed electric, magnetic and gravitomagnetic fields; (ii) spin reorientation intended to prevent recombination; (iii) transport of positrons through a solenoid and permanent-magnet guide; and (iv) annihilation on ordinary matter, with the released energy described as transported vacuum energy.

The described apparatus includes rotating electrets at approximately ±400 V, two rotating hollow steel spheres containing about 1 cm³ of mercury, rotating disk magnets, a pulsed solenoid of approximately 1000 G, and a five-cell magnetic guide. The claimed operating values include 200/175 rpm, 100 A and a 5 μs pulse, with a stated output of approximately 6.7×10⁸ J and 1.3×10⁵ MW. A separate estimate infers 3.89 MJ (about 1.1 kWh) from a 40 mm steel plate with a 35×35 mm hole and attributes this energy to positron annihilation.

These are **author-reported claims**, not independently established measurements. The hole-based estimate is an inverse energy attribution, not direct detection of positrons. The supplied synopsis reports no calibrated 511 keV annihilation spectrum, gamma-gamma coincidence measurement, complete input/output calorimetry, or independent replication.

### 3.2 Metallic-ion extension

The proposed extension treats the vacuum as supplying ionization energy to create X⁺ ions, which are accelerated into a metal cube, trapped by a surface charge layer, and later neutralized by an electron beam. The synopsis reports a 64.9 kg gold cube, approximately 1.98×10²⁶ atoms, and a claimed formation time of 0.56 s, implying a flux near 3.5×10²⁶ ions/s. These values require independent mass, charge-current, energy and radiation accounting. M⁺ denotes a cation, not an anion.

## 4. Physics audit: what the cited standard effects do and do not establish

### 4.1 Energy-time uncertainty and virtual pairs

The energy-time uncertainty relation is not a license to borrow a real pair's rest energy without an energy source. It does not define a reservoir of extractable energy or a lifetime during which a virtual pair can be converted into an on-shell pair at zero net cost. Real pair creation must satisfy energy-momentum conservation for the full system, including the external field/source and recoil. Spin manipulation does not remove this requirement.

### 4.2 Pair production and antimatter discovery

The discovery of positrons, antiprotons, antideuterons and antihelium establishes the existence of antimatter and the ability to produce it in appropriate high-energy processes. It does not establish that virtual pairs are pre-existing particles that can be separated by a static laboratory apparatus. The anti-⁴He observation belongs to the STAR Collaboration at RHIC in 2011, not to an immediate CERN follow-up to the 1965 antideuteron result.

### 4.3 Casimir, Lamb shift and (g−2)

These are genuine quantum phenomena, but their existence is not evidence for a cyclically extractable vacuum-energy supply. The ideal parallel-plate Casimir pressure is

\[
\frac{F}{A}=-\frac{\pi^2\hbar c}{240d^4}
=-\frac{\pi h c}{480d^4},
\]

where the sign denotes attraction under the usual convention. The separation dependence and dimensions are essential. A formula omitting \(d^{-4}\) is incomplete and dimensionally inconsistent as a pressure.

### 4.4 Gravitomagnetism and the Tajmar claim

Gravitomagnetism is a weak-field approximation within general relativity, not an independently adjustable magnetic-like field with arbitrary laboratory strength. The cited Tajmar rotating-superconductor reports are historical experimental claims; they have not become a generally accepted, independently replicated basis for the very large coupling required by the proposed generator. Any use in a device model must calculate the field from the actual mass-current distribution and compare it with electromagnetic forces and the experimental noise floor.

### 4.5 Spin and the proposed freeze-out

The total angular momentum of the complete system is the conserved quantity. Rotating constituent spin directions does not by itself establish a selection rule that forbids pair annihilation or recombination. A valid model must specify the initial and final quantum states, the interaction Hamiltonian, angular-momentum transfer to the apparatus, and transition amplitudes or rates.

### 4.6 Energy and safety accounting

Any positron flux large enough to account for megajoule-scale annihilation energy entails a correspondingly large gamma-ray signal and radiation dose. The proposal must therefore close the energy balance and radiation transport quantitatively. A magnetic guide or a termite/lead enclosure is not a substitute for shielding design based on the emitted photon spectrum, geometry and dose limits.

The connection to the FDT report should be a separate comparative subsection or appendix, not an additional “pillar” of FDT evidence. The Ratti material does not empirically corroborate FDT's density parameter, Clifford-torus interpretation, or quintuple identity. It is useful because it exposes the same evidential bottlenecks: vacuum ontology, a proposed field-to-particle conversion, a claimed energy source, and the absence of decisive particle-flux and energy-balance measurements.

**Boundary with the Morandotti/RGS–PRISM elasticity program.** The gravitomagnetic field discussed by Ratti is a weak-field GR analogy for rotating mass currents. It is not Cartan torsion, not a Burgers vector, and not a disclination curvature. In the elasticity benchmark, translational and rotational defects are represented by the coframe/connection pair and their torsion/curvature; any coupling to a gravitomagnetic sector would require a separately derived action, units, and observable map. No such identification is made here.

## 7. Falsifiable test protocol

1. **Positron identification:** calibrated HPGe or NaI(Tl) spectroscopy around 511 keV, with detector efficiency and background characterization; use coincidence timing and angular correlations to discriminate annihilation events from unrelated gamma backgrounds.
2. **Independent particle-flux measurement:** a detector arrangement and transport model calibrated for the expected positron energy and angular distribution; do not infer flux solely from a hole or thermal effect.
3. **Full energy balance:** measure all electrical inputs, motor work, pulsed magnetic energy, thermal outputs, radiation energy, and residual stored energy. Publish uncertainty propagation and blind controls.
4. **Field controls:** repeat with mercury spheres stationary, with inert substitutes, and with each field source independently disabled. Record the actual mass/current geometry and calculate the GR weak-field gravitomagnetic contribution before data collection.
5. **Material/ion extension:** measure ion charge current, mass gain/loss, composition/isotopic state, heat, radiation and electrical input independently; close the charge and mass balances.
6. **Independent replication:** preregister apparatus, detector calibration, thresholds, exclusions and analysis. Release raw time-stamped data and calibration certificates.

A positive result requires a reproducible positron signal and a closed energy balance, not merely a macroscopic thermal or mechanical effect. If no positron signature is found at a sensitivity sufficient to test the claimed flux, the positron-annihilation interpretation is not supported.
