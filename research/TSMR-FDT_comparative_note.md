## Abstract

This note places two distinct research programmes side by side. The first is a graph-directed probability measure generated by the single-line-flip walk on the 64 I Ching hexagrams. Its golden scalar coding, exact pair-difference automaton over Z[lambda], and integer path-count certificate yield a lower correlation-dimension bound of approximately 0.6041966 and, consequently, atomlessness.

The second is Manuel Alfaro's Fundamental Density Theory (FDT), as summarized from the material supplied for this note. FDT proposes a unified interpretation of c, a maximum force scale Fmax, quantum action, conic geometry, quantum wave mechanics, the Born rule, gravitational flow, and vortical media. The supplied record labels many derivations as derived. Those labels are internal to the FDT documents and are not independent validation. This note preserves the source classifications while distinguishing algebraic identities, conditional derivations, modelling assumptions, numerical checks, empirical readings, and prospective tests.

No mathematical equivalence between TSMR and FDT is asserted. Their common methodological ground is the use of explicit state spaces, evolution rules, measures/forms, exact identities, finite certificates, and a ledger of assumptions and open obligations.

## 2. TSMR-C-I: exact I Ching/Q6 Markov measure

### 2.1 State space and golden coding

Let lambda = phi^(-1), so lambda^2 + lambda = 1 and r = lambda^6 = 5 - 8 lambda.

A hexagram is h = (d0,...,d5) in {0,1}^6, with lines read bottom-to-top, yang = 1, yin = 0. Its scalar coding is
\[
s(h)=\sum_{k=0}^{5}d_k\lambda^k.
\]
At trigram level, 100 is Zhen (Thunder), 011 is Xun (Wind/Wood), and 1 = lambda + lambda^2. This is a collision of scalar evaluations, not an identification of the two I Ching trigrams.

The transition graph is Q6: two hexagrams are adjacent exactly when one line is flipped. Its simple random walk has P(h,h') = 1/6 for adjacent states, and zero otherwise. This is a precisely defined mathematical model, not a claim that it is the unique traditional I Ching transition rule.

### 2.2 Graph-directed measure

For a stationary Markov path (H_n) with transition matrix P, define
\[
Y=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r^{n-1}s(H_n).
\]
The state-conditioned measures satisfy
\[
\nu_i=\sum_jP_{ij}(S_j)_\#\nu_j,\qquad S_j(x)=rx+s(j).
\]
This is a graph-directed Markov measure, not the i.i.d. Erdős Bernoulli convolution.

The Q6 walk is irreducible with uniform stationary distribution. It is bipartite and has period two. Its eigenvalues are 1-k/3, with multiplicities binomial(6,k), k = 0,...,6. The eigenvalue -1 must not be silently removed by replacing the walk with a lazy walk.

The scalar map has 33 distinct values among 64 states, but 21 of the 33 scalar classes fail strong lumpability. The 64-state lift is therefore retained.

### 2.3 Exact pair-difference automaton

The pair automaton uses pairs (p,q) of Q6 states and a normalized difference delta in Z[lambda]:
\[
\delta_n=r^{-n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}r^{t-1}
       (s(H_t)-s(H'_t)).
\]
Its transition is
\[
(p,q,\delta)\longrightarrow
(j,k,(\delta+s(j)-s(k))/r),\quad j\sim p,\;k\sim q.
\]

The exact construction includes all 4096 ordered initial pairs (p,q,0), and uses both real and conjugate embeddings to bound reachable normalized differences. The resulting finite enclosure has:
- 5,566 reachable automaton states;
- 34,762 directed transitions;
- 13 exact values of delta = a + b lambda;
- exact Bellman hull extrema
\[
\min_iL_i=\frac7{40}-\frac{11}{40}\lambda,\quad
\max_iU_i=\frac{73}{40}+\frac{51}{40}\lambda;
\]
- global hull diameter D = 33/20 + 31 lambda/20.

The integer adjacency matrix A satisfies
\[
\|A^{20}\|_\infty=9412274067081088.
\]
With rho_* = (9412274067081088)^(1/20), the stated path-count argument gives
\[
C_\nu(Dr^n)\leq C_0(\rho_*/36)^n,
\]
and therefore
\[
\underline D_2(\nu)\geq
\frac{\log(36/\rho_*)}{\log(1/r)}
\simeq0.6041966.
\]
A geometric-scale Markov inequality and Borel–Cantelli then yield the same lower bound for the lower local dimension at nu-almost every point. In particular, nu is atomless.

**Scope:** this does not prove that the local dimension exists as a limit, that the measure is exact-dimensional, absolutely continuous, singular, or has interval support. It does not establish the full multifractal spectrum.

**Proof obligations for an independent paper:** publish the complete finite-state transition-generation specification; prove the real/conjugate bounds that guarantee coverage of every sufficiently close pair; make the path-count-to-correlation estimate explicit, including constants and scale conversion; and independently verify the Borel–Cantelli implication. The test suite is a reproducibility check, not a substitute for these proofs.

## 3. Literature boundary for overlap methods

The general method of finite-state overlap coding is not new.

1. **Lau–Ngai (2007), “A generalized finite type condition for iterated function systems”, Advances in Mathematics 208(2), 647–671.** They introduce generalized finite type for overlapping contractive similitudes, relate the attractor to a graph-directed IFS, and compute Hausdorff dimension using weighted incidence matrices. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aim.2006.03.007.
2. **Ngai–Wang–Dong (2010), “Graph-Directed Iterated Function Systems Satisfying the Generalized Finite Type Condition”, Nonlinearity 23(9), 2333–2350.** They extend generalized finite type to graph-directed IFSs with overlaps and obtain dimension results from weighted incidence matrices. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1088/0951-7715/23/9/013.
3. **Feng–Feng (2022), “Estimates on the dimension of self-similar measures with overlaps”, Journal of the London Mathematical Society 105, 2104–2135.** They give an algorithm for lower bounds on dimensions of self-similar measures with overlaps, including Bernoulli-convolution applications and matrix-pressure methods for Pisot parameters. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms.12555.
4. **Xie (2025), “L^q-spectrum of graph-directed self-similar measures that have overlaps and are essentially of finite type”, Indagationes Mathematicae 36(5), 1355–1404.** This develops closed formulas and differentiability results for the L^q-spectrum, q >= 0, of graph-directed self-similar measures with overlaps under an essentially finite type (EFT) condition, without requiring the graph open set condition. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.indag.2025.05.013; preprint https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09459.

### 3.1 Novelty boundary

The following are not claimed as new in themselves: finite overlap graphs, graph-directed reformulations, spectral-radius methods, matrix pressure, lower dimension estimates for overlapping measures, or multifractal L^q-spectra under finite-type hypotheses.

The candidate contribution is the specific exact realization: the I Ching hexagram state space, Q6 line-flip Markov dynamics, golden algebraic observable, exact pair-difference enclosure, and explicit integer certificate leading to the lower bound 0.6041966 and atomlessness.

This is not yet a certified literature-priority claim:
- A finite pair automaton does not by itself prove that the measure satisfies Xie's EFT definition. The exact EFT-family and cell partitions must be exhibited, or EFT must be shown not to apply.
- Feng–Feng's method must be compared at the level of hypotheses, transition objects, and output bound. A different implementation is not automatically a new principle.
- The novelty of the numerical certificate must be assessed against equivalent matrix/path-count constructions.
- The close-pair coverage lemma and dimension inference need a proof readable without executing the code.

**Current verdict:** general framework known; exact Q6/I Ching/golden instance is a plausible specific contribution; priority remains unverified.

### 4.2 Three-scale identity: c, Fmax, hbar

FDT organizes its conversions around three constants:
\[
c^2=\frac1{\varepsilon_0\mu_0}=\frac Em=\left(\frac dt\right)^2,
\]
\[
F_{\max}=\frac{c^4}{4G},\qquad
G=\frac{c^4}{4F_{\max}},\qquad
\kappa=\frac{2\pi}{F_{\max}}=\frac{8\pi G}{c^4},
\]
and
\[
ET_q=h,\qquad E_{\rm floor}t_{\min}=\hbar.
\]
The supplied value is Fmax approximately 3.0256 x 10^43 N.

The first line combines the Maxwell relation for the vacuum wave speed with a proposed strand/stroke interpretation of E/m and d/t. The Maxwell identity is established within classical electromagnetism; the interpretation of c as a strand rate is an FDT model interpretation, not a consequence of c^2 = 1/(epsilon0 mu0) alone.

The force scale is an exact algebraic rewriting of the definition Fmax = c^4/(4G). It is not an independently measured fourth constant. The coefficient identity kappa = 2pi/Fmax = 8piG/c^4 is exact given that definition.

FDT's length/time reading is
\[
E_0=F_{\max}d_s,\quad mc=F_{\max}t_s,\quad
d_s=2R_s,\quad t_s=d_s/c,
\]
so E0/m = c^2 = (d_s/t_s)^2. Dimensionally, Fmax d_s is energy and Fmax t_s is momentum. Their physical identification with a universal strand segment is a model claim requiring its own derivation.

For the proposed floor,
\[
d_{\min}=2\ell_P,\quad t_{\min}=2t_P,\quad
F_{\max}d_{\min}t_{\min}=\hbar.
\]
This equality follows algebraically from the standard Planck definitions and Fmax = c^4/(4G). It is an exact Planck-unit identity, not by itself evidence that nature contains a dynamically inaccessible or unoccupied floor. Further claims — E0 < E_floor, exactly one length above the floor, and T_br -> Fmax/(2pi) = 1/kappa — depend on FDT's definitions and closure assumptions.

The proposed strand kinematics are
\[
v^2+u^2=c^2,\qquad c^2d\tau^2=c^2dt^2-dx^2.
\]
The second equation is the standard Minkowski proper-time relation for timelike motion; the decomposition into translational and internal/helical components is an interpretation requiring separate justification. The pi/4 open-stroke helix and spin-1 assignment do not follow from the displayed identity alone.

### 4.3 Maximum-force and gravitational coupling

FDT writes
\[
F=F_{\max}\alpha_1\alpha_2=\frac{GMm}{r^2}
\]
on a stated domain of spherical supports. If alpha_1 = 2GM/(c^2r) and alpha_2 = 2Gm/(c^2r), the equality is an algebraic rewriting:
\[
\frac{c^4}{4G}
\frac{2GM}{c^2r}\frac{2Gm}{c^2r}
=\frac{GMm}{r^2}.
\]
This establishes the formula under those definitions; it does not independently derive Newtonian gravity from a microscopic mechanism.

The field-equation rearrangement is
\[
T_{\mu\nu}=\frac{F_{\max}}{2\pi}G_{\mu\nu}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
G_{\mu\nu}=\kappa T_{\mu\nu},
\]
provided kappa = 2pi/Fmax = 8piG/c^4. This is the standard Einstein equation written with a reparameterized coupling. A derivation of the field equations would require an independent derivation of the constitutive relation for T_mu_nu, not merely rearrangement.

### 4.4 Conic geometry, Kepler motion, and psi

The conic classification by e < 1, e = 1, e > 1 is the standard Kepler/Laplace–Runge–Lenz classification in an inverse-square potential, expressed in the proposed lifted geometry. It is not, without further argument, a new derivation of conic sections.

The supplied FDT formulas include

With ell = a(1-e^2) and Rs = 2GM/c^2, the last expression is the standard leading Schwarzschild result 6piGM/[c^2a(1-e^2)]. Its agreement is a consistency check of the parameterization, not evidence of a different observable. The Earth-observer helix advance and stated action-per-orbit values are model interpretations/numerical evaluations whose definitions and reference frames must be explicit.

### 4.5 Schrödinger/Madelung form and quantum claims

These are the Madelung equations and phase-winding condition associated with the Schrödinger equation, subject to regularity and nodal/topological qualifications. Writing i as a unit bivector Sigma = e1e2, Sigma^2 = -1, is compatible with geometric-algebra representations of the complex structure, but does not alone derive quantum mechanics.

The Coulomb spectrum
\[
E_n=-\frac{mK^2}{2\hbar^2n^2}

is the standard nonrelativistic hydrogenic spectrum for the appropriate Coulomb coupling and reduced mass. Any claim that FDT derives it from a new confinement operator must discharge O-9: specify the operator, domain, self-adjointness, spectrum, and mass identification. The supplied note itself marks parts of the mass/operator derivation as owed.

The profile term Q_rho = -(hbar^2/2m) Laplacian(rho)/rho, the interference cosine identity, and phase quantization are standard consequences/representations of the wavefunction formalism. A value predicted by standard quantum theory does not show that the FDT mechanism independently derives it.

### 4.6 Born-rule proposal

FDT proposes three routes to
\[
\Pr(k)=A_k^2=|\langle\hat e_k,\psi\rangle|^2:

(i) a frame-function route using port locality and Cauchy-type constraints; (ii) an energy-ledger/seating route; (iii) a deterministic threshold mechanism in which the first port whose closure drive exceeds a resident disturbance is selected.

The supplied mechanism uses
\[
\Upsilon_k(t)=\int^t\frac{I_k(t')}{1-\eta}\,dt',
\]
with an exponential resident threshold and a claimed uniform distribution of seating fraction. The time-resolved law is stated as
\[
\Pr(k^*=k,\;t^*\in[t,t+dt))=I_k(t)\,dt.
\]
The source explicitly marks port locality, seating independence, the resident Gaussian/circular law, and the seating rule as posited or conditional inputs. Consequently, the Born conclusion is conditional on these premises and on the correctness of the threshold construction. Numerical checks V1–V10 are evidence of internal consistency of the implementation, not proof that the premises describe physical measurement.

A formal audit should state Hilbert-space dimension, admissible frame functions, orthogonality assumptions, treatment of degenerate outcomes, and whether the argument invokes Gleason-type hypotheses or a separate dimension-two result. The three routes must be checked for hidden dependence on common assumptions.

## 6. TSMR–FDT comparison: legitimate intersections and non-claims

### 6.1 Legitimate mathematical common ground

1. **State space and dynamics.** TSMR specifies a finite Markov state space and transition matrix. FDT specifies strand, flow, seating, or vortex dynamics. Both require state variables and evolution laws to be explicit.
2. **Measures and cumulative coordinates.** TSMR constructs a stationary graph-directed measure and studies its dimension/regularity. FDT's Born proposal constructs outcome probabilities from a proposed seating measure. A rigorous bridge would require an explicit measurable map and proof that it transports one measure to the other.
3. **Exterior calculus.** FDT uses W = d(vars) and dW = 0. TSMR's broader programme uses chain complexes and boundary operators. The shared identity is d^2 = 0 / partial^2 = 0, but a common complex, grading, and physical interpretation have not been supplied.
4. **Finite certificates.** TSMR's integer path-count certificate and FDT's receipt scripts are reproducibility artifacts. Their epistemic force is limited to what the checked code and assumptions establish.
5. **Golden algebra.** TSMR uses Z[lambda], lambda^2 + lambda = 1. The supplied FDT summary contains no established derivation requiring this same golden ring. No golden/FDT identification is claimed.

### 6.2 Explicit non-claims

This note does not claim that:
- the Q6 Markov measure derives the Born rule;
- the I Ching trigram collision 100 / 011 is a physical quantum transition;
- the golden contraction r = lambda^6 is an FDT scale;
- FDT derives the Q6 automaton, or that the automaton validates FDT;
- the maximum-force rewriting independently derives Newtonian or Einstein gravity;
- reproducing standard GR/QM formulas in new variables is, by itself, a new physical theory;
- the 51-body in-sample comparison or CMS prospective tests establish a new field mechanism.

## 9. Minkowski light cone, projective dynamics, and the question-mark staircase

A further structural connection concerns the word *Minkowski*, which must be used carefully: Minkowski spacetime and Minkowski's question-mark function are distinct constructions. Their possible bridge is projective dynamics, not a direct identity.

### 9.1 Correct dimension and the projective null conic

A necessary dimensional correction: in 1+1-dimensional Minkowski spacetime the projectivized null cone consists of only two points, not an interval. The relevant construction takes place in a 2+1-dimensional Minkowski subspace (or a chosen 2+1 slice of 3+1 spacetime).

Use coordinates \((X,Y,Z)\) with quadratic form
\[
Q(X,Y,Z)=X^2+Y^2-Z^2.
\]
The future null cone is \(Q=0, Z>0\). Its projectivization is a conic in \(\mathbb{RP}^2\), hence a circle, and is parametrized by the projective spinor coordinate \([p:q]\in\mathbb{RP}^1\):
\[
[X:Y:Z]=[p^2-q^2:2pq:p^2+q^2].
\]
Indeed, \((p^2-q^2)^2+(2pq)^2=(p^2+q^2)^2\). The identity component of the Lorentz group is isomorphic to \(PSL(2,\mathbb R)\); its action on the null conic is the projective Möbius action on \(\mathbb{RP}^1\). This is the precise geometric source of the fractional-linear transformations. [See the cited 2+1 spacetime/projective-structure reference.]

### 9.2 How the Farey map is obtained — and what must be chosen

Put \(x=p/q\) in the chart \(p,q\geq0\), \(0\leq x\leq1\). The two branches of the Farey map are
\[
F(x)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{x}{1-x},&0\leq x\leq\tfrac12,\\[4pt]
\dfrac{1-x}{x},&\tfrac12\leq x\leq1.
\end{cases}
\]
They are exactly the projective actions of the integer matrices
\[
A_0=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-1&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
A_1=\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
\]
because
\[
A_0[p:q]=[p:q-p],\qquad
A_1[p:q]=[q-p:p].
\]
The first matrix has determinant +1 and the second determinant -1. Thus the branches lie in the projective action of \(PGL(2,\mathbb Z)\); the second uses an orientation-reversing component. Both preserve the projective null conic through the symmetric-square representation. On the interval, the branch condition is precisely whether \(p\leq q-p\) or \(p\geq q-p\), i.e. the subtractive Euclidean algorithm on the pair \((p,q-p)\).

This is the sought construction, with an important qualification. The Lorentz cone supplies the projective conic and the Möbius action, but it does **not** select the integer lattice \(\mathbb Z^2\), the positive cone \(p,q\geq0\), or the branch cut \(p=q/2\). Those are extra arithmetic/dynamical data. Once the integral spinor lattice and this Euclidean-algorithm partition are specified, the Farey map is induced by piecewise integral projective Lorentz transformations. It is therefore a *constructed arithmetic dynamics on the null conic*, not a consequence of Lorentz invariance alone.

For a 3+1-dimensional physical model this construction is restricted to a chosen 2+1-dimensional Minkowski subspace (equivalently, a great-circle section of the celestial sphere). Extending it canonically to the full celestial sphere would require additional structure and is not claimed here.

### 9.3 Minkowski's question-mark function as the conjugacy

For \(x=[0;a_1,a_2,\ldots]\), define
\[
?(x)=\sum_{k\geq1}(-1)^{k-1}
2^{-(a_1+\cdots+a_k-1)}.
\]
The continuous extension to rational endpoints gives an increasing homeomorphism of \([0,1]\). It conjugates the Farey map above to the tent map
\[
T(y)=
\begin{cases}
2y,&0\leq y\leq\tfrac12,\\
2(1-y),&\tfrac12\leq y\leq1,
\end{cases}
\qquad ?\circ F=T\circ ?.
\]
The conjugacy follows from the continued-fraction branch rules: on the left branch the first partial quotient decreases by one; on the right branch the first digit is one and the remaining coding is complemented. This is a classical theorem, not a new result of the present programme. [Sources: the dynamical-systems text and the reference on the question-mark function.]

Consequently, after the arithmetic choices in §9.2, the projective null-cone dynamics is conjugated by \(?\) to dyadic tent dynamics. The singularity of \(?\) means this is not a smooth Lorentz-coordinate change: it is a singular topological conjugacy that changes the natural coordinate measure.

### 9.4 The question-mark map as a singular change of projective coordinate

Minkowski's question-mark function \(?(x)\) is a strictly increasing singular homeomorphism of [0,1]. It maps the Farey/Stern–Brocot coding to dyadic coding and conjugates the Farey map to the tent map. Thus it can be interpreted as a nonlinear, singular reparametrization of a one-dimensional projective coordinate.

If a coordinate x on a family of null directions or on a conic section is identified with a normalized projective slope, then
\[
q=?(x)
\]
provides a well-defined alternative coordinate. This changes the measure and the regularity of the coordinate parametrization; it does not change the underlying null cone or the Lorentzian metric. A physical interpretation requires a separately derived dynamical or observational meaning for q.

### 9.5 Relation to the FDT cone parameter

FDT introduces
\[
\alpha_{\rm ext}=R_s/r_f,\qquad 0<\alpha_{\rm ext}<1.
\]
Since this parameter lies in the unit interval, one may define the candidate coordinate
\[
q_{\rm ext}=?( \alpha_{\rm ext}).
\]
This is mathematically legitimate as a reparametrization. It is not yet an FDT theorem: the supplied FDT material does not establish that alpha_ext carries Farey dynamics, that its natural invariant measure is Minkowski's question-mark measure, or that the singular coordinate q_ext is physically preferred.

The exact research question is therefore: can the cone's projective coordinate, under the FDT flow or orbit dynamics, be shown to evolve by a Farey-type map (or a Möbius system conjugate to it)? If yes, the question-mark function could linearize or symbolically recode that dynamics. If not, q_ext remains an optional singular coordinate change.

### 9.6 Relation to TSMR cumulative morphodynamics

For an atomless probability measure mu on an interval, its cumulative distribution
\[
F_\mu(x)=\mu([a,x])
\]
is continuous. If mu has full support, F_mu is strictly increasing and is a homeomorphism onto [0,1]. If mu is also singular with respect to Lebesgue measure, it is a singular increasing homeomorphism, of the same broad regularity type as the question-mark function.

The TSMR-C-I result establishes atomlessness for its Q6 Markov measure, but does not yet establish full support or singularity. Consequently, its cumulative function is continuous, but strict increase and singularity must not be asserted until those two additional properties are proved. The golden Bernoulli convolution and the Q6 Markov measure are distinct measures and must not be conflated.

A precise bridge would require proving that a measure generated by the cone/projective dynamics is transported by a cumulative map to Lebesgue measure, or that its cumulative map is conjugate to the Minkowski question-mark function. Neither statement is currently established.

### 9.7 Diagram of the proposed bridge

\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
\text{Minkowski null cone} & \longrightarrow & \text{projective null directions}\\
&&\downarrow\ \text{chosen Möbius/Farey dynamics}\\
&&\text{Farey/Stern--Brocot coding}\\
&&\downarrow\ ?(x)\\
&&\text{dyadic/tent-map coordinate}
\end{array}
\]

The arrow from projective null directions to Farey dynamics is supplied only after choosing the integral spinor lattice, positive cone, and Euclidean-algorithm branch partition in §9.2. Without those choices it is not canonical. The question-mark function then supplies the classical conjugacy to the tent map.

**Conclusion:** the construction now gives an explicit conditional bridge: a 2+1 Minkowski null conic carries a chosen arithmetic piecewise-projective dynamics; that dynamics is the Farey map; and Minkowski's question-mark function conjugates it to the tent map. What remains open for FDT is whether its physical cone variable and equations select this arithmetic structure, rather than merely allowing it as an imposed coordinate model.

## 10. Does the FDT radial variable generate Farey dynamics?

This section tests the proposed identification against the actual evolution law present in the supplied FDT synthesis, rather than merely observing that \(\alpha_{\rm ext}\in(0,1)\).

### 10.1 The discrete map that Farey dynamics would require

Write \(R=R_s>0\), \(r=r_f>R\), and
\[
\alpha=\frac{R}{r}\in(0,1).
\]
If \(\alpha\) is to evolve by the Farey map of §9, then its next-step value must be
\[
\alpha'=
\begin{cases}
\alpha/(1-\alpha),&0<\alpha\leq1/2,\\
(1-\alpha)/\alpha,&1/2\leq\alpha<1.
\end{cases}
\]
In the radial variable this is exactly
\[
\boxed{
r'=
\begin{cases}
r-R,&r\geq2R,\\[2pt]
R^2/(r-R),&R<r\leq2R.
\end{cases}}
\tag{10.1}
\]
To verify: substitute \(\alpha=R/r\) into each branch and use \(r'=R/\alpha'\). Both branches map the exterior interval \((R,\infty)\) onto \([R,\infty)\), with the branch point \(r=2R\) mapped to \(r'=R\). In dimensionless radius \(s=r/R>1\), the map is
\[
s'=
\begin{cases}
s-1,&s\geq2,\\
1/(s-1),&1<s\leq2.
\end{cases}
\]
This is a piecewise \(PGL(2,\mathbb Z)\) map: subtract one on the outer branch and invert after subtracting one on the inner branch. It is an exact reformulation of the Farey map, not a new physical law.

### 10.2 What follows if the FDT inflow is taken as a radial velocity

The supplied FDT synthesis gives
\[
\mathbf v_{\rm in}=-c\sqrt{\alpha}\,\hat{\mathbf r}.
\]
If, and only if, this is interpreted as the coordinate radial velocity of the same radius \(r_f(t)\), then
\[
\dot r=-c\sqrt{R/r}.
\]
Since \(\alpha=R/r\) and \(R\) is constant,
\[
\dot\alpha=-\frac{R}{r^2}\dot r
=\frac{c}{R}\alpha^{5/2}.
\]
This autonomous ODE has the exact solution
\[
\boxed{
\alpha(t)=
\left[
\alpha_0^{-3/2}
-\frac{3c}{2R}(t-t_0)
\right]^{-2/3}}
\tag{10.2}
\]
on the interval where the bracket is positive. Equivalently,
\[
r(t)^{3/2}=r_0^{3/2}
-\frac{3}{2}c\sqrt R\,(t-t_0).
\]
The flow is continuous, monotone, and deterministic; it is not the two-branch Farey map. Its time to reach \(\alpha=1\), under this coordinate-velocity interpretation, is
\[
t_*-t_0=\frac{2R}{3c}
\left(\alpha_0^{-3/2}-1\right).
\]
This finite-time expression is conditional on the identification of \(\mathbf v_{\rm in}\) with \(d r_f/dt\), which the supplied summary does not independently establish.

### 10.3 No-go statement from the currently supplied FDT data

The current FDT record specifies the compactness variable \(R_s/r_f\), a radial inflow profile, and static conic/orbit relations. It does **not** specify:
- a discrete return time or Poincaré section on which \(r_f\mapsto r_f'\) is defined;
- a branch variable selecting \(r_f\geq2R_s\) versus \(R_s<r_f\leq2R_s\);
- an integer spinor lattice or an equivalent arithmetic invariant;
- a derivation of either radial update in (10.1) from an action, geodesic equation, matching condition, or conservation law.

Therefore the Farey map is **not derivable from the supplied FDT equations as they stand**. The exact radial map (10.1) identifies what would have to be derived; the continuous ODE (10.2) shows that the natural reading of the supplied inflow does not itself produce it.

This is a precise obstruction, not a proof that no such extension exists. A valid FDT-to-Farey theorem would need to derive a discrete section/return map and prove that its two branches reduce to (10.1), with the branch partition and integer structure fixed by FDT dynamics rather than inserted by hand.

### 10.4 Falsifiable derivation target

A minimal theorem target is:

> **Candidate FDT–Farey theorem.** There exists an FDT-defined Poincaré section \(\Sigma\), a dimensionless radial observable \(\alpha=R_s/r_f\), and an FDT-derived branch partition of \(\Sigma\) such that the first-return map induces
> \[
> \alpha\circ\mathcal P=
> \begin{cases}
> \alpha/(1-\alpha),&\alpha\leq1/2,\\
> (1-\alpha)/\alpha,&\alpha\geq1/2.
> \end{cases}
> \]
> The section, return time, branch selection, and integer/projective structure must all be derived from the FDT equations and boundary data.

If this theorem is proved, the classical identity \(?\circ F=T\circ ?\) then gives the singular conjugacy to tent dynamics. Until then, \(?(R_s/r_f)\) is a permissible coordinate change, not a dynamically selected FDT observable.

**Status:** candidate theorem stated; not proved. Current result: exact conditional radial reformulation plus a conditional continuous-flow calculation; no FDT derivation of Farey dynamics yet.


### 10.5 The FDT critical value \(\alpha_c=2/3\) fixes a projective Farey chart

The FDT evidence report identifies three regimes separated by \(\alpha_c=2/3\). There is a unique real Möbius transformation fixing the endpoints 0 and 1 and sending \(2/3\) to the Farey branch point \(1/2\):
\[
\boxed{x=h(\alpha)=\frac{\alpha}{2-\alpha}},\qquad
h^{-1}(x)=\frac{2x}{1+x}.
\]
Uniqueness follows because a projective transformation is determined by its values at three distinct points. It is increasing on \((0,1)\), maps \((0,1)\) onto itself, and
\[
h(0)=0,\qquad h(2/3)=1/2,\qquad h(1)=1.
\]
Thus the FDT critical boundary is exactly the Farey branch boundary in this normalized projective chart. This alignment is derived from the three distinguished values; it does not yet derive a time evolution.

Conjugate the standard Farey map \(F\) by \(h\):
\[
G=h^{-1}\circ F\circ h.
\]
Direct substitution gives the FDT-coordinate map
\[
\boxed{
G(\alpha)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{2\alpha}{2-\alpha},&0<\alpha\leq2/3,\\[5pt]
\dfrac{4(1-\alpha)}{2-\alpha},&2/3\leq\alpha<1.
\end{cases}}
\tag{10.3}
\]
The two formulas agree at \(\alpha=2/3\), where \(G(2/3)=1\). The first branch maps \((0,2/3]\) increasingly onto \((0,1]\); the second maps \([2/3,1)\) decreasingly onto \((0,1]\). The branch partition now coincides exactly with the FDT attractive/repulsive threshold.

The induced radial form, using \(r=R_s/\alpha\), is
\[
\boxed{
r'=
\begin{cases}
r-R_s/2,&r\geq3R_s/2,\\[4pt]
\dfrac{R_s(2r-R_s)}{4(r-R_s)},&
R_s<r\leq3R_s/2.
\end{cases}}
\tag{10.4}
\]
This is the precise radial update that a FDT return map would have to reproduce if the critical value \(2/3\) is its branch point. It is projectively conjugate to Farey by construction.

Define the corresponding singular coordinate
\[
\boxed{q_{\rm FDT}(\alpha)=?\!\left(\frac{\alpha}{2-\alpha}\right)}.
\]
Then the classical Farey/question-mark conjugacy implies
\[
q_{\rm FDT}(G(\alpha))=T(q_{\rm FDT}(\alpha)).
\]
This is an exact theorem about the *constructed map* \(G\), not yet about physical FDT trajectories. It is better adapted than using \(? (\alpha)\) directly: it respects the FDT critical partition at \(2/3\).

### 10.6 Event section versus Poincaré return: the obstruction

Under the conditional inflow ODE (10.2), \(\dot\alpha>0\). The surface
\[
\Sigma_c=\{\alpha=2/3\}
\]
is a transverse **event section** for trajectories starting below the threshold, not a recurrent Poincaré section: each monotone radial trajectory crosses it at most once. The crossing time is
\[
t_c-t_0=\frac{2R_s}{3c}
\left[
\alpha_0^{-3/2}-(3/2)^{3/2}
\right],
\qquad 0<\alpha_0<2/3.
\]
After crossing, the same one-dimensional flow does not return to \(\Sigma_c\). Consequently it cannot by itself generate the two-branch return map (10.3). A genuine Poincaré map would require at least one additional dynamical degree of freedom (for example an angular/projective phase), a return mechanism, or a reflection/matching rule at the critical surface. None is specified in the supplied FDT synthesis.

**Refined status.**
- **Derived:** the unique endpoint-fixing Möbius chart that sends the FDT value \(2/3\) to Farey value \(1/2\); the conjugated map (10.3); its radial form (10.4); and the singular coordinate \(q_{\rm FDT}\).
- **Conditional:** the monotone event-crossing time, assuming the supplied inflow is \(dr_f/dt\).
- **Open:** whether FDT field equations, an action, or matching conditions produce the return mechanism and the map (10.3). The value \(2/3\) selects the correct projective branch point, but does not by itself supply recurrence or arithmetic dynamics.
