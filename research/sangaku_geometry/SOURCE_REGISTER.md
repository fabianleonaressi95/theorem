# Source register — Sangaku geometry corpus

This register distinguishes bibliographic verification from exact geometric reconstruction and from visual inspection of original figures.

| ID | Source | Geometric object | Evidence currently established | Remaining work |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SRC-001 | Sparavigna & Baldi, *Japanese Temple Geometry: A Digital Sangaku About a Regular Pentagon and the Golden Ratio* (2016), DOI 10.2139/ssrn.2836615 | Hirano construction; auxiliary circles with diameters AO/BO; circle centred at C through T | Full textual construction and equations transcribed; independent exact coordinate proof in SGC-004 | Inspect source PDF figures directly; compare every label and orientation with coordinate diagram |
| SRC-002 | Sparavigna & Baldi, *A Mathematical Study of a Symbol: The Vesica Piscis of Sacred Geometry* (2016), Philica 560 | Two equal circles, centre of each on the other circumference | Source text explicitly fixes equal radii and centre separation equal to radius; derives perpendicular chord and sqrt(3) ratio | Expand to exact labelled configuration and distinguish paper's symbolic interpretation from geometry |
| SRC-003 | Sparavigna & Baldi, *Flower of Life, Six-Fold Symmetry and Honeycomb Packing of Circles in the Mycenaean Geometry* (2016), DOI 10.2139/ssrn.2756099 | Mycenaean gold roundel, compass-drawn overlapping circles, hexagonal arrangement | Article identifies roundel #38, describes compass construction, six-fold rotational symmetry, and contrasts three-fold/chiral examples; an exact 19-circle triangular-lattice idealization is now formalized separately as SGC-005 | Inspect and calibrate the archaeological plate; compare its actual circle centres/radii with the ideal lattice; quantify residuals and uncertainty before historical identification |
| SRC-004 | Sparavigna & Baldi, *A Study of the Regular Pentagon with a Classic Geometric Approach* (2016), HAL hal-01295771 | Regular pentagon metric identities | Source record and text confirm side/diagonal/radius/apothem relations to golden ratio | Keep as metric reference; do not treat as separate Sangaku construction |
| SRC-005 | Bogomolny, “A Sangaku with Many Circles and Some,” Cut-the-Knot | SGC-003 equal-radius model with four outer circles and r3/r4 circles | Secondary page gives the exact incidence description and equations used in SGC-003; wording says the applet “purports to suggest” an 1837 Aichi Sangaku, citing *Temple Geometry* 1.5.7 | Inspect the cited 1989 source page and locate primary tablet/archival photograph; do not treat the web applet as primary evidence |
| SRC-006 | Fukagawa & Rothman, *Sacred Mathematics: Japanese Temple Geometry* (2008), Chapter 4, Problem 16, Figure 4.15, p. 101; solution p. 126 | 1837 Ohsu Kannon tablet: four equal r-circles inside R, plus p and q circles | Published text identifies the 1837 Ohsu Kannon attribution; Figure 4.15 visually inspected; solution derives p=q | Original tablet and Nagata Toshimasa manuscript not inspected; this is a distinct diagram from SGC-003 |

## Transcription policy

1. Preserve original point labels in a source transcription layer.
2. Add a separate normalized coordinate layer; do not silently relabel the source.
3. Record whether a diagram is a historical artifact, a modern redraw, or an idealized mathematical figure.
4. Distinguish exact Euclidean constructions from raster/pixel implementations.
5. Record every source claim with page/figure and evidence status.
6. A secondary reconstruction that cites a historical tablet is not itself the original tablet; compare its incidence graph with independently documented tablet diagrams before merging case identities.
