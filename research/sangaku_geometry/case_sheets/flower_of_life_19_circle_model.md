# SGC-005 — Flower of Life: exact 19-circle hexagonal model

## Status and scope

**MODEL / EXACT-COMPUTATION.** This case sheet defines a reproducible ideal Euclidean model: 19 congruent unit circles whose centres are the radius-two hexagonal patch of the triangular lattice. It is a canonical finite Flower-of-Life-type arrangement. It is **not** asserted to be a pixel-perfect reconstruction of the Mycenaean gold roundel discussed by Sparavigna and Baldi. Their 2016 paper describes a gold roundel decoration that resembles the Flower of Life and discusses six-fold symmetry, compass construction, curvilinear triangles and hexagonal circle packing; exact historical centre/radius data must be calibrated from the primary image before historical identification.

Source: A. C. Sparavigna and M. M. Baldi, *Flower of Life, Six-Fold Symmetry and Honeycomb Packing of Circles in the Mycenaean Geometry* (2016), DOI [10.2139/ssrn.2756099](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.2756099), [SSRN record](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2756099), [Politecnico di Torino repository](https://iris.polito.it/handle/11583/2786005).

## Definition

Let
\[
H_2=\{(m,n)\in\mathbb Z^2:\max(|m|,|n|,|m+n|)\leq 2\}.
\]
This set has 19 elements. Map axial lattice coordinates to the Euclidean plane by
\[
p(m,n)=\left(m+\frac n2,\frac{\sqrt3}{2}n\right).
\]
For each (m,n) in H2, place a unit circle centred at p(m,n). The central circle and the two hexagonal rings give 1+6+12=19 circles. This definition fixes a scale; all other scales follow by similarity.

## Exact invariants of the centre configuration

For two axial lattice points, the squared distance is
\[
|p(m,n)-p(m',n')|^2=a^2+ab+b^2,\quad a=m-m',\ b=n-n'.
\]
It is an integer. The coordinate field of the centres in this frame is Q(sqrt(3)); the field generated by their squared mutual distances and squared radii is Q. The field generated by the nonzero centre distances in this finite patch is
\[
\mathbb Q(\sqrt3,\sqrt7,\sqrt{13}),
\]
since the occurring squared distances include 3, 7 and 13, and all squared distances are integers whose square-free parts lie in {1,3,7,13}. The three square classes are independent, so this multiquadratic field has degree 8 over Q.

The centre set is invariant under the order-12 dihedral group D6, generated in axial coordinates by
\[
R(m,n)=(-n,m+n),\quad S(m,n)=(m+n,-n).
\]
This is the symmetry group of the unlabelled finite centre/circle arrangement. Any additional labels or construction arrows must be checked separately and can reduce the marked symmetry group.

These field statements concern the centres and radii only. They do **not** assert that every intersection point of every pair of circles belongs to Q(sqrt(3),sqrt(7),sqrt(13)). Intersection coordinates must be adjoined and audited separately.

## Incidence and intersection convention

Two unit circles with centre separation squared d² have two transverse intersections if d²<4, one tangency point if d²=4, and no real intersection if d²>4. The model includes all 19 circles, not only the visible arcs selected in a particular artistic rendering. A future diagram transcription must record which arcs and intersection points are actually marked in the source.

## Historical boundary

Sparavigna–Baldi discuss a Mycenaean gold roundel and compare its decoration with a Flower-of-Life pattern. They also discuss other six-fold and three-fold decorations, including chiral examples. This supports studying six-fold symmetry as a geometric feature; it does not establish that the artifact was generated by exactly the 19-circle construction defined here, nor does it establish a ritual interpretation. The archaeological image, its scale, circle centres, radii, and uncertainty should be digitized before making that stronger claim.
