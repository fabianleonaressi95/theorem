# Case SGC-004 — Hirano's Sangaku construction of the regular pentagon

**Source:** A. C. Sparavigna and M. M. Baldi, *Japanese Temple Geometry: A Digital Sangaku About a Regular Pentagon and the Golden Ratio* (2016), DOI 10.2139/ssrn.2836615. The source's full text was cross-checked against the accessible ResearchGate text record and Politecnico di Torino repository metadata. The repository-hosted PDF was identified but returned HTTP 403 to the retrieval client; visual figure-by-figure inspection remains pending.

**Evidence status:** SOURCE-TRANSCRIBED / EXACT-ALGEBRA. The construction description and equations reproduce the article's textual definition. The coordinate derivation is independently exact. The source's digital pixel rendering is not treated as an exact Euclidean construction.

## 1. Source construction, transcribed

Let the initial circle have centre O and radius 1. Let AB and CD be perpendicular diameters, with
\[
A=(-1,0),\quad B=(1,0),\quad C=(0,1),\quad D=(0,-1).
\]
Let P and Q be the centres of the circles with diameters AO and BO, respectively:
\[
P=(-1/2,0),\quad Q=(1/2,0),\quad r_P=r_Q=1/2.
\]
The line CQ meets the circle centred at Q at T. Draw the circle centred at C with radius CT. Its intersections E,F with the original unit circle are consecutive vertices of a regular pentagon, as claimed in the source.

This is Hirano's construction, reported by Sparavigna–Baldi from the manuscript *Sanpo Jyojutu Kaigi* associated with Chorin Kawakita. The source describes the digital Sangaku as a pixel-based rendering of the geometric construction.

## 2. Exact reconstruction

The auxiliary circle equations are
\[
(x+1/2)^2+y^2=1/4,\qquad (x-1/2)^2+y^2=1/4,
\]
or equivalently
\[
x^2+y^2+x=0,\qquad x^2+y^2-x=0.
\]
The line from C to Q is parametrized by
\[
X(\lambda)=(\lambda/2,1-\lambda).
\]
The point T is the intersection on this ray with the Q-circle other than Q. Since \(CQ=\sqrt5/2\) and \(QT=1/2\),
\[
\lambda_T=1-1/\sqrt5,\qquad
T=\left(\frac12-\frac{1}{2\sqrt5},\frac1{\sqrt5}\right).
\]
Thus
\[
CT=CQ-QT=\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}=:\rho.
\]

The final circle is
\[
x^2+(y-1)^2=\rho^2,
\]
and the original circle is \(x^2+y^2=1\). Subtracting gives
\[
y=1-\frac{\rho^2}{2}=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{4}.
\]
Therefore the two intersections are
\[
E,F=\left(\pm\frac{\sqrt{10-2\sqrt5}}4,\frac{1+\sqrt5}{4}\right).
\]
Their chord has squared length
\[
|EF|^2=4x_E^2=\frac{5-\sqrt5}{2},
\]
the squared side length of a unit-circumradius regular pentagon. Also
\[
E\cdot F=\frac{\sqrt5-1}{4}=\cos(72^\circ),
\]
so the central angle is exactly \(72^\circ\). This verifies that E,F are adjacent pentagon vertices.

## 3. Algebraic fields and symmetry

With the stated coordinate frame, all construction coordinates lie in
\[
K_{\rm coord}=\mathbb Q\left(\sqrt5,\sqrt{10-2\sqrt5}\right),
\]
a real extension of degree four. The squared side length and squared diagonal length lie in \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\). The side length itself is
\[
\ell=\frac12\sqrt{10-2\sqrt5}.
\]
The completed regular pentagon has dihedral symmetry \(D_5\) of order 10. The full construction apparatus (including the selected point C and selected line CQ) is marked and asymmetric; its label-preserving symmetry group must not be conflated with the symmetry group of the completed pentagon. The Galois group is not asserted to be \(D_5\).

## 4. Exactness boundary

The mathematical construction above is exact with straight lines and circles. The article's digital image is generated by a FORTRAN pixel procedure and colour-boundary detection; that raster procedure is a visualization/implementation, not the proof of exact incidence. This case sheet validates the underlying Euclidean construction independently of pixel thresholds.
