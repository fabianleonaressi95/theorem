# Gravitomagnetism, MHD, quantum hydrodynamics and fluxons: a bridge to RGS–PSU–PRISM–QLG

**Research note — 11 November 2026**  
**Status:** structured research programme; standard equations are stated, while the discrete RGS/PRISM coupling and any application to the Ratti apparatus remain open. This note is not a claim that the proposed vacuum-extraction mechanism works.

## 1. Why these subjects belong in one research map

The relevant common structure is not that gravity, electromagnetism and fluid flow are the same interaction. It is that each admits a description in terms of fields, currents, transport, circulation/holonomy, conservation laws and (in suitable limits) flux transport.

The useful sequence is:

1. Maxwell theory: field strength and electric current.
2. Magnetohydrodynamics (MHD): electromagnetic fields coupled to a conducting fluid; magnetic induction, Lorentz force and flux transport.
3. Quantum hydrodynamics (QHD): density and phase variables derived from a wavefunction; quantum stress, circulation and vortices.
4. Superconducting fluxons: phase-winding solitons carrying quantized flux/fluxoid in Josephson systems.
5. Gravitoelectromagnetism (GEM): a weak-field, slow-motion rewriting of selected general-relativistic effects generated by mass-energy currents.
6. Einstein–Cartan (EC) and RGS–QLG: coframe, spin current, connection, torsion and curvature. These are related geometric variables, but are not interchangeable with the GEM magnetic field.

## 2. Electromagnetism and MHD

Let (F=dA) be the electromagnetic field-strength 2-form. The homogeneous Maxwell equation is
[
dF=0,
]
and the inhomogeneous equation is (d\star F=\star J), with convention-dependent unit factors.

In non-relativistic resistive MHD, the induction equation is
[
\partial_t\mathbf B
=\nabla\times(\mathbf u\times\mathbf B)
+\eta_m\nabla^2\mathbf B,
\qquad \nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0,
]
where \(\eta_m\) is magnetic diffusivity. The fluid momentum equation contains the Lorentz force density \(\mathbf J\times\mathbf B\), and the energy equation contains electromagnetic work/Poynting transport.

In the ideal limit, Ohm's law is
[
\mathbf E+\mathbf u\times\mathbf B=0,
]
so
[
\partial_t\mathbf B=\nabla\times(\mathbf u\times\mathbf B).
]
For a material surface \(S(t)\) transported by the fluid,
[
\frac{d}{dt}\int_{S(t)}\mathbf B\cdot d\mathbf S=0.
]
This is Alfvén flux freezing. It is conditional: finite resistivity, reconnection layers, Hall terms, pressure anisotropy and electron inertia can invalidate the ideal closure at the relevant scales. A magnetic field line is not literally a material string.

**RGS–PRISM opportunity:** implement the induction equation as a cochain evolution on the cubical complex, preserving the discrete divergence constraint and a discrete Faraday/Bianchi identity. The finite incidence operators already available in the Q6/PRISM programme can encode the topology; metric/Hodge weights and the constitutive law must be specified separately. The target is a structure-preserving discretisation, not an assertion that a graph by itself is a plasma.

## 3. Quantum hydrodynamics

For a one-component wavefunction
[
\psi=\sqrt n\,e^{iS/\hbar},
\]
the Madelung variables are density \(n\) and phase \(S\). For a particle of mass \(m\) and charge \(q\), define
[
\mathbf v=\frac{\nabla S-q\mathbf A}{m}.
]
The Schrödinger equation gives the continuity equation
[
\partial_t n+\nabla\cdot(n\mathbf v)=0
]
and a momentum equation containing the Lorentz force and the quantum potential
[
Q=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{\nabla^2\sqrt n}{\sqrt n}.
]
For a Pauli/spin fluid, additional magnetization, spin-torque and spin-gradient terms appear; they are not captured by the scalar Madelung model alone.

The phase is defined modulo \(2\pi\). Away from zeros of \(\psi\), a single-valued phase gives locally irrotational flow (after accounting for the gauge potential). At phase singularities, quantized circulation and vortices can occur. Thus the right bridge to QLG is through a gauge-covariant phase/current and a spinor-valued extension, not by identifying an arbitrary velocity field with a quantum current.

**RGS–QLG opportunity:** the existing two-component QLG fibre and the T049C/T049D Dirac embeddings provide representation-theoretic groundwork. The next missing objects are a normalized density, a gauge-covariant phase/current, a continuity equation, and a derived stress/Noether tensor. The existing T049B axial current is a candidate spin-current input, not yet a complete QHD state.

## 4. Fluxons, vortices and quantized flux

In a long Josephson junction, a fluxon is a nonlinear Josephson vortex: a localized \(2\pi\) kink in the phase difference. In conventional junctions the associated flux is the superconducting flux quantum
[
\Phi_0=\frac{h}{2e}.
]
The fluxon behaves as a soliton and can propagate along the junction. In engineered high-kinetic-inductance junctions the magnetic flux carried by a vortex can differ from \(\Phi_0\); the quantized object is then the fluxoid, including the supercurrent contribution. Therefore one must distinguish magnetic flux, fluxoid and phase winding.

A discrete plaquette winding can be represented schematically by
[
n_p=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{e\subset\partial p}
\operatorname{wrap}(\Delta\theta_e-\tfrac{q}{\hbar}A_e),
]
with orientation and branch conventions fixed. This expression is a proposed lattice observable; a valid implementation must handle compact phases, gauge transformations and branch-cut consistency. It is not yet a theorem about Q6 or PRISM.

**RGS–PRISM opportunity:** search for integer plaquette winding sectors, stable localized excitations, pair creation/annihilation and transport under a specified energy functional. Compare them with Josephson sine-Gordon fluxons only after deriving the discrete action and continuum scaling. The term “fluxon” should be reserved for the superconducting/Josephson object or explicitly qualified when used for a new RGS excitation.

## 5. Gravitomagnetism: what the connection really is

In the weak-field, slow-motion limit of general relativity, the metric can be decomposed into gravitoelectric and gravitomagnetic potentials. The gravitomagnetic vector potential is sourced by mass currents (more precisely, by the relevant components of the stress-energy tensor); its curl gives a gravitomagnetic field. This GEM analogy is useful for frame dragging, gyroscope precession and spin-curvature effects.

The analogy with Maxwell theory is not identity:
- coupling constants and signs differ;
- gravity couples universally to stress-energy, not to electric charge;
- the GEM equations depend on gauge and normalization conventions;
- the weak-field approximation is not an independent laboratory field knob;
- for a compact rotating laboratory object the effect is extraordinarily small and must be calculated from its actual mass distribution and angular momentum.

A spin-rotation/gravitomagnetic Stern–Gerlach term can be studied in linearized GR, but this does not establish a large spin-control effect or positron production in the Ratti device.

**Required Ratti audit:** use the actual dimensions, masses, angular velocities and geometry of each rotating component; compute its mass-current multipoles and the corresponding weak-field GEM field; propagate uncertainties; compare with electromagnetic fields and detector sensitivity. Do not infer a gravitomagnetic field from a magnetic field measurement, and do not tune it independently of the mass-current source.

## 6. GEM is not Cartan torsion

In a coframe/connection formulation,
[
T^a=de^a+\omega^a{}_b\wedge e^b,
\qquad
R^a{}_b=d\omega^a{}_b+\omega^a{}_c\wedge\omega^c{}_b.
]
Torsion is the translational field strength of the Cartan connection and couples to spin in Einstein–Cartan theory; curvature is the rotational field strength. In minimal Einstein–Cartan theory torsion is algebraically sourced by spin and is not an independent propagating wave field.

The gravitomagnetic field in GEM is instead a weak-field quantity built from metric perturbations and mass currents. It is not the torsion 2-form, not a Burgers vector, and not a disclination curvature. A model may contain both sectors, but a coupling between them must follow from an explicit action and field equations.

This distinction is consistent with the current repository records:
- T047 derives the \(a^{-6}\) Weyssenhoff spin-squared scaling under its stated assumptions; it does not give a dust-like \(a^{-3}\) torsion term.
- T049B defines the Lorentzian Dirac axial current and an EC contact invariant, while leaving the Noether normalization to the canonical spin tensor and the gauge-covariant inter-site transport open.
- T049C/T049D classify candidate QLG-to-Dirac embeddings; they do not yet derive a physical QHD state.

## 7. A common discrete language for Q6/PRISM

A possible architecture, to be derived and tested, is:

| Continuum object | Discrete candidate | Required check |
|---|---|---|
| Gauge connection \(A\) or \(\omega\) | oriented edge variables / link holonomies | gauge covariance and orientation |
| Field strength \(F\) or curvature \(R\) | plaquette holonomy / 2-cochain | discrete Bianchi identity |
| Electric/mass/spin current | edge or vertex current cochain | continuity / Noether conservation |
| Magnetic flux | oriented plaquette 2-cochain | discrete divergence constraint |
| Fluid density and velocity | vertex density + link transport | positivity and continuity |
| Quantum phase | compact vertex phase | gauge covariance and winding |
| Vorticity/fluxon | integer plaquette winding / localized defect | stability, energy and refinement |
| Coframe and torsion | edge coframe + connection mismatch | correct dimensions and Cartan identity |

The Q6 2-skeleton supplies incidence topology; the PRISM graph supplies a larger transport network. Neither alone supplies physical units, constitutive parameters, a Lorentzian metric, or a continuum limit. Hodge stars, material laws and comparison maps are indispensable.

## 8. Research sequence and falsifiable gates

**Gate A — classical field audit**
1. Reconstruct the magnetic field of the Ratti magnets and coils from geometry/current data.
2. Solve the Lorentz trajectories for already-existing charged particles.
3. Close electrical, mechanical, thermal and radiative energy balances.
4. Estimate GEM fields from mass currents independently.

**Gate B — MHD structure**
1. Define density, velocity, conductivity/resistivity and boundary conditions.
2. Implement the discrete induction equation and continuity equation.
3. Verify discrete divergence preservation, flux balance and energy transfer.
4. Add non-ideal/Hall terms only with a stated scale ordering.

**Gate C — QHD and spin**
1. Derive the Madelung equations from the chosen Schrödinger/Pauli/Dirac model.
2. Fix charge, mass, phase and current normalizations.
3. Derive spin torque/magnetization terms and the Noether current.
4. Compare continuum and discrete continuity/energy identities.

**Gate D — fluxon sector**
1. Specify a compact-phase action (e.g. a derived Josephson/sine-Gordon analogue).
2. Derive integer winding and energy of a localized kink.
3. Test stability, propagation, collisions and refinement.
4. Keep superconducting fluxons distinct from any RGS-defined excitation.

**Gate E — gravitational coupling**
1. Derive GEM from a declared weak-field metric convention.
2. Compute fields from laboratory stress-energy, not from analogy.
3. If EC torsion is also included, derive both sectors from one action and check units, symmetries and conservation laws.
4. Only then compare predicted observables with experiment.

**No-go boundary for the Ratti vacuum-extraction claim:** these bridges can improve the calculation of fields, transport, spin dynamics and diagnostics. They do not by themselves fill the missing QED pair-production mechanism. Positron production still requires an on-shell production calculation with energy-momentum conservation, a predicted rate and a detectable annihilation signature. MHD/QHD/fluxon analogies cannot replace that calculation or the calorimetric balance.
