- Complex: cubical 2-skeleton of the 6-cube, 64 vertices, 192 edges, 240 square faces.
- Models: NL and NLT, using the vector fields of Eqs. (12)-(13) and (16)-(17) in Ghorbanchian et al. (2021), with squares replacing simplicial triangles.
- Seeds: 11, 23, 37, 51, 67.
- Coupling: 21 points, sigma in [0, 2], Delta_sigma = 0.1.
- Integrator: RK4, dt = 0.025.
- Dwell steps per sigma: 10, 40, 160, 640.
- Effective sweep rates Delta_sigma/(dwell * dt): 0.4, 0.1, 0.025, 0.00625.
- Frequencies: node omega ~ Normal(Omega0, 1/tau0), link omega_tilde ~ Normal(Omega1, 1/tau1), with Omega0=Omega1=2 and tau0=tau1=1. The common node drift is removed in the co-rotating frame; the link mean is retained.
- For each seed, the forward and backward branches use identical intrinsic frequencies. The backward branch starts from the terminal state of the corresponding forward branch.
- The table reports the mean absolute branch gap over the 21 sigma values, averaged over seeds, with standard deviation across the five seeds. Loop area is in the (sigma, order parameter) plane.

## Comparison with the 2021 paper

The paper reports, on its simplicial-complex models, that NL has discontinuous transitions in R0 and R1-down while R1-up follows an independent transition; NLT couples all three order parameters into a discontinuous transition. The Q6 data show a qualitative resemblance:

- NL: the R1-up branch gap falls strongly with slower sweeps, while R0 and R1-down retain larger gaps at the slowest tested rate.
- NLT: all three order parameters retain appreciable branch gaps at the slowest tested rate, with larger seed-to-seed variability than NL in several observables.

This is a qualitative comparison only. The source paper studies simplicial complexes, including random/scale-free models and connectomes; this project uses one fixed finite cubical complex. The transition thresholds are not shown to coincide exactly in Q6, and no thermodynamic-limit statement follows.

## Interpretation and limits

1. The NL branch separation decreases substantially as the sweep is slowed. This is evidence of a significant rate-dependent component. The residual gap at the slowest tested rate is a candidate for quasi-static hysteresis, not proof of a rate-independent loop.
2. NLT retains larger branch separation at dwell 640, but the five-seed ensemble and the single Q6 size do not establish convergence to a unique quasi-static loop.
3. The backward protocol uses the forward terminal state. Independent initialization of the backward branch, fixed-frequency/multiple-initial-condition ensembles, finer sigma grids near transitions, and longer dwell-time extrapolation remain necessary.
4. No finite-size scaling is included. Q6 has 64 vertices and cannot establish a thermodynamic limit.
5. Synchronization hysteresis is not constitutive material hysteresis. No industrial actuator, magnetization, strain or B-H dataset has been fitted in this experiment.
