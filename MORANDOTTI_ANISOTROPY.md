# Morandotti two-core anisotropy: operational definition

## Geometric frame

For core centres \(c_0,c_1\), define the preferred axis
\[
e_\parallel=\frac{c_1-c_0}{\lVert c_1-c_0\rVert},\qquad
e_\perp=(-e_{\parallel,y},e_{\parallel,x}),\qquad
Q=(e_\parallel,e_\perp).
\]
The present benchmark has two equal circular holes in a circular outer domain, but the pair selects a longitudinal direction. This is a geometric anisotropy of the two-core configuration, not an anisotropic constitutive material law.

## Interaction-matrix anisotropy

The six cell modes are ordered as \((c_0:0,x,y;c_1:0,x,y)\). For each 2-by-2 translational block \(B_{ij}\) of the Laplacian interaction matrix, resolve
\[
\widetilde B_{ij}=Q^T B_{ij}Q.
\]
The signed longitudinal/transverse contrast is
\[
A_{ij}=\frac{\widetilde B_{ij,\parallel\parallel}
-\widetilde B_{ij,\perp\perp}}
{|\widetilde B_{ij,\parallel\parallel}|
+|\widetilde B_{ij,\perp\perp}|},
\]
when the denominator is nonzero. Both mixed components are retained; they must not be silently discarded.

## Residual anisotropy

For each active core and each boundary component, let \(r_\parallel\) and \(r_\perp\) be the normal-derivative residuals for the translational modes resolved in the preferred frame. The implementation records these as longitudinal/transverse modes in the co-rotating frame, including for rotated configurations. Report
\[
A_r=\frac{r_\parallel^2-r_\perp^2}
{r_\parallel^2+r_\perp^2},
\]
and the ratio \(r_\parallel/r_\perp\) when defined. Scalar mode residuals remain separate.

## Core traction anisotropy

For each integrated core force \(F_k\), report its longitudinal and transverse projections and the signed contrast
\[
A_{F,k}=\frac{F_{k,\parallel}^2-F_{k,\perp}^2}
{F_{k,\parallel}^2+F_{k,\perp}^2}.
\]
The normal convention remains the outward normal of the material domain, pointing into the hole.

## Interpretation and falsifiability

These quantities make the proposed chain testable:
\[
\text{two-core geometry}\to\text{directional operator response}
\to\text{directional residuals and traction resultants}.
\]
They quantify correlation and directional structure; they do **not** prove that anisotropy is the unique cause of residuals. The implemented orientation audit performs two distinct sweeps at fixed mesh size: (A) rotate the core centres and Burgers vectors together; (B) rotate the core centres while keeping Burgers vectors fixed in laboratory coordinates. In each run, the translational cell modes, Frank–Burgers source components, and reported force components are expressed in the co-rotating longitudinal/transverse frame before solving or comparing. It compares the local interaction matrix, directional boundary residuals and core traction components. For an isotropic continuum model, sweep A should be covariant up to floating-point and finite-element geometry effects; angle-dependent remeshing noise is removed. Sweep B isolates the relative orientation of the dislocation source and the two-core axis; its traction response can therefore change even though the cell operator and its boundary residuals do not depend on the defect source. The sweep is a first controlled test, not a full causal proof: mesh refinement at every angle, residual normalization by boundary measure/field scale, and separation-parameter variation remain required.

The default orientation sweep uses 0°, 30° and 60° at a common mesh size, alongside the existing three-grid benchmark. The in-place rotation removes angle-dependent Gmsh remeshing differences, but finite-element discretization and curved-boundary geometry errors remain; a mesh/orientation convergence study is still required. The numerical audit is not a proof of asymptotic convergence, force equilibrium, or a material law. The report retains both laboratory-frame and local-frame defect vectors so the basis transformation is auditable. The Airy stress potential does not define a velocity field, so Kelvin circulation is not inferred from these diagnostics.


## Core-separation sweep

The distance experiment starts from one reference mesh at separation
\(d_0=0.56\), core radius \(a=0.12\), and target mesh size \(h=0.06\).
The implementation now constructs that mesh once and reuses the same mesh
object, geometry-DOF map, and cell connectivity for every target separation.
Before each run, it restores the saved reference coordinates and applies the
coordinate deformation; Gmsh is not called again inside the distance loop.

The two compactly supported Wendland-C2 weights are centred on the reference
cores. Their support radius is
\[
r_{\mathrm{supp}}=\min(r_{\max},d_0-a)
=\min(0.50,0.56-0.12)=0.44.
\]
This radius is fixed by the reference geometry, not by the target separation.
Consequently, the radial weight generated by one core vanishes on the other
core's reference boundary. The weights are normalized to one at their own
core radius, so each core boundary follows its prescribed target centre while
the outer boundary remains fixed.

The sweep uses \(d\in\{0.48,0.56,0.64,0.72\}\), with material parameters
and laboratory-fixed Frank/Burgers data unchanged. It records the free edge
gap \(g=d-2a\), the longitudinal and transverse entries of the off-diagonal
interaction block \(M_{01}\),
\[
A(d)=\frac{M_{\parallel}(d)-M_{\perp}(d)}
{|M_{\parallel}(d)|+|M_{\perp}(d)|},\qquad
C(d)=\frac{\|M_{01}(d)\|_F}{\|M_{00}(d)\|_F},
\]
the mixed longitudinal/transverse entries, and core traction resultants for
each defect case. The report records both the minimum signed corner-triangle
area and the minimum sampled determinant of the cubic coordinate-map
Jacobian. The latter is evaluated on a 12-subdivision barycentric lattice in
each reference triangle; the run aborts if any sampled determinant is
non-positive. This is a numerical geometric-quality check, not a formal
certificate that the Jacobian is positive at every point between samples.

This remains a screening study at one distance-sweep mesh size
(\(h=0.06\)); the three-mesh benchmark is at the reference separation.
Neither \(A(d)\) nor \(C(d)\) is yet a continuum distance law. Before fitting
exponential, algebraic, or limiting models, repeat the sweep at multiple mesh
resolutions and test sampling robustness of the curved-element Jacobian.
No decay law is assumed in advance.


### Reproduction command

Run the reference benchmark and the default distance sweep in the DOLFINx 0.11 environment with:



## Dislocation–disclination sources and deformation metric

For core (i), the finite-dimensional source block is
\[
\Phi_i=(\Omega_i,\;\Omega_i x_i+B_{i,y},\;
\Omega_i y_i-B_{i,x}).
\]
The implementation now preserves the linear split
\[
\Phi=\Phi_{\Omega}+\Phi_B,
\qquad
a_{\Omega}=M^{-1}\Phi_{\Omega},\quad
a_B=M^{-1}\Phi_B.
\]
The associated algebraic quadratic form is decomposed as
\[
\frac12\Phi^TM^{-1}\Phi
=\underbrace{\frac12\Phi_{\Omega}^TM^{-1}\Phi_{\Omega}}_{Q_{\Omega}}
+\underbrace{\frac12\Phi_B^TM^{-1}\Phi_B}_{Q_B}
+\underbrace{\Phi_{\Omega}^TM^{-1}\Phi_B}_{Q_{\Omega B}}.
\]
The mixed term is retained with its sign. These are source-quadratic diagnostics of the discrete interaction system; they are **not labelled physical energies** until the normalization and constitutive interpretation of (M) are established.

The reconstructed Airy stress is also split linearly into Frank and Burgers contributions. For each source case the report stores the integrated stress second moments
\[
S(\sigma)=\int_{\mathcal D}\sigma^T\sigma\,dx,
\qquad
S_{\Omega B}=S(\sigma_{\Omega}+\sigma_B)
-S(\sigma_{\Omega})-S(\sigma_B),
\]
and the longitudinal/transverse eigenvalue contrast of the total moment tensor. The cross tensor records the interaction between the two source sectors; it is not discarded by adding two independent anisotropy scores.

Separately, the mesh deformation metric is computed from paired reference/current curved-element Jacobians at identical reference points:
\[
F=J_{\mathrm{current}}J_{\mathrm{reference}}^{-1},
\qquad G=F^TF,
\qquad
\mathcal A_G=\frac{\lambda_{\max}(G)-\lambda_{\min}(G)}
{\lambda_{\max}(G)+\lambda_{\min}(G)}.
\]
This (G) measures the coordinate-map stretch of the numerical mesh. It is not the stress tensor, not a material constitutive anisotropy, and not a substitute for the Frank/Burgers source data. Its distribution is reported separately from the defect-induced stress anisotropy.

The resulting testable chain is
\[
(\Omega_i,B_i,\mathbf x_i)
\longrightarrow (\Phi_{\Omega},\Phi_B,Q_{\Omega B})
\longrightarrow \sigma_{\Omega},\sigma_B, S_{\Omega B}
\longrightarrow \text{directional stress response},
\]
with the mesh metric (G) retained as a separate geometric/numerical control. This is a finite-dimensional numerical decomposition, not yet a general continuum theorem or a proof of a topological Möbius contribution.


## Frank–Burgers decomposition of interior traction jumps

For each reconstructed Airy stress, the interior-facet traction jump is
computed with the same UFL normal-jump convention:
\[
j=\llbracket\sigma n\rrbracket
=j_{\Omega}+j_B.
\]
The audit now stores the separate Frank and Burgers norms and the signed
quadratic cross term
\[
\int_{\mathcal F_h^{\mathrm{int}}}|j|^2\,dS
=
\int|j_{\Omega}|^2\,dS
+\int|j_B|^2\,dS
+2\int j_{\Omega}\cdot j_B\,dS.
\]
It also stores the directional second-moment tensors
\[
T(j)=\int_{\mathcal F_h^{\mathrm{int}}}j\otimes j\,dS,
\qquad
T_{\Omega B}=T(j)-T(j_{\Omega})-T(j_B),
\]
both in laboratory coordinates and in the longitudinal/transverse core
frame. The total tensor's eigenvalue contrast is reported separately from
the signed cross tensor. These diagnostics directly test whether the mixed
defect sector contributes to the unresolved interior traction jump; a
nonzero cross term alone does not establish causality or convergence.
The decomposition is performed on the reconstructed defect stresses, not
on the source-independent boundary residuals of the six cell basis fields.

## Frank–Burgers tensorial interaction law (T091)

For the reconstructed interior-facet traction jump, split
\[
j=\llbracket\sigma n\rrbracket=j_F+j_B
\]
into Frank/disclination and Burgers/dislocation sectors. Define
\[
T_F=\int j_F\otimes j_F\,dS,\qquad
T_B=\int j_B\otimes j_B\,dS,
\]
\[
T_{FB}=\int(j_F\otimes j_B+j_B\otimes j_F)\,dS.
\]
The exact discrete composition law is
\[
T=T_F+T_B+T_{FB},\qquad
\int|j|^2\,dS=\int|j_F|^2\,dS+\int|j_B|^2\,dS
+2\int j_F\cdot j_B\,dS.
\]
The mixed term is signed and carries directional information. It can be positive, negative or zero; it must be assembled rather than discarded.

For the two-core axis \(e_\parallel\) and its perpendicular \(e_\perp\), define
\[
\mathcal A_\parallel(T)=
\frac{e_\parallel^TTe_\parallel-e_\perp^TTe_\perp}
{\operatorname{tr}T}.
\]
Its numerator and denominator decompose through \(T_F,T_B,T_{FB}\), giving the exact axis-resolved anisotropy composition formula in T091. The eigenvalue anisotropy reported by the benchmark is a separate rotation-invariant nonlinear diagnostic; it is not additive across sectors.

The five-level mixed-source computation records a positive mixed traction contribution of about 17–28% of the total squared jump for this particular source configuration. This is numerical evidence for a non-negligible interaction term in that benchmark, not a universal sign statement or continuum law. Full derivation, table, hypotheses and limitations: [T091](theorems/T091-frank-burgers-tensorial-anisotropy-composition-law.md).
