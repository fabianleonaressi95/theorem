# T108 — Boolean influences, the Q6 Laplacian, and cubical cohomology

**Classification:** (A) exact finite-dimensional Fourier/Walsh identities on \(Q_6\); (B) proved six-variable influence bound; (C) exact cochain identities for gradients; (D) no dimension-uniform proof of the Aaronson–Ambainis (AA) conjecture is claimed.

**Dependencies:** T104 (cycle–cocycle distinction), T105 (64 individual hexagram sites), T107 (Gray-torus graph identification), and the standard Walsh–Fourier analysis of the Boolean cube.

## 1. The AA statement and its scope

For \(p:\{0,1\}^n\to[0,1]\), define
\[
\operatorname{Inf}_i(p)=2^{-n}\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^n}
\big(p(x)-p(x\oplus e_i)\big)^2.
\]
The 2026 AA theorem gives, for a real polynomial of degree at most \(d\), variance at least \(\varepsilon\), and range in \([0,1]\),
\[
\max_i\operatorname{Inf}_i(p)\ge
\frac{8\sqrt{15}}{125\pi^4}\,
\frac{\varepsilon^{5/2}}{d^6}.
\]
T108 does **not** reprove this dimension-uniform theorem. It derives the exact fixed-dimension consequence on the six-cube and identifies its cochain meaning.

## 2. Exact Walsh–Fourier identity on Q6

Let \(V(Q_6)=\{0,1\}^6\), with uniform probability measure, and write the Walsh expansion
\[
f(x)=\sum_{S\subseteq[6]}\widehat f(S)\chi_S(x),
\qquad
\chi_S(x)=(-1)^{\sum_{i\in S}x_i}.
\]
Then
\[
\operatorname{Var}(f)=\sum_{S\ne\varnothing}\widehat f(S)^2.
\]
Flipping coordinate \(i\) changes the sign precisely of those Walsh characters for which \(i\in S\). Parseval therefore gives the exact identity
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Inf}_i(f)=
4\sum_{S\ni i}\widehat f(S)^2.}
\]
Summing over the six coordinates,
\[
\boxed{\sum_{i=1}^6\operatorname{Inf}_i(f)
=4\sum_{S\ne\varnothing}|S|\widehat f(S)^2.}
\]
In particular,
\[
\sum_i\operatorname{Inf}_i(f)\ge4\operatorname{Var}(f),
\qquad
\boxed{\max_i\operatorname{Inf}_i(f)\ge
\frac{2}{3}\operatorname{Var}(f).}
\]
This last bound holds for **every** real function on the 64 vertices; no polynomial-degree or range assumption is needed. It is a fixed-\(n=6\) Poincaré consequence, not a replacement for the AA theorem as \(n\) varies.

If \(f\) is the restriction to the Boolean cube of a real polynomial of total degree at most \(d\), its unique multilinear representation has degree at most \(\min(d,6)\). Equivalently, \(\widehat f(S)=0\) for \(|S|>d\). Thus
\[
\sum_i\operatorname{Inf}_i(f)
=4\sum_{1\le |S|\le d}|S|\widehat f(S)^2
\le4d\,\operatorname{Var}(f).
\]
The lower bound above is the useful influence guarantee; the upper bound records the degree-filtered Dirichlet energy.

## 3. Graph Laplacian formulation

Let \(A\) be the adjacency matrix of \(Q_6\), \(L=6I-A\), and use the normalized inner product
\[
\langle f,g\rangle=2^{-6}\sum_x f(x)g(x).
\]
The Walsh character \(\chi_S\) is an eigenvector of \(L\) with eigenvalue \(2|S|\). Hence
\[
\langle f,Lf\rangle
=2\sum_{S\ne\varnothing}|S|\widehat f(S)^2
=\frac12\sum_i\operatorname{Inf}_i(f).
\]
The spectral gap is exactly \(2\), and
\[
\langle f,Lf\rangle\ge2\operatorname{Var}(f).
\]
This is the same six-dimensional influence estimate written as a graph Dirichlet-form inequality. Under the Gray-pair identification \(Q_6\cong C_4\square C_4\square C_4\), it is invariant under the graph isomorphism; it does not depend on interpreting the third \(C_4\) factor as a physical coordinate.

## 4. The cochain statement: gradients are exact cocycles

Orient each Q6 edge from its endpoint with \(x_i=0\) to the endpoint with \(x_i=1\). For a vertex function \(f\in C^0(Q_6;\mathbb R)\), define the edge cochain
\[
(df)(x,i)=f(x\oplus e_i)-f(x),\qquad x_i=0.
\]
This is the cellular coboundary \(\delta_0f\), so it is exact by definition. On every square face in coordinates \(i,j\), its oriented circulation telescopes:
\[
\delta_1\delta_0f=0.
\]
Thus \(df\in\ker\delta_1\), and its sum around every graph cycle vanishes. The influence is the coordinate-resolved squared norm of this exact edge cochain:
\[
\operatorname{Inf}_i(f)
=2^{-6}\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^6}
(f(x)-f(x\oplus e_i))^2
=2^{-5}\sum_{\substack{x_i=0\\x\in\{0,1\}^6}}(df(x,i))^2.
\]
The factor \(2^{-5}\) appears because each undirected \(i\)-edge is counted twice in the vertex average.

**Crucial distinction.** The graph cycle space of Q6 has dimension \(192-64+1=129\), but after filling all square faces the full cubical six-cube is contractible and \(H^1=0\). The 129 graph cycles are generated by square boundaries; they are not 129 independent cohomology classes. T104's graph cuts, graph cycles, cubical cocycles, and cubical cohomology must not be conflated. T108 uses only the exact gradient \(df\), so no nontrivial cohomology class is needed to define influence.

## 5. What Q6 establishes — and what it cannot establish

**Proved exactly for Q6**
1. The Walsh influence identity and the six-coordinate sum rule.
2. The Poincaré bound \(\max_i\operatorname{Inf}_i(f)\ge(2/3)\operatorname{Var}(f)\) for every real function on 64 vertices.
3. The Laplacian/Dirichlet-form identity and spectral gap \(2\).
4. Every gradient edge cochain is closed on square faces and has zero circulation on every graph cycle.

**Not established**
1. A bound uniform in \(n\) with polynomial dependence on degree \(d\).
2. A new proof, simplification, or improvement of the 2026 AA theorem.
3. A physical interpretation of Walsh influence as electric charge, material defect density, or measured circuit response.
4. Any identification of Q6 cohomology with quantum query complexity.

The finite Q6 estimate is deliberately elementary. The genuinely difficult content of AA is dimension-uniformity: \(n\) is arbitrary while the degree is controlled. Any claimed RGS–PRISM advance must reproduce that quantifier structure and all constants/hypotheses, not extrapolate from \(n=6\).
