# Q6, Green–Kubo and Krystallo: an exact scale bridge and the supergolden obstruction

**Date:** 2026-11-23  
**Status:** exact finite-graph identities + exact OU/Green–Kubo reduction; physical identification of the stress observable remains open.

## 1. Scope and conclusion

There is a rigorous bridge between the Krystallo radial scale, the hypercube family, and a Green–Kubo coefficient:

- Krystallo at quarter-turns gives the same exponential scale as the number of vertices of the hypercube: (K(n\pi/2)=2^n=|V(Q_n)|).
- The hypercube Laplacian has exact levels (2k) with multiplicities \(\binom nk\).
- For a reversible Ornstein–Uhlenbeck dynamics generated by that Laplacian, Green–Kubo is an explicit inverse-spectral sum weighted by \(1/(2k)\).

This yields a genuine mathematical scale bridge. It does **not** yet identify a physical stress tensor, a continuum transport coefficient, or the supergolden constant. In fact, the standard reversible Green–Kubo generator cannot have the supergolden companion matrix as its relaxation matrix, because that matrix has non-real eigenvalues whereas a reversible relaxation generator is self-adjoint and nonnegative.

## 2. Krystallo scale equals hypercube volume

Take the radial law
\[
K(\theta)=2^{2\theta/\pi}.
\]
It obeys
\[
K(\theta+\pi/4)=\sqrt2 K(\theta),\qquad
K(\theta+\pi/2)=2K(\theta).
\]
At the quarter-turn samples \(\theta_n=n\pi/2\),
\[
K_n=K(\theta_n)=2^n=|V(Q_n)|.
\]
Thus the two sampled sequences agree exactly. This is only an identity between sampled sequences: it does not identify the spiral with the hypercube graph, its metric, incidence structure, or a dynamical trajectory on \\(Q_n\\). A structural bridge would require an independently defined map or refinement rule preserving specified graph/cell data.

## 3. Exact hypercube spectrum

For
\[
Q_n=K_2\,\square\cdots\square\,K_2,
\]
the graph Laplacian is the Kronecker sum
\[
L_n=\sum_{j=1}^{n}I^{\otimes(j-1)}\otimes
\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}
\otimes I^{\otimes(n-j)}.
\]
The Walsh characters diagonalize this operator. The eigenvalues and multiplicities are
\[
\lambda_k=2k,\qquad m_k=\binom nk,\qquad k=0,\ldots,n.
\]
In particular, \(Q_6\) has 64 vertices, 192 edges, degree 6, diameter 6, and Laplacian spectrum
\[
0^{(1)},\;2^{(6)},\;4^{(15)},\;6^{(20)},\;8^{(15)},\;10^{(6)},\;12^{(1)}.
\]
There are 63 nonconstant modes. These are exact graph-theoretic facts, not numerical fits.

## 4. Green–Kubo on the hypercube: exact OU realization

Let \(X_t\in\mathbb R^{2^n}\) be the reversible OU process
\[
dX_t=-\gamma L_nX_t\,dt+
\sqrt{2\gamma\sigma^2L_n}\,dW_t,
\qquad \gamma>0.
\]
The constant mode is stationary and is removed by restricting to the mean-zero subspace. On that subspace the invariant covariance is \(\Sigma=\sigma^2 I\). For a mean-zero observable
\[
J(t)=b^TX_t,
\]
the equilibrium correlation is
\[
C_J(t)=\langle J(t)J(0)\rangle
=\sigma^2 b^Te^{-\gamma L_nt}b.
\]
Let \(P_k\) denote the orthogonal projector onto the Laplacian eigenspace with eigenvalue \(2k\). Then
\[
C_J(t)=\sigma^2\sum_{k=1}^{n}
e^{-2\gamma kt}\,\|P_kb\|^2.
\]
The Green–Kubo integral is therefore
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal G_n(b)=\int_0^\infty C_J(t)\,dt
=\frac{\sigma^2}{2\gamma}
\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\|P_kb\|^2}{k}.
}
\]
If \(J\) is a shear-stress observable, the corresponding viscosity is
\[
\eta_n=\frac{1}{k_BT V_{\rm phys}}\,\mathcal G_n(b),
\]
with the usual convention that \(J\) is the total integrated shear stress. The physical volume and the microscopic identification of \(b\) must be supplied by the model; they are not determined by the graph spectrum.

### 4.1 Isotropic benchmark

For \(b\) uniformly distributed on the unit sphere of the 63-dimensional mean-zero subspace of \(Q_6\),
\[
\mathbb E\|P_kb\|^2=\frac{\binom6k}{63}.
\]
Since
\\[
\\sum_{k=1}^{6}\\frac{\\binom6k}{k}
=\\frac{1517}{60},
\\]
the averaged dimensionless spectral sum, before the Green--Kubo prefactor, is
\\[
\\mathbb E\\left[\\sum_{k=1}^{6}\\frac{\\|P_kb\\|^2}{k}\\right]
=\\frac1{63}\\sum_{k=1}^{6}\\frac{\\binom6k}{k}
=\\frac{1517}{3780}.
\\]
The Green--Kubo integral includes the additional factor \\(\\sigma^2/(2\\gamma)\\), hence
\\[
\\boxed{
\\mathbb E\\mathcal G_6(b)
=\\frac{\\sigma^2}{2\\gamma}\\,\\frac{1517}{3780}
=\\frac{1517\\sigma^2}{7560\\gamma}.
\\]
Here \\(b\\) is sampled uniformly from the unit sphere in the 63-dimensional mean-zero vertex space; the projectors are orthogonal in that space and the constant eigenspace is excluded. Thus \\(1517/3780\\) is the mean spectral sum, while \\(1517/7560\\) is the coefficient after the factor (1/2) in Green--Kubo.

Consequently
\[
\mathbb E\eta_6=
\frac{1517\sigma^2}
{7560\,\gamma\,k_BT V_{\rm phys}}.
\]
This is an exact benchmark for the specified OU model, not a measured or predicted material viscosity.

### 4.2 Large-dimension scaling

For an isotropic mean-zero observable in \(Q_n\), the spectral weights are binomial:
\[
\mathbb E\|P_kb\|^2=\frac{\binom nk}{2^n-1},\quad k\ge1.
\]
Hence
\[
\mathbb E\mathcal G_n
=\frac{\sigma^2}{2\gamma(2^n-1)}
\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\binom nk}{k}.
\]
Equivalently, this is \(\sigma^2/(2\gamma)\) times the mean of \(1/K\), conditioned on \(K>0\), for \(K\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,1/2)\). Concentration around \(K=n/2\) gives
\[
\mathbb E\mathcal G_n
\sim\frac{\sigma^2}{\gamma n}
\qquad(n\to\infty).
\]
At the Krystallo quarter-turn samples one may label \\(n=2\\theta/\\pi\\), so this particular model's isotropic mean scales as \\(1/\\theta\\) at large sampled dimension. This compares two independently defined formulae; it is not a transport law derived from the Krystallo spiral.

The asymptotic statement concerns this specified reversible OU model and isotropic observable ensemble.

## 5. The Q6 plaquette-curvature identity is a separate exact sector

For the cubical 2-face variables \(\Omega_p\) and the normalization used in the Q6 curvature record,
\[
\Delta\mu_4
=8w^4\sum_{p=1}^{240}(\cos\Omega_p-1),
\qquad
\mathcal F_{Q6}
=\sum_{p=1}^{240}(1-\cos\Omega_p)
=-\frac{\Delta\mu_4}{8w^4}.
\]
At \(w=2\), \(\Delta\mu_4=-128\mathcal F_{Q6}\). This is an exact finite-complex identity under the stated normalization.

The cochain projector
\\[
P_{\\rm cl}=I-B_3(B_3^TB_3)^+B_3^T
\\]
has rank 129 in the recorded Q6 construction and projects onto the closed 2-cochain sector, when (B_3:C_3\\to C_2) is the cubical boundary matrix and standard Euclidean cell inner products are used. This projector is not the graph-vertex Laplacian \\(L_{Q6}\\). Nor does the cosine curvature action automatically equal a Green--Kubo stress autocorrelation. The companion face-cochain note defines a reversible OU model on this closed space; it is a specified model choice, not a physical identification.

## 6. What the supergolden sector can and cannot be

The supergolden number \(s>0\) is defined by
\[
s^3-s^2-1=0,
\]
and the recorded matrix
\[
M_s=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&1\\1&0&0\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix}
\]
has characteristic polynomial \(x^3-x^2-1\).

But the reversible OU relaxation operator \(\gamma L_n\) is self-adjoint and positive semidefinite. Every restriction to an invariant subspace is therefore self-adjoint with real nonnegative spectrum. The polynomial \(x^3-x^2-1\) has discriminant \(-31\), hence one real root and a non-real conjugate pair. Therefore:

**No reversible Green–Kubo relaxation operator obtained by self-adjoint compression of the hypercube Laplacian can equal \(M_s\), or be similar to it.**

This is not a disproof of every possible supergolden connection. It says the supergolden matrix, if it is relevant, must act in a different role: for example, as a directed transfer/renormalization map, a non-reversible three-channel update, or a separate geometric state-transition operator. Such an operator would need to be derived from a concrete three-component rule. It cannot be inferred from the scalar Krystallo law or from the reversible Green–Kubo integral.

## 7. Precise next theorem target

The meaningful next target is not “derive \(s\) from Q6” in the abstract. It is:

1. Define a three-component state on Q6 (or on the 240 plaquette variables) with an explicit geometric meaning.
2. Derive its one-step transfer map from the cubical incidence/orientation data and the 45-degree Krystallo refinement rule.
3. Prove whether that map is reversible/self-adjoint or genuinely directed.
4. Couple the resulting state to a specified stress observable and derive the Green–Kubo correlation.
5. Compute the characteristic polynomial exactly. Only if it is \(x^3-x^2-1\), with no fitted coefficients, is the supergolden identification established.

Until steps 1–5 are supplied, the bridge is:
- **proved:** Krystallo/hypercube scale identity; hypercube spectrum; OU Green–Kubo inverse-spectral formula; Q6 isotropic benchmark.
- **proved under the recorded normalization:** Q6 plaquette-curvature identity and closed-cochain projector rank.
- **open:** identification of the physical stress observable, coupling of the 240-face sector to the vertex Laplacian, and a geometric derivation of a three-channel supergolden map.
- **excluded for the reversible route:** direct identification of \(M_s\) with a self-adjoint Green–Kubo relaxation generator.
