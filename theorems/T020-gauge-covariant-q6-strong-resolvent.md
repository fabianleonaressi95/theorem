# T020 — Gauge-covariant strong-resolvent convergence of the Q6 Wilson refinement

**Status: CONDITIONAL THEOREM / proof reduced to an explicit consistency hypothesis.** The finite-lattice gauge identities are exact for unitary links. The continuum-limit statement below follows if the sampled links satisfy the stated smooth parallel-transport consistency. The accompanying numerical audit is a reproducibility tool; it is not evidence until run and its output is recorded.

## 1. Continuum data

Let \(\mathbb T^6_2=(\mathbb R/2\mathbb Z)^6\), \(h_n=1/n\), and \(\Lambda_n=(\mathbb Z/2n\mathbb Z)^6\). Let \(\mathcal A_i\in C^\infty(\mathbb T^6_2;\mathfrak{su}(3))\), with anti-Hermitian convention, and set
\[
\mathcal H=L^2(\mathbb T^6_2;\mathbb C^8\otimes\mathbb C^3),\qquad
A_{\mathcal A}=m_0I+\sum_{i=1}^6(\Gamma_i\otimes I_3)(\partial_i+\mathcal A_i).
\]
The domain is \(H^1(\mathbb T^6_2;\mathbb C^{24})\). The \(\Gamma_i\) are Hermitian Clifford matrices. Since \(\mathcal A_i\) is anti-Hermitian, each covariant derivative is skew-adjoint on the periodic torus, and the continuum operator is accretive after subtracting \(m_0\).

## 2. Link fields and lattice operator

For each oriented positive edge \((x_j,x_j+he_i)\), let \(U_{n,i}(j)\in SU(3)\) be the parallel transporter with the convention
\[
U_{n,i}(j)=\mathcal P\exp\!\left(\int_0^h
\mathcal A_i(x_j+te_i)\,dt\right).
\]
Define the unitary covariant shift
\[
(S_{n,i}\psi)(j)=(I_8\otimes U_{n,i}(j))\psi(j+e_i).
\]
For fixed \(r>0\), define
\[
A_n^{\mathcal A}=m_0I+
\frac1{2h}\sum_i(\Gamma_i\otimes I_3)(S_{n,i}-S_{n,i}^*)
+\frac r{2h}\sum_i(2I-S_{n,i}-S_{n,i}^*).
\]
The centered term is anti-Hermitian. Moreover,
\[
\langle\psi,(2I-S_{n,i}-S_{n,i}^*)\psi\rangle_n
=\|(I-S_{n,i})\psi\|_n^2\geq0.
\]
Thus for every \(z\) with \(\Re z<m_0\),
\[
\|(A_n^{\mathcal A}-z)^{-1}\|\leq(m_0-\Re z)^{-1}.
\]
This estimate is exact and independent of the link field and lattice spacing.

## 3. Gauge covariance

For any sitewise gauge transformation \(g_n(j)\in SU(3)\), let
\[
U^g_{n,i}(j)=g_n(j)U_{n,i}(j)g_n(j+e_i)^{-1},
\qquad (G_n\psi)(j)=(I_8\otimes g_n(j))\psi(j).
\]
Then \(S^g_{n,i}=G_nS_{n,i}G_n^{-1}\), and consequently
\[
A_n^{\mathcal A^g}=G_nA_n^{\mathcal A}G_n^{-1}.
\]
This is an exact finite-dimensional identity, not an asymptotic statement.

## 4. Consistency hypothesis

Let \(P_n=J_n^*\) be cell averaging and \(J_n\) the isometric piecewise-constant embedding used in T019, now with fibre \(\mathbb C^{24}\). Assume the link sequence is generated by the smooth connection above and that for every smooth periodic section \(\phi\),
\[
\left\|J_nA_n^{\mathcal A}P_n\phi-
A_{\mathcal A}\phi\right\|_{L^2}\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
\|J_nP_n\phi-\phi\|_{L^2}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{C}
\]
For smooth parallel transports and a fixed smooth connection, (C) is the expected first-order finite-difference consistency statement; its detailed cell-average estimate must be checked for the chosen sampling convention. It is stated explicitly here rather than hidden inside the resolvent argument.

## 5. Conditional theorem

**Theorem.** Under (C), for every \(z\) with \(\Re z<m_0\) and every \(f\in\mathcal H\),
\[
J_n(A_n^{\mathcal A}-z)^{-1}J_n^*f
\longrightarrow
(A_{\mathcal A}-z)^{-1}f
\quad\text{strongly in }\mathcal H.
\]

**Proof.** For \(\phi\in C^\infty\), (C) gives
\[
J_n(A_n^{\mathcal A}-z)P_n\phi
\longrightarrow(A_{\mathcal A}-z)\phi.
\]
The operator \(A_{\mathcal A}-z\) maps its domain onto \(\mathcal H\), and the image of the smooth core is dense. The lattice resolvents are uniformly bounded by \((m_0-\Re z)^{-1}\). Applying the standard dense-range resolvent approximation argument to this core proves the displayed strong convergence. \(\square\)

This is generalized strong-resolvent convergence between varying Hilbert spaces. It is not norm-resolvent convergence.

## 7. Scope

**Exact:** finite-lattice gauge covariance; Wilson positivity; uniform left-half-plane resolvent bound.

**Conditional:** generalized strong-resolvent convergence, conditional on (C).

**Not established:** norm-resolvent convergence, gauge-field integration, reflection positivity of a full interacting theory, fermion determinant control, interacting continuum QCD, confinement, baryon masses, or a derivation of a physical \(3+1\)-dimensional theory.
