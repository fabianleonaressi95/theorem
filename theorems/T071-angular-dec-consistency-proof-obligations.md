# T071 — Angular DEC consistency: local expansion and proof obligations

**Status: local expansion derived; global consistency estimate is a conditional target, not yet proved; l=1 refinement regression added, execution pending. Not a Mosco theorem.**

## Setting

Let \(\mathcal T_h\) be a geodesic triangulation of the unit sphere. Vertices are \(x_j\), primal edges are \(e=(j,k)\), primal lengths are \(\ell_e\), and circumcentric dual edges have signed lengths \(\ell_e^*>0\). Assume each dual edge crosses its primal edge orthogonally and the circumcentric dual cells form a partition of the sphere.

For nodal samples \(R_hu=(u(x_j))_j\), define

\[
\mathcal E_h(R_hu)=
\sum_{e=(j,k)}\frac{\ell_e^*}{\ell_e}
\bigl(u(x_j)-u(x_k)\bigr)^2.
\]

The target continuum energy is

\[
\mathcal E(u)=\int_{S^2}|\nabla_{S^2}u|^2\,d\Omega.
\]

## Local midpoint expansion — established

For an edge \(e=(j,k)\), let \(m_e\) be its geodesic midpoint and \(t_e\) its unit tangent. Taylor expansion along the unit-speed great circle through the edge gives, for \(u\in C^3(S^2)\),

\[
u(x_k)-u(x_j)
=\ell_e\,\partial_{t_e}u(m_e)+O(\ell_e^3).
\]

The quadratic term cancels because the endpoints are symmetric about the midpoint. Therefore

\[
\frac{\ell_e^*}{\ell_e}
\bigl(u(x_j)-u(x_k)\bigr)^2
=
\ell_e\ell_e^*
\bigl|\partial_{t_e}u(m_e)\bigr|^2
+O(\ell_e^3\ell_e^*).
\]

This is an edgewise expansion only. By itself it does not prove that the sum of the leading terms is a quadrature for the full Dirichlet integral.

## Uniform mesh hypotheses needed

A global estimate would require a family with:
1. geodesic, shape-regular triangles, uniform lower angle bound and bounded aspect ratios;
2. quasi-uniform scale \(h=\max_e\ell_e\to0\);
3. spherical Delaunay/circumcentric admissibility, positive signed dual lengths and a non-overlapping dual partition;
4. uniform primal-to-dual length ratio bounds;
5. bounded vertex valence;
6. a verified geometric quadrature identity or estimate relating the assembled edge-diamond directional samples to \(\int_{S^2}|\nabla u|^2d\Omega\).

The sixth item is not a cosmetic technicality: orthogonality and positive weights alone do not prove the global energy identity or an error bound on a curved mesh.

## Conditional consistency target — not yet a theorem

The desired result is an estimate of the form

\[
\left|\mathcal E_h(R_hu)-\mathcal E(u)\right|
\le C h\,\|u\|_{C^3(S^2)}^2,
\]

with \(C\) independent of \(h\), under the uniform hypotheses above plus a proved edge-diamond quadrature estimate. The current repository does not yet contain that quadrature proof. Accordingly this inequality is recorded as a proof target, not as an established proposition.

A valid proof must construct the local dual diamonds on the sphere, show that they form a partition up to controlled curvature errors, and compare the edgewise directional samples with the full tensor \(\nabla u\otimes\nabla u\). It must also bound the sum of local remainders uniformly in \(h\).

## Degree-one harmonic calibration

For \(u(x)=x_3\) on the unit sphere,

\[
-\Delta_{S^2}u=2u,\qquad
\int_{S^2}u^2\,d\Omega=\frac{4\pi}{3},\qquad
\mathcal E(u)=\frac{8\pi}{3}.
\]

The continuum Rayleigh quotient is exactly 2. The existing spherical_l1_rayleigh diagnostic computes the discrete quotient with the same oriented dual weights as the graph assembly. A new regression test checks that its absolute error from 2 decreases across icosphere levels 0–3.

Previously reproduced diagnostic values (before the oriented-crossing hardening, which leaves the accepted dual lengths unchanged when the crossing condition holds) were:

| 2 | 1.9799420404 | 0.0200579596 |

These values are a finite-level numerical diagnostic, not a proof of the uniform mesh hypotheses or the global estimate. The new regression test has not yet been executed after the latest code changes.

## Relation to the Ellis radial product

For separated modes \(v_{ij}=p(u_i)Y(x_j)\), the product graph energy factors into a radial term times the spherical mass and a spherical DEC term times the radial quadrature. Angular consistency is therefore a necessary component of smooth-core consistency for the warped Ellis product.

It does not establish:
- a uniform discrete Rellich estimate;
- the Mosco weak-liminf inequality M1;
- the recovery-sequence property M2;
- spectral convergence;
- a refinement generated by the original Q6/RGS seed.
