**Classification:** (A) exact finite graph/chain geometry, reusing the verified Q6–PRISM kinematic map; (B) exact reversible Gibbs model on the complex unit sphere; (C) exact first-order edge-coherence susceptibility at zero coupling; (D) conditional Markovian fluctuation–dissipation identity; (E) open physical interpretation of translational/rotational defects and their cross-response.

## 1. Geometry: the graph-preserving map is Gray, not ordinary binary coding

Write the six bits as three ordered pairs:
\[
h=(b_0b_1\mid b_2b_3\mid b_4b_5).
\]
Define the Gray coordinate map
\[
g(00)=0,\quad g(01)=1,\quad g(11)=2,\quad g(10)=3,
\]
and
\[
\Gamma(h)=\big(g(b_0b_1),g(b_2b_3),g(b_4b_5)\big)
=(x,y,z)\in\mathbb Z_4^3.
\]
Since the four vertices of \(Q_2\) form a four-cycle, this map is a graph isomorphism
\[
\boxed{Q_6\cong C_4\square C_4\square C_4.}
\]
It preserves all 192 one-bit-flip edges. The ordinary pair code \(2b_{2a}+b_{2a+1}\) remains a bijection of labels but is not the graph-preserving coordinate map: it sends the two opposite vertices of a bit-pair square to opposite points of the numeric four-cycle.

The existing exact kinematic construction groups \((x,y)\) as a periodic \(4\times4\) material-cell coordinate and \(z\) as a four-state internal fibre:
\[
\pi:\mathbb Z_4^3\to\mathbb Z_4^2,\qquad \pi(x,y,z)=(x,y).
\]
The physical candidate base is the flat discrete torus
\[
\mathbb T^2_{4a}=(\mathbb R/4a\mathbb Z)^2,
\qquad X_{h}=a(x,y)\pmod{4a},
\]
where \(a>0\) is a declared length scale. The \(z\)-coordinate is an internal fibre, not a third physical coordinate in this two-dimensional realization.

The existing audit constructs the 64-to-16 vertex projection, 192-to-32 edge map, and 192-to-16 product-face map. It checks exactly
\[
D_0^{Q_6}P_0=P_1D_0^{\rm base},\qquad
D_1^{\rm prod}P_1=P_2D_1^{\rm base},
\]
and the boundary-of-boundary identities. The quotient has 16 vertices, 32 edges, 16 faces, and fibre cardinality four. These are exact finite combinatorial identities. The scale \(a\), material interpretation, and continuum limit are not fixed by the graph.

**Edge accounting.** The 192 Q6 edges split into 96 edges that flip one of the lower three bits and 96 that flip one of the upper three bits, as in T105. Under the three Gray-pair coordinates, each pair contributes 64 edges. These are different partitions of the same edge set; do not call the 96/96 split a count of “shared edges” between two graphs.

## 2. A specified equilibrium ensemble on the 64 complex amplitudes

Let
\[
z=(z_h)_{h\in V(Q_6)}\in S^{127}\subset\mathbb C^{64},
\qquad \sum_h|z_h|^2=1.
\]
For each undirected Q6 edge \(e=\{h,k\}\), define the real coherence
\[
c_e(z)=\operatorname{Re}(z_h\overline{z_k}).
\]
Choose an interaction energy
\[
H_J(z)=-J\sum_{e\in E(Q_6)}c_e(z),
\]
where \(J\) has units of energy. At inverse temperature \(\beta=(k_BT)^{-1}\), define
\[
d\mu_{\beta,J}(z)=Z_{\beta,J}^{-1}
e^{-\beta H_J(z)}\,d\sigma_{127}(z),
\]
where \(d\sigma_{127}\) is normalized Haar surface measure on the sphere.

This explicitly separates two cases:
- \(J=0\): \(\mu_{\beta,0}=\sigma_{127}\), so the Born probabilities are exactly \(\operatorname{Dirichlet}(1^{64})\), and all off-diagonal mean coherences vanish.
- \(J\ne0\): the edge interaction biases the Haar ensemble. The Born probability vector is generally no longer uniform on the simplex; its distribution must be computed or measured under the stated Gibbs density.

Thus the nontrivial edge correlations are generated by the declared interaction, not by Q6 adjacency alone.

### 2.1 Exact linear susceptibility of an edge coherence

For any fixed edge \(e\), Haar fourth moments give
\[
\mathbb E_{\sigma}[c_e]=0,\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_{\sigma}(c_e)=\frac{1}{2d(d+1)},\quad d=64.
\]
Distinct undirected edge coherences have zero Haar covariance. Differentiating the Gibbs expectation at \(J=0\),
\[
\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial J}
\langle c_e\rangle_{\beta,J}\right|_{J=0}
=\beta\,\operatorname{Cov}_{\sigma}
\left(c_e,\sum_{e'}c_{e'}\right)
=\frac{\beta}{2d(d+1)}
=\boxed{\frac{\beta}{8320}}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\langle c_e\rangle_{\beta,J}
=\frac{\beta J}{8320}+O((\beta J)^2)
\]
for small dimensionless coupling \(\beta J\). This is an exact first derivative at \(J=0\), not an assertion that the linear approximation remains accurate at arbitrary coupling.

## 3. Reversible dynamics that realizes the Gibbs measure

A concrete continuous-time model is overdamped diffusion on the sphere with generator
\[
\mathcal L_J f
=D\left(\Delta_{S^{127}}f
-\beta\,\nabla_{S^{127}}H_J\cdot\nabla_{S^{127}}f\right),
\qquad D>0.
\]
Here \(\nabla_{S^{127}}\) and \(\Delta_{S^{127}}\) are the intrinsic gradient and Laplace–Beltrami operator. With the stated sign convention, the process is reversible with stationary density \(d\mu_{\beta,J}\) relative to surface measure. This is a specified stochastic model; it is not closed-system Schrödinger evolution, nor is it claimed to describe a real quantum processor without a physical noise derivation.

For generalized observables \(X_b(z)\), apply a weak time-dependent perturbation
\[
H_{J,f}(z,t)=H_J(z)-\sum_b f_b(t)X_b(z).
\]
For a reversible overdamped equilibrium process, the response kernel is
\[
R_{ab}(t)=-\beta\,\Theta(t)\frac{d}{dt}
\left\langle\delta A_a(t)\,\delta X_b(0)\right\rangle_{\rm eq},
\]
provided the perturbation enters through the stated conjugate potential and the process is in its stationary regime. The integrated step response recovers the static susceptibility \(\beta\operatorname{Cov}_{\rm eq}(A_a,X_b)\), subject to decay of the connected correlation at long times.

A numerical FDT check must compare this correlation-derived response with an independently measured weak-field response of the same generator, observables, temperature, and normalization. A Green–Kubo transport coefficient additionally requires a physically defined current and its units; the finite graph alone does not supply them.

## 4. Discrete translational and rotational defect observables

The base torus gives a declared combinatorial spatial support. It does not by itself make the complex amplitudes a crystal displacement or a local orientation. The two defect sectors must be introduced as separate measured/model fields.

### 4.1 Translational closure and Burgers data

Let \(\beta_e\in\mathbb R^2\) be an oriented edge distortion cochain on the 16-vertex base torus, with \(\beta_{\bar e}=-\beta_e\). For an oriented plaquette \(p\), define the Burgers closure
\[
b_p=\sum_{e\in\partial p}\beta_e.
\]
If \(\beta_e\) is the exact difference of a single-valued displacement \(u_v\), then \(b_p=0\) on every face. A nonzero value requires a non-exact distortion, a branch cut/core, or independently measured plastic distortion. It is not obtained merely by assigning arbitrary vertex phases.

### 4.2 Rotational holonomy and Frank data

Let \(\omega_e\in\mathbb R\) be an oriented local-frame connection on the same base edges, with \(\omega_{\bar e}=-\omega_e\). Its plaquette holonomy is
\[
\Omega_p=\operatorname{wrap}_{(-\pi,\pi]}
\left(\sum_{e\in\partial p}\omega_e\right).
\]
For an \(n\)-fold orientational order parameter \(\Psi_n=|\Psi_n|e^{in\alpha}\), the corresponding Frank angle is defined modulo the chosen lattice symmetry by the winding of \(n\alpha\). The frame connection must be calibrated; it is not interchangeable with the translational cochain \(\beta_e\).

The pair \((b_p,\Omega_p)\) is therefore a pair of independent observables: translational closure failure and rotational holonomy. T104's \(\mathbb F_2\) plaquette flux remains a third, separate discrete observable.

### 4.3 First test: are the two measured sectors linearly distinguishable?

Before fitting cross-dynamics, assemble paired, centered sample matrices \(B\) and \(F\), whose columns are the measured Burgers components and Frank-angle/frame components after calibration and declared normalization. Compute their numerical ranks by SVD and the largest canonical correlation
\[
\rho_{\max}=\|Q_B^\top Q_F\|_2,
\]
where \(Q_B,Q_F\) are orthonormal bases for the respective column spaces. If \(\rho_{\max}=1\) within the numerical tolerance, the two sample sectors have a common linear direction; if the rank is deficient, some declared components are redundant. Report the singular values, ranks, and \(\rho_{\max}\), not only a binary label.

The numerical tolerance must be fixed relative to measurement/calibration precision before analysis. Use a block bootstrap to obtain an uncertainty interval for \(\rho_{\max}\). If the interval is compatible with one at the declared resolution, do **not** claim that the sectors are linearly distinguishable. If it is separated from one, this only rules out exact/near-exact linear overlap at that resolution: it does not prove statistical independence. The held-out cross-prediction test in §5 addresses predictive dependence beyond linear collinearity.


## 5. Preregistered test of sector independence and cross-response

Before examining held-out data, fix:
- the 16-site embedding and length scale \(a\);
- the definitions, units, and calibration uncertainty of \(\beta_e\), \(\omega_e\), \(b_p\), and \(\Omega_p\);
- the equilibrium window, burn-in, sampling interval, perturbation amplitudes, and response lags;
- the primary null hypothesis of no cross-predictive information between the translational and rotational sectors after conditioning on declared controls.

Use separate training and held-out time blocks. Estimate the two auto-correlation blocks and the cross-correlation blocks. Test incremental held-out prediction in both directions:
\[
b(t)\leftarrow b\text{-history, controls}
\quad\hbox{versus}\quad
b(t)\leftarrow b\text{-history},\Omega\text{-history, controls},
\]
and analogously for \(\Omega\). Preserve temporal dependence in null resampling by using block permutations or a stationary block bootstrap, not independent point shuffling. Correct the family of primary tests with Holm's procedure at family-wise \(\alpha=0.01\). Report effect sizes and 95% block-bootstrap confidence intervals. Call a cross-sector effect only if the adjusted test rejects and the held-out predictive gain has a confidence interval strictly above zero. Otherwise report no detected cross-sector effect at the stated power; do not claim proof of independence.

For FDT, preregister the lag window and compare the correlation-derived kernel with the independently measured impulse/step response. Agreement means the two estimates' uncertainty intervals are statistically compatible at the declared confidence level; do not choose a tolerance after seeing the data.

## 6. Exact, model-dependent, and open conclusions

**Exact finite statements**
- The Gray-pair map is a graph isomorphism \(Q_6\cong C_4^3\).
- The existing Q6-to-\(C_4^2\) quotient and its vertex/face chain-map checks are exact finite identities.
- At \(J=0\), Haar/Born probabilities have the uniform \(\operatorname{Dirichlet}(1^{64})\) law and mean off-diagonal coherences vanish.
- The first derivative of an edge coherence with respect to \(J\) at zero coupling is \(\beta/8320\).

**Specified model statements**
- The Gibbs density and reversible diffusion generator define a legitimate equilibrium model and a corresponding overdamped FDT.
- The first-order edge response is a prediction of this model, not a measurement of a physical circuit.

**Open physical questions**
- whether the Gray-torus quotient is the correct calibrated spatial embedding of the actual 64-site circuit;
- whether measured circuit amplitudes are described by the sphere-valued Gibbs model;
- whether independent translational distortion and frame-connection data can be reconstructed;
- whether Burgers/Frank observables show a reproducible cross-response and satisfy FDT under measured dynamics;
- whether a continuum/material interpretation survives refinement and changes of embedding.

No material dislocation, disclination, transport coefficient, or RGS–Q6 physical equivalence is declared proved here.
