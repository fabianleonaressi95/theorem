# T149 — Exact symmetric period-three and period-six Fibonacci–Fricke orbits

**Date:** 25 November 2026  
**Classification:** exact symbolic theorem; finite-dimensional monodromy.  
**Status:** exact algebraic classification of the reversor-symmetric locus \(x=z\) for \(T^6(p)=p\); Floquet statements are separated into regular even-period and singular odd-period cases. The level-\(c=4\) spectra are exact algebraic evaluations.

## Abstract

For the Fibonacci trace map
\[
T(x,y,z)=(2xy-z,x,y),\qquad
I(x,y,z)=x^2+y^2+z^2-2xyz-1,
\]
with reversor \(R(x,y,z)=(z,y,x)\), we determine the nontrivial symmetric period-three orbit and all three algebraic branches of the symmetric locus \(T^6(p)=p\): one period-six branch A, one period-two branch B, and one period-six branch C. We compute their Fricke levels and exact monodromy polynomials. On \(c=4\), the period-six families give one orbit of type A and two of type C, with exact tangential traces \(402\) and \(536\pm98\sqrt{89}\).

T149 supplements T147 and T148, which analyze the distinct singular endpoints \(p_1=(1,1,1)\) and \(p_0=(0,0,0)\) of the period-two branch.

## 1. Reversibility

Direct substitution gives \(I\circ T=I\). Its Jacobian is
\[
DT(x,y,z)=\begin{pmatrix}2y&2x&-1\\1&0&0\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \boxed{\det DT=-1}.
\]
Consequently \(\det DT^q=(-1)^q\). The map
\[
R(x,y,z)=(z,y,x)
\]
satisfies
\[
R^2=1,\qquad RTR=T^{-1}.
\]
Every \(p=(a,b,a)\) is fixed by \(R\). If such a point has exact period six, its orbit carries the natural \(D_6\)-action generated by \(T\) and \(R\).

## 2. The nontrivial symmetric period-three orbit

The nontrivial symmetric period-three orbit is
\[
(-1,1,-1)\longmapsto(1,-1,-1)
\longmapsto(-1,-1,1)\longmapsto(-1,1,-1).
\]
Its Fricke value is \(I=0\). At \(p=(-1,1,-1)\),
\[
DT^3(p)=
\begin{pmatrix}
15&-10&-6\\
-6&3&2\\
2&-2&-1
\end{pmatrix},
\]
and
\[
\boxed{\chi_{DT^3(p)}(\lambda)
=(\lambda+1)(\lambda^2-18\lambda+1).}
\]
The full ambient monodromy spectrum is
\[
\boxed{\{-1,\;9+4\sqrt5,\;9-4\sqrt5\}.}
\]
The orbit lies at singular points of \(\Sigma_0\), where \(\nabla I=0\). This is therefore an ambient monodromy result, not a tangential Floquet statement on a regular Fricke surface.

The real solution set of \(T^3(x,y,z)=(x,y,z)\) consists of the two fixed points
\[
p_0=(0,0,0),\qquad p_1=(1,1,1),
\]
and the three points of the exact period-three orbit displayed above. One convenient elimination form of the fixed-point equations is
\[
\begin{cases}
8x^3y^2-8x^2yz-2xy^2+2xz^2-x+yz=0,\\
2x^2y-xz-y=0,\\
xy-z=0.
\end{cases}
\]
Substituting \(z=xy\) reduces the real solutions to \(p_0,p_1\) and the three-cycle. In particular, there is no exact period-three orbit on \(c=4\); the nontrivial three-cycle is on the singular level \(c=0\).


## 3. Exact factorization of the symmetric \(T^6\)-fixed locus

For \(p=(a,b,a)\in\operatorname{Fix}(R)\), the condition that \(T^3(p)\) is again fixed by \(R\) is
\[
\boxed{4a(2ab-a-b)(2ab-a+b)=0.}
\]
It yields precisely three algebraic branches:

- **A:** \(a=0\), namely \(p_A(b)=(0,b,0)\), of exact period six for \(b\ne0\).
- **B:** \(2ab-a-b=0\), namely \(p_2(a)=(a,a/(2a-1),a)\), the previously studied period-two branch (where defined).
- **C:** \(2ab-a+b=0\), namely \(p_C(a)=(a,a/(2a+1),a)\), of exact period six for \(a\ne0,-1\) and \(2a+1\ne0\).

The implication from \(T^3(p)\\in\\operatorname{Fix}(R)\) to \(T^6(p)=p\) follows from reversibility: \(RT^3=T^{-3}R\). Conversely, every point of the symmetric locus fixed by \(T^6\) is represented by one of these branches. The two factors B and C must not be merged: B has period two, whereas C has exact period six away from its excluded lower-period parameter values.

## 4. Two symmetric period-six families

For \(p=(a,b,a)\), two nontrivial period-six families are as follows.

### Type A
\[
\boxed{p_A(b)=(0,b,0),\qquad b\ne0.}
\]
Its orbit is
\[
\begin{aligned}
(0,b,0)&\to(0,0,b)\to(-b,0,0)\\
&\to(0,-b,0)\to(0,0,-b)\to(b,0,0)\to(0,b,0).
\end{aligned}
\]
Its Fricke level is
\[
\boxed{c_A=b^2-1.}
\]

### Type C
\[
\boxed{p_C(a)=\left(a,\frac{a}{2a+1},a\right),}
\]
where \(2a+1\ne0\), \(a\ne0,-1\) for exact period six. Its six points are
\[
\begin{aligned}
p_0&=(a,a/(2a+1),a),\\
p_1&=(-a/(2a+1),a,a/(2a+1)),\\
p_2&=(-a,-a/(2a+1),a),\\
p_3&=(-a/(2a+1),-a,-a/(2a+1)),\\
p_4&=(a,-a/(2a+1),-a),\\
p_5&=(a/(2a+1),a,-a/(2a+1)),
\end{aligned}
\qquad T(p_j)=p_{j+1\pmod6}.
\]
Its Fricke level is
\[
\boxed{
c_C(a)=\frac{(a+1)^2(4a^2-2a-1)}{(2a+1)^2}.
}
\]

The branch B is the period-two branch already treated in T147–T148. At \(a=-1\), branch C reaches the period-three point \((-1,1,-1)\), so its period drops from six to three. At \(a=0\), branch C reaches \(p_0\), where its period drops to one. These exceptional points are excluded from the exact-period-six family.

## 5. Floquet determinant and tangential monodromy

For a regular Fricke level, invariance of \(I\) gives a unit normal multiplier for any periodic monodromy. Hence the product of the two tangential multipliers is \((-1)^q\) for a period-\(q\) orbit. In particular, for even period six the tangential determinant is \(+1\), so the tangential characteristic polynomial is reciprocal. For odd period three it is \(-1\), but the explicit period-three orbit above lies on the singular level \(c=0\), so no regular tangential plane is available there.

## 6. Tangential monodromy on regular period-six levels

At an exact period-six point \(p\) on a regular Fricke level, put \(M_6(p)=DT^6(p)\). Differentiating \(I\circ T^6=I\) fixes the normal covector. Since \(\det DT=-1\), but the period is six and \(\det DT^6=+1\), the ambient characteristic polynomial factors as
\[
\chi_{M_6(p)}(\lambda)
=(\lambda-1)(\lambda^2-\tau\lambda+1),
\qquad
\tau=\operatorname{tr}(M_6(p))-1.
\]
The reciprocal quadratic is the characteristic polynomial of the two-dimensional tangential monodromy.

### Type A at \(c=4\)

The level equation gives \(b=\pm\sqrt5\); these parameter values belong to the same orbit because \(T^3p_A(b)=p_A(-b)\). Exact multiplication gives
\[
\boxed{\chi_{M_6}(\lambda)
=(\lambda-1)(\lambda^2-402\lambda+1).}
\]
The tangential trace is \(402\).

### Type C at \(c=4\)

The level equation is
\[
4a^4+6a^3-17a^2-20a-5=0,
\]
with factorization over \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{89})\):
\[
4\left(a^2+\frac{3-\sqrt{89}}4a+\frac{3-\sqrt{89}}8\right)
 \left(a^2+\frac{3+\sqrt{89}}4a+\frac{3+\sqrt{89}}8\right)=0.
\]
The four real roots form two period-six cycles, paired by \(T^3\). The two exact tangential traces are
\[
\boxed{\tau_C^\pm=536\pm98\sqrt{89},}
\]
and the corresponding tangential characteristic polynomials are
\[
\boxed{\lambda^2-\bigl(536\pm98\sqrt{89}\bigr)\lambda+1.}
\]
The roots of the quadratic with coefficient \(3+\sqrt{89}\) give the plus trace; those of the quadratic with coefficient \(3-\sqrt{89}\) give the minus trace.

## 7. Branch collisions and the two distinct endpoint mechanisms

The two period-six branches have different limiting behavior. On A,
\[
c_A(b)+1=b^2,
\]
so the branch reaches \(p_0=(0,0,0)\) at \(b=0\). On C,
\[
c_C(a)+1=3a^2-6a^3+16a^4+O(a^5)
\]
as \(a\to0\), again reaching \(p_0\). At this point
\[
DT(p_0)^6=I_3,
\]
while \(DT^2(p_0)\) has ambient spectrum \(\{1,e^{2\pi i/3},e^{-2\pi i/3}\}\). Since \(p_0\) is singular on its Fricke level, this is an ambient linear representation statement, not a tangent-plane spectrum on a regular surface.

At the other end of C, writing \(a=-1+\epsilon\),
\[
c_C(a)=5\epsilon^2+O(\epsilon^3),
\]
and \(p_C(-1)=(-1,1,-1)\), the symmetric period-three orbit. The period-six branch therefore closes on the period-three orbit at \(c=0\), where the period-three ambient monodromy has multiplier \(-1\). This is a candidate period-doubling collision, not yet a standard flip-bifurcation theorem: the collision lies on a singular Fricke level and nondegeneracy conditions have not been established.

These are distinct mechanisms: an order-six linear resonance at \(p_0\), and a period-three/period-six collision at the singular three-cycle.

## 8. Periodic-orbit count at \(c=4\)

Within the reversor-symmetric locus \(x=z\), the level \(c=4\) contains one exact period-six Type-A orbit, two exact period-six Type-C orbits, and two period-two orbits on the separate period-two branch. The nontrivial symmetric period-three orbit lies on \(c=0\), not \(c=4\). This is not a classification of all possibly nonsymmetric periodic orbits on the full Fricke surface.

## 9. Exact relation between period-two and period-six traces at \(c=4\)

On the period-two branch B at \(c=4\), the tangential period-two traces are \(\tau_2^\pm=2\pm\sqrt{89}\). If \(A\) is the period-two tangential monodromy, then the period-six iterate is \(A^3\), and Cayley–Hamilton gives
\[
\operatorname{tr}(A^3)=(\operatorname{tr}A)^3-3\operatorname{tr}A.
\]
Therefore
\[
(2\pm\sqrt{89})^3-3(2\pm\sqrt{89})=536\pm98\sqrt{89}.
\]
These are exactly the two tangential traces obtained on the two C period-six cycles at \(c=4\). This is an exact algebraic trace identity. It does not by itself identify the period-six C cycles with the period-two orbit; they are distinct periodic orbits, and the equality is at the level of the trace of the cubed two-dimensional monodromy.

## 10. Theorem statement and proof

**Theorem.** The symmetric period-three orbit and the three branches A, B, C of the symmetric \(T^6\)-fixed locus have the stated exact parameterizations and Fricke levels. The exact-period-six portions of A and C, their branch collisions, and their level-\(c=4\) monodromies have the properties stated above. At \(c=4\), the Type-A tangential trace is \(402\), while the two Type-C tangential traces are \(536\pm98\sqrt{89}\).

**Proof.** The orbit formulae follow by direct substitution into \(T\), and the Fricke values by substitution into \(I\). The monodromy matrices are products of
\[
DT(x,y,z)=
\begin{pmatrix}2y&2x&-1\\1&0&0\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
On regular period-six levels, invariance of \(I\) fixes the normal covector; because \(\det DT^6=+1\), the tangential quadratic is reciprocal. Substitution of \(b^2=5\), and reduction modulo the Type-C quartic, gives the exact traces displayed above. The period-three matrix is obtained by multiplying the three exact Jacobians at its orbit points. \(\square\)

## 11. Scope

- The factorization classifies the symmetric locus \(x=z\), not all period-six orbits on the full Fricke surface; nonsymmetric cycles may occur in reversor-related pairs.
- The period-three orbit is singular on \(\Sigma_0\); its spectrum is ambient, not a regular-surface tangential spectrum.
- The traces \(402\) and \(536\pm98\sqrt{89}\) are exact dynamical monodromy traces. They are not identified here with physical resonance frequencies or Pellis spectral data.
- The relation to the Q6 hexagram involution is treated separately in T150.


## 8. Determinant sign, exact symmetric-locus factorization, and endpoint collisions

The one-step Jacobian is
\[
DT(x,y,z)=
\begin{pmatrix}2y&2x&-1\\1&0&0\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \boxed{\det DT=-1}.
\]
Consequently \(\det DT^q=(-1)^q\). In particular, the period-six monodromy has determinant \(+1\), while the period-three ambient monodromy has determinant \(-1\). The reciprocal tangential quadratic at a regular period-six point is therefore justified by \(\det DT^6=+1\), not by a false one-step unimodularity assertion. The period-three orbit lies on a singular Fricke level and its previously stated characteristic polynomial remains an ambient monodromy polynomial.

For \(p=(a,b,a)\), direct substitution in \(T^3(p)\in\operatorname{Fix}(R)\) gives the exact factorization
\[
\boxed{4a(2ab-a-b)(2ab-a+b)=0.}
\]
The components are:
\[
p_A(b)=(0,b,0),\qquad
p_2(a)=\left(a,\frac{a}{2a-1},a\right),\qquad
p_C(a)=\left(a,\frac{a}{2a+1},a\right).
\]
The middle component is the period-two family already treated in T147–T148; it is not a new exact period-six branch. For \(b\ne0\), type A has exact period six. Type C has exact period six when \(a\ne0,-1\) and \(2a+1\ne0\).

The Fricke levels at the two singular endpoints obey
\[
c_A(b)+1=b^2,
\qquad
c_C(a)+1=3a^2-6a^3+16a^4+O(a^5)
\quad(a\to0).
\]
Thus both period-six branches meet \(p_0=(0,0,0)\) at \(c=-1\), where
\[
DT(p_0)^3=-I_3,\qquad DT(p_0)^6=I_3.
\]
This is an order-six ambient linear resonance, not by itself a nondegenerate bifurcation theorem.

At the other endpoint,
\[
p_C(-1)=(-1,1,-1),
\qquad
c_C(a)=5(a+1)^2+O((a+1)^3).
\]
The type-C period-six family therefore meets the nontrivial period-three orbit at \(c=0\). The period-three monodromy has an ambient multiplier \(-1\), but a standard flip-bifurcation conclusion still requires a regular local return map and the relevant nondegeneracy conditions; those have not been established here.
