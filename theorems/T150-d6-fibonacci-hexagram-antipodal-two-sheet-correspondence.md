# T150 — D6-equivariant two-sheet structure of the Fibonacci hexagonal orbit and its Q6 realization

**Date:** 25 November 2026  
**Classification:** exact finite combinatorics and group action.  
**Status:** proved as a finite \(D_6\)-set isomorphism on the specified six-state Q6 suborbit. The geometric two-triangle interpretation and the local \(C_3\) representation at \(p_0\) are stated with their precise scope. Extension to all 64 hexagrams and identification with Pellis operators remain open.

## Abstract

A reversor-symmetric period-six orbit of the Fibonacci trace map carries a dihedral \(D_6\)-action. Its half-turn \(T^3\) is an involution pairing opposite orbit points, while \(T^2\) cycles each of the two alternating three-point subsets. We construct an explicit six-element \(D_6\)-orbit inside the 64 Q6 hexagrams, each written as an ordered pair of three-bit trigrams. On this orbit, Q6 antipodal complement is exactly the half-turn of the hexagon. This gives an exact orbit-level PSU/Anti-Twin realization: three antipodal pairs arranged on the two alternating vertex triangles. It does not identify all 64 Q6 states with one Fibonacci orbit.

## 1. Two distinct six-position structures: trigrams and interlaced triangles

A geometric regular hexagram (Star of David) has six vertices and symmetry group \(D_6\), of order 12: six rotations by multiples of \(60^\\circ\), and six reflections. Three reflection axes pass through opposite vertices; the other three pass through midpoints of opposite edges. Its vertices split into the two alternating triples, each the vertex set of an equilateral triangle; these are the two interlaced triangles.

For an I Ching hexagram, the six line bits are conventionally grouped into two consecutive three-bit blocks, the lower and upper trigrams. This consecutive trigram split is not literally the alternating-vertex split of the Star of David. The common structure is the six-position set with its dihedral action; the two partitions must not be conflated.

## 2. Q6 as an ordered pair of trigrams

Let
\[
\mathbb H_3=(\mathbb Z_2)^3,\qquad
Q_6=(\mathbb Z_2)^6=\mathbb H_3\times\mathbb H_3.
\]
A hexagram is an ordered pair
\[
x=(u\mid v),\qquad
u=(x_0,x_1,x_2),\quad v=(x_3,x_4,x_5).
\]
The two trigrams are the two ordered three-bit blocks of one six-bit state.

Define the antipodal complement
\[
J(u\mid v)=(u+\mathbf1\mid v+\mathbf1),
\qquad \mathbf1=(1,1,1)\in(\mathbb Z_2)^3.
\]
Then
\[
J^2=1,\qquad Jx\ne x\quad(x\in Q_6).
\]
Consequently the 64 hexagrams split into exactly 32 antipodal pairs \(\{x,Jx\}\), and \(Q_6/\langle J\rangle\) has 32 elements. On the real permutation module \(\mathbb R[Q_6]\), the induced involution has \(+1\) and \(-1\) eigenspaces both of dimension 32.

Thus PSU/Anti-Twin is an antipodal pairing internal to the 64 ordered-trigram states; it is not a disjoint union of two independent copies of Q6.

## 3. The D6 action on a symmetric Fibonacci six-orbit

Let
\[
T(x,y,z)=(2xy-z,x,y),\qquad R(x,y,z)=(z,y,x).
\]
The identities
\[
R^2=1,\qquad RTR=T^{-1}
\]
make \(R\) a reversor. Let \(p=(a,b,a)\) have exact period six. Its orbit
\[
\mathcal O(p)=\{p,Tp,T^2p,T^3p,T^4p,T^5p\}
\]
carries the transitive dihedral action generated by
\[
r(T^kp)=T^{k+1}p,\qquad
s(T^kp)=RT^kp=T^{-k}p.
\]
These satisfy
\[
r^6=s^2=1,\qquad srs=r^{-1}.
\]
The stabilizer of \(p\) is exactly \(\langle s\rangle\), so
\[
\mathcal O(p)\simeq D_6/\langle s\rangle.
\]

The half-turn
\[
S=T^3
\]
is an involution on the orbit. It pairs
\[
p\leftrightarrow T^3p,\qquad
T^2p\leftrightarrow T^5p,\qquad
T^4p\leftrightarrow Tp.
\]
The two alternating triples
\[
\mathcal T_0=\{p,T^2p,T^4p\},\qquad
\mathcal T_1=\{Tp,T^3p,T^5p\}
\]
are each cycled by \(T^2\), and exchanged by \(T\). The half-turn \(S\) exchanges the triples and pairs their elements. On \(\mathbb R[\mathcal O(p)]\), \(S\) has eigenspaces of dimensions \(3+3\), with orthogonal projectors
\[
P_\pm=\frac12(I\pm S).
\]
These projectors give symmetric/antisymmetric combinations across opposite pairs; they are not the indicator projections onto \(\mathcal T_0\) and \(\mathcal T_1\).

## 4. An explicit six-state D6 orbit in Q6

Arrange the six line positions of each hexagram at the vertices of a regular hexagon. Let \(r\) act by cyclic shift \(i\mapsto i+1\pmod6\), and let \(s\) act by reflection \(i\mapsto2-i\pmod6\). These generate the full 12-element symmetry group. The chosen \(s\) is a vertex-axis reflection; its conjugates give the other two vertex axes, while the remaining three reflections are edge-axis reflections. The two alternating position triples are the two interlaced equilateral triangles. This geometric partition is distinct from the consecutive trigram block split.

Take
\[
q_0=(000\mid111).
\]
It is fixed by \(s\). Its six rotations are
\[
\begin{aligned}
q_0&=000\mid111,\\
rq_0&=001\mid110,\\
r^2q_0&=011\mid100,\\
r^3q_0&=111\mid000,\\
r^4q_0&=110\mid001,\\
r^5q_0&=100\mid011.
\end{aligned}
\]
They are distinct. Hence this Q6 orbit has stabilizer exactly \(\langle s\rangle\) and is also \(D_6/\langle s\rangle\).

On these six hexagrams the antipodal complement is the half-turn:
\[
\boxed{Jq_0=r^3q_0,\qquad Jr^kq_0=r^{k+3}q_0.}
\]
Each displayed state has its second trigram equal to the bitwise complement of its first. The selected orbit therefore consists of three antipodal pairs. It is a six-state suborbit of Q6, not the whole 64-state space.

## 5. Exact equivariant identification

For any exact period-six point \(p=(a,b,a)\) in the reversor-fixed plane, define
\[
\Phi_p:\mathcal O(p)\longrightarrow Q_6,\qquad
\Phi_p(T^kp)=r^kq_0,\quad k=0,\ldots,5.
\]
Both orbits have stabilizer \(\langle s\rangle\), so this is a well-defined bijection onto the six-state Q6 orbit of \(q_0\). It is \(D_6\)-equivariant:
\[
\Phi_p(Tx)=r\Phi_p(x),\qquad
\Phi_p(Rx)=s\Phi_p(x).
\]
In particular,
\[
\boxed{\Phi_p(T^3x)=J\Phi_p(x).}
\]
Thus the Fibonacci half-turn involution agrees exactly with the restriction of Q6 antipodal PSU/Anti-Twin complement under this orbit-level identification.

The alternating Fibonacci triples map to the even- and odd-rotation triples of the six selected hexagrams. Under the regular-hexagon realization, these are exactly the two alternating vertex sets (the two interlaced triangles). The antipodal involution pairs opposite vertices, one from each triangle; the quotient has three antipodal classes. This statement concerns the six positions/vertices in the geometric realization, not the consecutive lower/upper trigram blocks.

## 6. The order-three linearization at the singular endpoint \(p_0\)

At \(p_0=(0,0,0)\),
\[
DT(p_0)=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&-1\\1&0&0\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad DT(p_0)^3=-I_3,
\qquad DT(p_0)^6=I_3.
\]
Thus \(DT^2(p_0)\) has order three and spectrum
\[
\{1,\omega,\omega^2\},\qquad \omega=e^{2\pi i/3}.
\]
The real three-dimensional permutation representation of a triangle rotation also has this spectrum and minimal polynomial \(X^3-1\). Since the three eigenvalues are distinct, both real matrices are cyclic and are similar over \(\mathbb R\). Hence the ambient linearization \(DT^2(p_0)\) is isomorphic, as a real \(C_3\)-representation, to the permutation representation on the three vertices of one triangle.

**Singularity caveat.** The Fricke level through \(p_0\) is singular. This is an isomorphism of ambient three-dimensional linear representations, not an identification with a two-dimensional tangent plane to a regular Fricke surface. It also does not by itself construct an operator intertwiner with Pellis.

## 7. Theorem, proof, and evidence boundary

**Theorem.** Every exact period-six orbit through a point of \(\operatorname{Fix}(R)\) is \(D_6\)-equivariantly isomorphic to the Q6 orbit of \(q_0=000\mid111\) under the specified dihedral action on the six line positions. Under this isomorphism, \(T^3\) corresponds to Q6 antipodal complement \(J\). The alternating position classes are the two interlaced triangles, exchanged by the half-turn; the orbit-level quotient consists of three opposite pairs, while the global Q6 involution partitions all 64 hexagrams into 32 pairs. The consecutive lower/upper trigram partition remains a distinct structure.

**Proof.** The reversor relation gives the Fibonacci orbit the \(D_6\)-set structure \(D_6/\langle s\rangle\). The hexagram \(q_0\) is fixed by \(s:i\mapsto2-i\), and its six rotations are distinct, so its orbit has the same stabilizer. The map \(T^kp\mapsto r^kq_0\) is therefore well-defined and equivariant. Since \(r^3q_0=Jq_0\), and complement commutes with all bit-position permutations, \(T^3\) corresponds to \(J\) on the entire six-state orbit. Finally, \(J\) is fixed-point-free on the 64 binary strings, giving 32 global antipodal pairs. \(\square\)

**Proved exactly**
- The geometric regular hexagram has the 12-element symmetry group \(D_6\), with three vertex-axis and three edge-axis reflections.
- Its six vertices split into two alternating equilateral triangles; this is distinct from the consecutive lower/upper trigram split of an I Ching hexagram.
- The ambient \(DT^2(p_0)\) representation is real-similar to the permutation representation of \(C_3\) on a triangle.
- Q6 is the ordered-trigram-pair space \((\mathbb Z_2)^3\times(\mathbb Z_2)^3\).
- \(J\) is a fixed-point-free involution with 32 antipodal pairs.
- A reversor-symmetric exact period-six Fibonacci orbit is a transitive \(D_6\)-set.
- The six-state Q6 orbit of \(000\mid111\) is the same \(D_6\)-set.
- The explicit map \(\Phi_p\) intertwines the dynamics/reversor with the hexagram dihedral action and sends \(T^3\) to \(J\).

**Still open**
- A canonical decomposition of all 64 Q6 states into Fibonacci-generated six-orbits, or an identification of all 32 antipodal pairs with one six-cycle.
- An intertwiner between the full Q6 graph Laplacian, the 188-dimensional RGS operator, and the physical Pellis transfer operator.
- A continuum two-sheet geometry or wormhole interpretation.
- A physical identification of the T147 golden multipliers or T148 order-three endpoint with Pellis resonance frequencies.

The two three-vertex triangles are the alternating position classes; the projectors \(P_\\pm=(I\\pm T^3)/2\) instead select symmetric/antisymmetric combinations across opposite vertices. Thus the two rank-three eigenspaces are not the coordinate spans of the two triangles.

The result is stronger than the counting observation \(2\times3=6\): it is an explicit \(D_6\)-equivariant bijection on a six-state Q6 suborbit, with the antipodal involution exactly matched to \(T^3\).


## 8. Canonical two-sheet/three-mode factorization of the six-dimensional orbit module

The six-dimensional permutation module has a canonical factorization once the antipodal pairs are used as mode labels. Let \(e_k\) denote the basis vector at \(T^kp\), and let \(f_{\epsilon,j}\) be the basis of \(\mathbb C^2_{\rm sheet}\otimes\mathbb C^3_{\rm mode}\). Define
\[
\Phi(e_{2j})=f_{0,j},\qquad
\Phi(e_{2j+3\ {\rm mod}\ 6})=f_{1,j},
\quad j=0,1,2.
\]
This is a permutation-unitary map. Let \(C_3e_j=e_{j+1\ {\rm mod}\ 3}\) and \(F_3e_j=e_{-j\ {\rm mod}\ 3}\). In sheet-major order,
\[
\Phi r\Phi^{-1}=
\begin{pmatrix}0&C_3^2\\ C_3^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\Phi s\Phi^{-1}=
\begin{pmatrix}F_3&0\\0&F_3\end{pmatrix}.
\]
In particular,
\[
\boxed{
\Phi r^2\Phi^{-1}=I_2\otimes C_3,\quad
\Phi r^3\Phi^{-1}=\sigma_x\otimes I_3,\quad
\Phi s\Phi^{-1}=I_2\otimes F_3.
}
\]
Thus the six-orbit module is exactly two sheets, each carrying a three-mode cyclic representation. The half-turn is the sheet exchange, and the two parity projections have rank three.

This factorization is a representation-space intertwiner. It does not assert that the full Q6 graph Laplacian preserves the six-state subspace, nor does it identify the channel space with a physical Pellis transfer operator. A raw \(2\times2\) Cauchy transfer has the wrong dimension; an operator-level identification requires a physically derived three-mode multiplicity and compatible boundary domains. The corresponding transfer lift and its multiplicity test are recorded in the companion Pellis C dossier.


## 9. Realization of the three-mode factor as a spherical dipole representation

The three-point permutation representation also has a canonical real orthogonal realization inside the natural three-dimensional \(\ell=1\) representation of \(O(3)\), restricted to the planar-triangle symmetry subgroup \(D_3\). This \(D_3\) subgroup is generated by a proper \(120^\circ\) rotation and an improper reflection; it is a subgroup of \(O(3)\), not of \(SO(3)\). Define
\[
W_3=
\begin{pmatrix}
1/\sqrt3&1/\sqrt3&1/\sqrt3\\
2/\sqrt6&-1/\sqrt6&-1/\sqrt6\\
0&1/\sqrt2&-1/\sqrt2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For the cyclic shift \(C_3e_j=e_{j+1}\) and reflection \(F_3e_j=e_{-j}\),
\[
W_3C_3W_3^T=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0\\
0&-\frac12&-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\\
0&\frac{\sqrt3}{2}&-\frac12
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
W_3F_3W_3^T=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-1).
\]
The first coordinate is the invariant axis; the remaining two form the real \(m=\pm1\) angular pair. The displayed rotation has determinant \(+1\), while \(\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-1)\) has determinant \(-1\); together they generate the indicated copy of \(D_3\) in \(O(3)\). The \(C_3\) rotation subgroup lies in \(SO(3)\), but the full dihedral action does not. Therefore \((I_2\otimes W_3)\Phi\) identifies the Fibonacci six-orbit representation with two copies of the spherical dipole representation.

This is an exact representation-theoretic realization and a candidate geometric interpretation for three standing-wave modes per sheet. It does not establish that the Golden Spheres Resonance operator selects the \(\ell=1\) sector, nor that its boundary conditions preserve this subspace. Those are separate physical/operator questions.
