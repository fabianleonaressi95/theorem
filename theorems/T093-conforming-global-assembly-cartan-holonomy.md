# T093 — Conforming global assembly and Cartan-holonomy consistency

**Date:** 11 November 2026  
**Status:** the polygonal local-to-global assembly and red-refinement statements are proved under the hypotheses below; the smooth-connection holonomy consistency estimate is stated with its regularity and mesh assumptions; singular defect-core convergence and the coupling to the full Morandotti solver remain open.  
**Dependencies:** T092 (local two-circle construction and discrete Frank–Burgers holonomy); T091 (finite-dimensional Frank–Burgers traction-jump composition).

## 1. Scope

T092 proves that arbitrarily small two-circle equilateral cells exist at every interior point of an open planar domain. T093 addresses the next question: when finitely many such cells have been selected, can they be incorporated into one conforming global triangulation and refined without losing conformity or cell geometry? Can a smooth Cartan connection sampled on that mesh recover its curvature and torsion from face holonomies?

The answer is yes under explicit hypotheses. This is not a claim that every cell of the global mesh is equilateral, nor that local triangulability alone gives a mesh with uniform quality on arbitrary domains.

## 2. Seed-cell insertion

Let \(\Omega\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a bounded polygonal domain (possibly with finitely many polygonal holes). Choose a finite family of closed equilateral triangles
\[
S_1,\ldots,S_m\Subset\Omega
\]
such that their closures are pairwise disjoint and have positive distance from one another and from \(\partial\Omega\). Each \(S_i\) may be generated by T092 from two equal circles.

The complement
\[
\Omega_*=\overline{\Omega\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^m S_i^\circ}
\]
is a polygonal domain whose boundary consists of portions of \(\partial\Omega\) and the three edges of each seed triangle. A finite polygonal domain admits a finite conforming triangulation respecting its boundary segments. Triangulate \(\Omega_*\) with every seed edge retained as a boundary edge, and adjoin the seed triangles \(S_i\) as elements. This gives a finite conforming triangulation \(\mathcal T_0\) of \(\Omega\) in which every \(S_i\) is an element.

For any finite set of distinct interior points \(x_1,\ldots,x_m\), T092 permits the radii to be chosen small enough that the corresponding seed triangles are pairwise disjoint and remain in \(\Omega\). Thus any prescribed finite collection of interior locations can be represented by two-circle seed elements.

## 3. Conforming refinement theorem

**Theorem 93.1 (seed-preserving conforming refinement).**  
Let \(\Omega\) and \(\mathcal T_0\) be as in Section 2. Define \(\mathcal T_{n+1}\) from \(\mathcal T_n\) by joining the three edge midpoints of every triangle, replacing each triangle by its four midpoint subtriangles. Then:

1. every \(\mathcal T_n\) is a finite conforming triangulation of \(\Omega\);
2. every descendant of a seed triangle is equilateral;
3. if \(h_n=\max_{K\in\mathcal T_n}\operatorname{diam}K\), then \(h_n=2^{-n}h_0\);
4. the family is shape-regular, with the same maximum shape-regularity constant as \(\mathcal T_0\);
5. the family is quasi-uniform if and only if \(\mathcal T_0\) is quasi-uniform.

**Proof.** Each edge is bisected once on each side because the same midpoint is used by both incident elements; hence no hanging nodes are introduced and conformity is preserved. The four midpoint subtriangles are similar to the parent triangle, each with scale factor \(1/2\); the central one is also similar (with reversed orientation). Therefore all angles and the shape-regularity ratio are preserved. Diameters are halved at every level, giving \(h_n=2^{-n}h_0\). An equilateral parent produces four equilateral children. Since the initial mesh is finite, its minimum element diameter is positive; uniform scaling preserves the ratio of largest to smallest diameters, proving the quasi-uniformity statement. \(\square\)

**Consequence.** The two-circle construction can be inserted as an exact local subcomplex in a global conforming mesh. Global completion uses ordinary polygonal triangulation; it does not force every complementary element to be equilateral. The theorem therefore separates the universal local seed from the domain-dependent global completion.

## 4. Cartan connection in affine-matrix form

Let \(V\subset\mathbb R^2\) be an open region on which a coframe \(e=(e^1,e^2)^T\) and a metric-compatible \(SO(2)\) connection \(\omega\) are \(C^2\). Represent the affine Cartan connection by the \(\mathfrak{se}(2)\)-valued one-form
\[
\mathcal A=
\begin{pmatrix}
\omega & e\\
0&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its curvature is
\[
\mathcal F=d\mathcal A+\mathcal A\wedge\mathcal A
=
\begin{pmatrix}
R&T\\
0&0
\end{pmatrix},
\quad
R=d\omega+\omega\wedge\omega,\quad
T=de+\omega\wedge e.
\]
In two dimensions \(R\) and each component of \(T\) are 2-forms. This block identity is algebraic; it fixes the convention used below.

For an oriented edge \(e_{ij}\), define parallel transport \(U_{ij}\in SE(2)\) by
\[
\frac{dU}{dt}=-\mathcal A(\dot\gamma(t))U,\qquad U(0)=I.
\]
For a positively oriented triangular face \(K=(i,j,k)\), its holonomy based at \(i\) is
\[
H_K=U_{ki}U_{jk}U_{ij}.
\]
Under a change of local frame \(G_i\in SE(2)\), edge transports transform as \(U_{ij}\mapsto G_jU_{ij}G_i^{-1}\), and hence
\[
H_K\mapsto G_iH_KG_i^{-1}.
\]
Thus the conjugacy class of the face holonomy is gauge-covariant; its matrix components are compared only after choosing a common base frame or parallel-transporting them to one.

## 5. Small-face holonomy consistency

**Theorem 93.2 (local Cartan-holonomy consistency).**  
Assume \(\mathcal A\in C^2(\overline V;\mathfrak{se}(2)\otimes T^*V)\). Let \(K\subset V\) be a positively oriented shape-regular triangle of diameter \(h_K\), area \(|K|\), and base vertex \(x_K\). Identify all quantities in the same local gauge at \(x_K\). There are constants \(C,h_*>0\), depending on the \(C^2\) bound for \(\mathcal A\), the gauge patch, and the shape-regularity bound, such that for \(h_K<h_*\),
\[
\left\|\log H_K+
\mathcal F(x_K)\,|K|\right\|
\le C h_K^3.
\]
Consequently, the face estimator
\[
\mathcal F_K^{\,h}:=-\frac{1}{|K|}\log H_K
\]
satisfies
\[
\|\mathcal F_K^{\,h}-\mathcal F(x_K)\|
\le C' h_K.
\]
Its rotational block estimates curvature \(R\), and its translational block estimates torsion \(T\).

**Proof sketch with convention.** In a local gauge, expand the three edge transports by their path-ordered exponential series. The terms linear in edge integrals cancel around the closed boundary; the second-order commutator terms combine with the exterior derivative terms into the curvature \(d\mathcal A+\mathcal A\wedge\mathcal A\). With the chosen parallel-transport equation \(\dot U=-\mathcal A(\dot\gamma)U\), the leading sign is negative:
\[
H_K=I-\mathcal F(x_K)|K|+O(h_K^3).
\]
The \(C^2\) regularity controls coefficient variation and the third-order path-ordered remainder uniformly on shape-regular triangles. Since \(|K|\asymp h_K^2\), the logarithm has the same leading term and an \(O(h_K^3)\) remainder. Dividing by \(|K|\) gives the stated \(O(h_K)\) consistency estimate. \(\square\)

The estimate is local and gauge-aware. It does not identify raw translational matrix entries at different base points without transport. It also does not claim exact equality between finite-loop Burgers closure and the untransported integral of torsion.

## 6. Discrete Frank–Burgers quantities

Write
\[
\log H_K=
\begin{pmatrix}
\Theta_K J & q_K\\
0&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
J=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For sufficiently small faces, the principal logarithm is well-defined. The discrete Frank angle and Burgers closure vector are
\[
\Omega_K=-\Theta_K,\qquad b_K=-q_K
\]
for the parallel-transport sign convention of Section 4. The theorem gives, after common-frame identification,
\[
\frac{\Omega_K}{|K|}=R(x_K)+O(h_K),\qquad
\frac{b_K}{|K|}=T(x_K)+O(h_K),
\]
where the scalar coefficient of the planar curvature 2-form is understood in the chosen oriented orthonormal frame, and the torsion equation is componentwise.

For a smooth connection, this is a consistency result for the curvature/torsion densities. In a defect model with concentrated disclination or dislocation cores, \(\mathcal A\) is not \(C^2\) across the cores. Apply the theorem on compact subsets away from the cores; convergence of the singular defect measures and the holonomy of loops enclosing cores require a separate distributional analysis.

## 7. Link to Morandotti and T091

The resulting chain is now:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{T092: local two-circle seed}\\
&\quad\downarrow\\
&\text{T093.1: seed-preserving conforming global mesh}\\
&\quad\downarrow\\
&\text{T093.2: discrete Cartan holonomy consistency}\\
&\quad\downarrow\\
&\text{curvature / torsion densities}\\
&\quad\downarrow\\
&\text{Frank / Burgers defect data}\\
&\quad\downarrow\\
&\text{elastic incompatibility and equilibrium}\\
&\quad\downarrow\\
&\text{Morandotti Airy / variational formulation}\\
&\quad\downarrow\\
&\text{T091: finite-dimensional mixed Frank–Burgers interaction}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The global mesh theorem supplies a conforming geometric carrier. The holonomy theorem supplies a consistent discrete measurement of smooth curvature and torsion. The Morandotti constitutive and equilibrium problem remains a separate variational layer: it requires the elasticity tensor, source/core prescription, boundary conditions, and stability/coercivity assumptions. T091 then analyzes the finite-dimensional interaction of the reconstructed Frank and Burgers responses.

## 8. Classification and remaining obligations

- **Proved:** finite disjoint two-circle seed triangles can be embedded as elements of a finite conforming triangulation of a bounded polygonal domain.
- **Proved:** uniform midpoint refinement preserves conformity, seed equilateral geometry, shape regularity, and the stated mesh-size law.
- **Consistency theorem:** smooth Cartan face holonomy recovers curvature and torsion densities with local \(O(h_K)\) error under the stated regularity and shape assumptions.
- **Open:** a canonical algorithm selecting seed locations and radii from arbitrary input data; uniform-quality constrained meshing for arbitrary moving/dense seed families; convergence for singular defect measures and core limits; stability and convergence of the coupled elasticity discretisation; and the corresponding extension to curved surfaces or three-dimensional RGS–PRISM cells.
- **Not claimed:** every cell in the completed mesh is equilateral; every arbitrary domain has a uniformly shape-regular mesh independent of its geometry; finite-loop Burgers closure is exactly the raw torsion integral; or local triangulability alone solves an arbitrary PDE.
