# T070 — Oriented circumcentric dual on the spherical icosphere

**Status: geometric criterion implemented; automated refinement test added; execution not yet verified here. Not a DEC convergence theorem.**

## Question

For a spherical primal edge {a,b} shared by two spherical triangles, is the distance between their face circumcentres a valid circumcentric dual-edge length?

A positive unsigned distance is not sufficient. The shortest geodesic joining the two circumcentres must cross the primal edge, and it must do so orthogonally. If both circumcentres lie on the same side of the primal edge, their distance is not the length of that edge's circumcentric dual.

## Signed-angle criterion

Let a,b be unit vectors and define the normalized chord midpoint

\[
m=\frac{a+b}{\lVert a+b\rVert}.
\]

The two spherical face circumcentres c_0,c_1 are chosen as outward unit normals to their planar triangular faces. Since each circumcentre is equidistant (in spherical distance) from a and b,

\[
c_i\cdot(a-b)=0.
\]

Thus both centres lie on the great circle in the plane orthogonal to a-b. The primal edge midpoint m lies in the same plane. The tangent direction at m along this dual great circle is orthogonal to the primal edge tangent: the primal tangent is parallel to b-a, whereas the dual tangent is orthogonal to a-b.

Choose the unit tangent e in the direction of the projection of c_0 onto the tangent plane at m. Define signed angular coordinates

\[
\theta_i=\operatorname{atan2}(c_i\cdot e,c_i\cdot m).
\]

The dual crosses the primal edge at m precisely when the two circumcentres are on opposite sides:

\[
\theta_0>0,\qquad \theta_1<0.
\]

Provided additionally that

\[
0<\theta_0-\theta_1<\pi,
\]

the oriented dual-arc length is

\[
\ell^*_{ab}=\theta_0-\theta_1.
\]

The angular DEC conductance is then

\[
w_{ab}=\frac{\ell^*_{ab}}{\ell_{ab}},
\]

where \ell_ab is the spherical primal-edge length.

## Implementation

The prototype centralizes this criterion in the function _signed_circumcentric_dual_length and uses it both when assembling angular graph conductances and when computing the spherical l=1 Rayleigh diagnostic.

The routine raises an error if:
- the primal edge is antipodal or has no unique normalized midpoint;
- either circumcentre is degenerate at the midpoint;
- the two signed angles do not have opposite signs;
- the resulting dual arc is non-finite, non-positive, or at least pi.

No absolute-value repair, clipping, or replacement by an unsigned centre distance is allowed.

A regression test builds the icosphere at subdivision levels 0, 1, 2, and 3. It checks the expected number of angular edges \(30\cdot4^q\) and that all assembled conductances remain finite and positive. The constructor itself enforces the opposite-side crossing condition on every edge.

## What follows — and what does not

If the test passes, it verifies that the generated icosphere has a valid positive oriented circumcentric dual at those four tested levels. It does **not** prove:
- consistency of the circumcentric DEC energy for every smooth function;
- a uniform coercivity or discrete Rellich estimate;
- Mosco liminf or recovery;
- spectral convergence;
- that this product mesh is generated by the original RGS/Q6 seed.

The next analytical step is a local Taylor-expansion consistency estimate for the angular energy, with constants uniform over the refinement family. This must use the signed dual geometry and the actual spherical metric, not merely positivity of the weights.
