# C60 surface structured deformations: theorem framework

**Date:** 2026-11-21  
**Classification:** **candidate theorem / conditional framework**  
**Status:** literature-backed formulation; the C60-specific relaxation theorem is not proved.

## Abstract

We formulate the two-dimensional-to-three-dimensional structured-deformation problem for a compact triangulated surface \(\Sigma\) (the C60 surface) embedded in \(\mathbb R^3\). The macroscopic deformation is \(g:\Sigma\to\mathbb R^3\), while its non-disarranged approximate differential is a field \(G\in L^1(\Sigma;\operatorname{Hom}(T\Sigma,\mathbb R^3))\). The disarrangement density is
\[
M:=\nabla_\Sigma g-G.
\]
The distributional jump of \(g\) along edges is a separate interfacial term; it is not \(M\).

The result is separated into three parts: (I) the surface structured-deformation space and measure decomposition; (II) the metric/rank admissibility constraint; (III) a conditional integral-representation theorem for the relaxed energy. Part I is a manifold formulation of the SBV framework. Part II is a direct pointwise consequence once its hypotheses are certified. Part III is a **candidate C60 extension** of established planar dimension-reduction/structured-deformation results and requires a proof on the compact triangulated surface.

## 1. Geometry, notation, and the correct objects

Let \(\Sigma\subset\mathbb R^3\) be a compact, oriented, two-dimensional Lipschitz surface with a finite shape-regular triangulation \(\mathcal T\). For C60, \(\mathcal T\) is the truncated-icosahedral face complex. Write \(dA\) for area measure and \(T_x\Sigma\) for the approximate tangent plane at \(dA\)-a.e. \(x\).

For \(g:\Sigma\to\mathbb R^3\), denote its approximate tangential differential by
\[
\nabla_\Sigma g(x)\in\operatorname{Hom}(T_x\Sigma,\mathbb R^3).
\]
The space \(SBV(\Sigma;\mathbb R^3)\) is defined intrinsically by a finite Lipschitz atlas and compatible chart representatives. The definition must be independent of the chosen atlas.

For \(g\in SBV(\Sigma;\mathbb R^3)\), the distributional tangential derivative has decomposition
\[
D_\Sigma g
 = \nabla_\Sigma g\,\mathcal H^2\!\llcorner\Sigma
 + [g]\otimes\nu_g\,\mathcal H^1\!\llcorner J_g .
\tag{1.1}
\]
Here \(J_g\) is the approximate jump set, \([g]\in\mathbb R^3\) is the jump amplitude, and \(\nu_g\in T_x\Sigma\) is its unit conormal. Formula (1.1) assumes the special-BV class: there is no Cantor part. If a proposed limit has a Cantor part, it is outside this SBV formulation and must be treated in BV or a measure-valued extension.

A structured deformation on \(\Sigma\) is a pair \((g,G)\) with
\[
g\in SBV(\Sigma;\mathbb R^3),\qquad
G\in L^1(\Sigma;\operatorname{Hom}(T\Sigma,\mathbb R^3)).
\]
Its bulk disarrangement density is
\[
M(x)=\nabla_\Sigma g(x)-G(x)
\quad\text{for }dA\text{-a.e. }x.
\tag{1.2}
\]
Thus \(M\) is a \(3\times2\) tangent-linear field, not an edge-supported measure.

**Do not identify these objects:**
- \(\nabla_\Sigma g\): approximate differential of the macroscopic map;
- \(G\): limiting field of gradients of the determining sequence;
- \(M=\nabla_\Sigma g-G\): bulk disarrangement density;
- \([g]\otimes\nu_g\,\mathcal H^1\llcorner J_g\): macroscopic jump measure.

A jump of \(g\) belongs to the macroscopic jump set. In the classical structured-deformation approximation, \(M\) is generated by submacroscopic jumps of approximating maps, not by simply relabelling the jump of \(g\).

## 2. Part I — Surface structured-deformation space

### Definition 2.1 (determining sequence on a surface)

A sequence \(g_n:\Sigma\to\mathbb R^3\) determines \((g,G)\) if:
1. \(g_n\to g\) in \(L^1(\Sigma;\mathbb R^3)\);
2. the tangential gradients satisfy
   \[
   \nabla_\Sigma g_n\,\mathcal H^2
   \stackrel{*}{\rightharpoonup}
   G\,\mathcal H^2
   \quad\text{as tangent-valued Radon measures};
   \tag{2.1}
   \]
3. the sequence has uniformly bounded energy in the chosen admissible class;
4. if the selected DPO model requires injective approximants, each \(g_n\) is globally one-to-one on \(\Sigma\), or satisfies the exact weaker non-interpenetration condition adopted by that model.

The topology in (2.1) is weak-* convergence of measures, not pointwise convergence and not merely convergence of sampled matrices. The precise coercivity and admissibility conditions depend on the chosen bulk and surface energy densities.

### Proposition 2.2 (measure decomposition)

If \(g\in SBV(\Sigma;\mathbb R^3)\) and \(G\in L^1(\Sigma;\operatorname{Hom}(T\Sigma,\mathbb R^3))\), then (1.1) and (1.2) give
\[
D_\Sigma g
 = (G+M)\,dA
 + [g]\otimes\nu_g\,\mathcal H^1\!\llcorner J_g.
\tag{2.2}
\]
This is an identity of tangent-valued Radon measures. It does not assert that every pair \((g,G)\) is generated by an injective determining sequence. That generation question is the admissibility problem.

*Proof.* Substitute \(\nabla_\Sigma g=G+M\) into the SBV decomposition (1.1). \(\square\)

### Coordinate invariance

In a chart \(X:U\subset\mathbb R^2\to\Sigma\), the represented matrices are \(3\times2\). Under a chart change, their columns transform by the tangent-coordinate Jacobian. The definitions of \(D_\Sigma g\), \(G\), and \(M\) are bundle-valued and intrinsic; a global fixed \(3\times2\) matrix field is not the correct coordinate-free object unless a global tangent frame exists.

For C60, use face charts and explicit transition maps. Do not assume a global smooth tangent frame on the whole surface.

## 3. Part II — Metric and rank admissibility

Let \(F:=\nabla_\Sigma g\). At almost every point choose an orthonormal basis of \(T_x\Sigma\), so \(F\) and \(G\) are represented by \(3\times2\) matrices.

### Proposition 3.1 (isometric differential implies rank two)

If
\[
F(x)^T F(x)=I_2
\quad\text{for }dA\text{-a.e. }x,
\tag{3.1}
\]
then
\[
\operatorname{rank}F(x)=2,\qquad
J_2F(x):=\sqrt{\det(F(x)^TF(x))}=1
\quad\text{for }dA\text{-a.e. }x.
\tag{3.2}
\]
The same conclusion holds for \(G\) if \(G^TG=I_2\) a.e.

*Proof.* Equation (3.1) says the two singular values of \(F\) are both one. Hence \(F\) is injective on the two-dimensional tangent plane and its two-dimensional Jacobian equals one. \(\square\)

This proposition is pointwise and does not imply global injectivity, absence of self-intersections, or compatibility across edges. For a piecewise-smooth C60 map, it also does not certify rank on vertex caps or transition regions unless those regions are included in the a.e. analysis.

### C60 facewise specialization

On each face \(f\), let \(B_f\in\mathbb R^{3\times2}\) be an oriented orthonormal tangent frame. If the actual macroscopic face map has
\[
F_f=B_fR(\phi_f),\qquad R(\phi_f)\in SO(2),
\tag{3.3}
\]
then \(F_f^TF_f=I_2\). If the proposed structured field is
\[
G_f=B_fR(\theta_f),
\tag{3.4}
\]
then \(G_f^TG_f=I_2\) and
\[
M_f=B_f\bigl(R(\phi_f)-R(\theta_f)\bigr).
\tag{3.5}
\]
In particular,
\[
\|M_f\|_F^2
=4\bigl(1-\cos(\phi_f-\theta_f)\bigr).
\tag{3.6}
\]
If the energy uses \(\frac12\|M_f\|_F^2\), its coefficient is instead \(2(1-\cos(\phi_f-\theta_f))\). The convention must be explicit.

Equation (3.6) is a local algebraic identity, not a relaxation theorem.

### Required C60 consistency gate

The current repository notes contain two different assignments for the facewise fields:
- C60_FACEWISE_DF_STRUCTURED_FIELD_AUDIT.md sets \(F_f=B_f\), \(G_f=B_fD_f\), and \(M_f=B_f(I-D_f)\);
- C60_DPO_COMPATIBILITY_AUDIT_2026-11-21.md also describes a tree-faithful case with \(F_f=G_f=B_fD_f\), \(M_f=0\), and separately a variational pair \(F_f=B_fR(\phi_f)\), \(G_f=B_fR(\theta_f)\).

These are not interchangeable conventions. Before any numerical value of \(M\), any energy, or any theorem claim is attached to the C60 construction, the project must identify which map is the actual macroscopic \(g\), which field is the proposed \(G\), and which construction is only a comparison/ansatz. This note deliberately does not choose between them.

A complete immersion certificate must cover:
1. all face interiors;
2. all edge strips and their gluing maps;
3. all vertex caps, including the exact cap differential;
4. orientation compatibility across interfaces;
5. global non-interpenetration/injectivity if required by the selected DPO approximation theorem.

A sampled positive singular-value minimum is numerical evidence, not a proof. A Newton residual near machine precision is not an exact-zero or isolation certificate. Use outward-rounded interval bounds and, near candidate zeros, a validated interval-Newton/Krawczyk argument if isolated degeneracies are claimed.

## 4. Part III — Conditional relaxed-energy representation

Fix \(p\geq1\) and bulk/interfacial densities satisfying the coercivity, growth, continuity, and subadditivity hypotheses needed by the chosen structured-deformation relaxation theorem. A model surface energy before relaxation may be written
\[
E_n(u)=
\int_\Sigma W(x,\nabla_\Sigma u)\,dA
+\int_{J_u}\psi(x,[u],\nu_u)\,d\mathcal H^1.
\tag{4.1}
\]
The precise hypotheses must be stated for the actual \(W,\psi\); they are not automatic from the geometry of C60.

Define the relaxed functional by
\[
\mathcal F_\Sigma(g,G):=
\inf\left\{
\liminf_{n\to\infty}E_n(g_n):
g_n\text{ determines }(g,G)
\right\}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 (candidate C60 surface integral representation)

**Conditional statement — not yet proved for C60.**  
Assume:
- (A1) \(\Sigma\) is a compact oriented Lipschitz two-manifold with finite Lipschitz atlas and a finite shape-regular triangulation;
- (A2) the local bulk and interfacial densities satisfy the precise hypotheses of a valid Euclidean structured-deformation integral-representation theorem in every chart;
- (A3) the chartwise cell formulas are compatible under tangent-coordinate changes and glue to intrinsic densities;
- (A4) the admissible determining sequences satisfy the required compactness, recovery-sequence, and (if imposed) global injectivity/non-interpenetration conditions;
- (A5) boundary terms are absent (closed surface) or are explicitly included if \(\partial\Sigma\neq\varnothing\).

Then the expected representation is
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal F_\Sigma(g,G)
=\int_\Sigma
W_{\rm SD}\bigl(x,\nabla_\Sigma g(x),G(x)\bigr)\,dA
+\int_{J_g}
h_{\rm SD}\bigl(x,[g](x),\nu_g(x)\bigr)\,d\mathcal H^1.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]
The densities \(W_{\rm SD}\) and \(h_{\rm SD}\) are defined by the corresponding local blow-up/cell problems in tangent coordinates. Their explicit values depend on \(W,\psi\) and cannot be inferred from C60 geometry alone.

The theorem is an expected manifold transfer of the Euclidean/local theory. The missing proof is the passage from chartwise relaxation to a globally compatible intrinsic functional, including gluing and recovery sequences across face/edge/cap interfaces. If (A3) or (A4) fails, (4.3) must not be asserted.

### Proof programme

1. **Lower bound.** Localize the energy in charts, blow up at \(\mathcal H^2\)-a.e. bulk point and \(\mathcal H^1\)-a.e. jump point, apply the Euclidean lower-bound theorem, and prove chart overlaps do not double-count energy.
2. **Upper bound.** Construct local recovery sequences for bulk and jump cell problems, patch them with a partition of unity, and estimate transition cost near edges and vertices. The C60 vertex-cap estimate must be proved for the actual cap maps and weights; a sampled scaling plot is not enough.
3. **Compatibility.** Show local recovery sequences agree in the required trace/topology on chart overlaps and preserve admissibility/injectivity.
4. **Intrinsic density.** Verify transformation laws of the cell formulas under tangent-coordinate changes and orientation changes.
5. **Compactness.** Establish uniform bounds needed to extract the limiting pair and exclude an unaccounted Cantor part if the target space is SBV.

No step is supplied merely by proving \(F^TF=I_2\).

## 5. What the literature establishes — and what it does not

### Directly relevant theorem

Carita, Matias, Morandotti and Owen, *Dimension Reduction in the Context of Structured Deformations*, Journal of Elasticity **133** (2018), 1–35, DOI [10.1007/s10659-018-9670-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10659-018-9670-9), study a three-dimensional continuum reduced to a two-dimensional domain with values in \(\mathbb R^3\). Their reduced structured-deformation variables include
\[
g\in SBV(\omega;\mathbb R^3),\quad
G\in L^1(\omega;\mathbb R^{3\times2}),
\]
and a director field \(d\), with an integral representation under their stated hypotheses. Their Theorem 3.5 is the closest direct precedent for the \(2\to3\) matrix dimensions used here. The paper also compares the two orders of dimension reduction and structured-deformation relaxation and a simultaneous limiting procedure.

**Scope limit:** their domain is a planar open set \(\omega\subset\mathbb R^2\), with a specific thin-domain model and specific energy assumptions. It is not a theorem for a compact curved triangulated surface, and it does not establish C60 injectivity, cap gluing, or the energy density for the present model.

### General structured-deformation framework

Choksi and Fonseca, *Bulk and Interfacial Energies for Structured Deformations of Continua*, Archive for Rational Mechanics and Analysis **138** (1997), 37–103, provide the foundational bulk/interfacial relaxation framework. Later measure-valued structured-deformation work makes explicit the independent domain dimension \(N\) and target dimension \(d\), with gradient fields in \(\mathbb R^{d\times N}\). This supports the dimensional consistency of \(3\times2\) fields; it does not by itself supply the compact-surface theorem.
