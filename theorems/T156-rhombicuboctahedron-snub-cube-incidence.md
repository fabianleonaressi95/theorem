# T156 — Rhombicuboctahedron-to-snub-cube incidence correspondence

**Status:** exact finite combinatorial correspondence certified for the coordinate models; continuous rigid motion and the reported 22.5-degree kinematics remain open.

## 1. Geometric clarification of the source construction

The solid called the *rhombic cuboctahedron* in the In2Infinity material is the standard **rhombicuboctahedron** (small rhombicuboctahedron), with 24 vertices, 48 edges, 8 triangular faces and 18 square faces. Its standard decomposition is two square cupolae joined by a central octagonal prism. Of the 18 squares, 12 share an edge with a triangle; the remaining six are the cupola base squares and do not share an edge with a triangle.

This face distinction gives a precise combinatorial reading of the source's statement that twelve square faces split while six remain square. It is not an arbitrary selection of twelve faces.

## 2. Incidence correspondence

Take the 24-vertex coordinate model of the rhombicuboctahedron used in T154, generated by signed permutations of
\[
(1,1,1+\sqrt2),
\]
and the chiral 24-vertex snub-cube model generated from the Tribonacci root
\[
t^3-t^2-t-1=0
\]
by the parity-restricted signed-permutation orbit of \((1,t,t^{-1})\). Normalize each model to unit edge length.

The twelve split squares are exactly those with an edge also belonging to a triangular face. Each such square has two possible diagonals. We enumerated all
\[
2^{12}=4096
\]
diagonal assignments and compared the resulting vertex-face incidence structures with the snub-cube incidence structure.

**Result:** exactly two assignments are isomorphic to the snub-cube incidence structure. Relative to a deterministic cyclic ordering of the twelve split squares, they are the alternating patterns
\[
010101010101,\qquad 101010101010.
\]
The six remaining squares are unchanged. Both assignments have
\[
(V,E,F)=(24,60,38),\qquad (F_3,F_4)=(32,6),
\]
and satisfy Euler's relation.

This is stronger than matching face counts: the full vertex-face incidence structures agree, including which vertices belong to each face. Since the source solids are convex, the common two-vertex intersections of distinct faces determine their shared edges. The two complementary patterns are distinct diagonal choices; identifying them with the two geometric snub-cube enantiomorphs still requires an orientation/chirality check on the explicit vertex maps.

## 3. What the result does and does not establish

- **Certified:** the twelve-face split is the natural set of twelve lateral squares in the cupola/prism decomposition.
- **Certified for the coordinate-derived incidence models:** precisely the two alternating diagonal patterns yield the snub-cube incidence type.
- **Not established:** the 22.5-degree rotation as a rigid motion; a hinge-axis assignment; preservation of all edge lengths during a continuous path; planarity throughout the path; collision-free motion; or the chirality/orientation map.
- **Not inferred:** any magnetic, conductive, orbital, or atomic-radius explanation.

In particular, an incidence isomorphism is discrete data. It does not imply that the two embedded polyhedra are connected by an isometric flex, nor that the flex angle is \(\pi/8\).

## 4. Next geometric gate

To test the source's kinematic claim, the next record must supply:

1. an explicit vertex bijection from the rhombicuboctahedron coordinates to the snub-cube coordinates for each alternating pattern;
2. the twelve square-face hinge axes and the six fixed square faces;
3. a parameterized coordinate path \(X_v(s)\), \(s\in[0,1]\);
4. exact or interval-certified checks of edge lengths, face planarity, nonintersection, and endpoint angle;
5. an orientation-sensitive comparison of the two complementary patterns.

Until these are supplied, T156 is an incidence theorem/audit, not a Jitterbug-motion theorem.
