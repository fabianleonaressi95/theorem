# T071 — Conditional angular DEC energy consistency on the Ellis icosphere

**Status: analytical consistency proposition under explicit mesh hypotheses; numerical l=1 regression added; test execution pending. This is not a Mosco theorem.**

## Setting

Let \\(\\mathcal T_h\\) be a geodesic triangulation of the unit sphere. Vertices are \\(x_j\\), primal edges are \\(e=(j,k)\\), primal lengths are \\(\\ell_e\\), and circumcentric dual edges have signed lengths \\(\\ell_e^*>0\\). Assume each dual edge crosses its primal edge orthogonally and the circumcentric dual cells form a partition of the sphere.

For nodal data \\(u_h=(u(x_j))_j\\), define

\\[
\\mathcal E_h(u_h)=
\\sum_{e=(j,k)}\\frac{\\ell_e^*}{\\ell_e}
\\bigl(u(x_j)-u(x_k)\\bigr)^2.
\\]

The continuum energy is

\\[
\\mathcal E(u)=\\int_{S^2}|\\nabla_{S^2}u|^2\\,d\\Omega.
\\]

## Mesh hypotheses

The consistency statement below requires a family with:
1. geodesic, shape-regular triangles, with a uniform lower bound on angles and bounded aspect ratios;
2. quasi-uniform scale \\(h=\\max_e\\ell_e\\to0\\);
3. spherical Delaunay/circumcentric admissibility, so every signed dual length is positive and the dual cells form a non-overlapping partition;
4. uniform bounds on primal-to-dual length ratios;
5. bounded vertex valence.

These are family-wide hypotheses. Passing the crossing test at four finite levels does not establish them uniformly.

## Local consistency mechanism

For an edge \\(e=(j,k)\\), let \\(m_e\\) be its geodesic midpoint and \\(t_e\\) its unit tangent. Taylor expansion along the unit-speed geodesic through the edge gives, for \\(u\\in C^3(S^2)\\),

\\[
u(x_k)-u(x_j)
=\\ell_e\\,\\partial_{t_e}u(m_e)+O(\\ell_e^3).
\\]

The quadratic term cancels because the endpoints are symmetric about the midpoint. Consequently,

\\[
\\frac{\\ell_e^*}{\\ell_e}
\\bigl(u(x_k)-u(x_j)\\bigr)^2
=
\\ell_e\\ell_e^*
\\bigl|\\partial_{t_e}u(m_e)\\bigr|^2
+O(\\ell_e^3\\ell_e^*).
\\]

The leading factor \\(\\ell_e\\ell_e^*\\) is the scale of the circumcentric edge diamond. Orthogonality identifies the primal direction with the direction sampled by the nodal difference; the Delaunay partition and shape regularity control the assembly of these local contributions. Standard interpolation/quadrature estimates on such a uniformly regular orthogonal dual family then give a first-order global consistency bound.

## Conditional proposition

Under the mesh hypotheses above, there is a constant \\(C\\), independent of \\(h\\), such that for every \\(u\\in C^3(S^2)\\),

\\[
\\left|\\mathcal E_h(R_hu)-\\mathcal E(u)\\right|
\\le C h\\,\\|u\\|_{C^3(S^2)}^2,
\\]

where \\(R_hu=(u(x_j))_j\\) is nodal restriction.

The constant depends on the uniform shape-regularity, quasi-uniformity, dual/primal ratio, and valence bounds. The proposition is conditional on those hypotheses; it is not established merely by the present finite-level implementation.

## Calibration by degree-one harmonics

For \\(u(x)=x_3\\) on the unit sphere,

\\[
-\\Delta_{S^2}u=2u,\\qquad
\\int_{S^2}u^2\\,d\\Omega=\\frac{4\\pi}{3},\\qquad
\\mathcal E(u)=\\frac{8\\pi}{3}.
\\]

Hence its continuum Rayleigh quotient is exactly 2. The existing spherical_l1_rayleigh diagnostic computes the discrete quotient using the same oriented dual weights as the graph assembly. A new regression test checks that its absolute error from 2 decreases across icosphere levels 0–3.

This is a finite-family numerical consistency check, not a proof of the uniform mesh hypotheses or the stated error bound.

## Relation to the Ellis radial product

For separated modes \\(v_{ij}=p(u_i)Y(x_j)\\), the product graph energy factors into a radial term times the spherical mass and a spherical DEC term times the radial quadrature. Thus angular consistency is one necessary component of smooth-core consistency for the warped Ellis product.

It does not establish:
- a uniform discrete Rellich estimate;
- the Mosco weak-liminf inequality M1;
- the recovery-sequence property M2;
- spectral convergence;
- a refinement generated by the original Q6/RGS seed.
